# Defect register

The standing list of what the gates could not see. One entry per defect that reached a build: the symptom as it presented, what the investigation found, the root cause, and the check that covers the defect's whole CLASS from now on.

Unlike a lesson, an entry stays after it becomes a gate. The register is not a to-do list — it is the record of where defects came from, which is the best available guide to where the next one is.

**What lives here, and what rolled over.** This file holds the 12 most recent CLOSED entries plus every OPEN one, whatever its age, newest first. Everything older and closed is in [`defect-register-past.md`](defect-register-past.md), moved verbatim. An OPEN entry never rolls over — it leaves this file by being closed, not by ageing, and a heading that says the defect is still live is not a closure however it spells it: "(LATENT, not fixed)", "(PARTLY FIXED: ...)" and "(... still unfixed)" all keep the entry here, and each of those three had been archived once before the gate learned to refuse them. The rollover is done by the task whose new entry pushes this file past 12 closed entries, in that task's own commit, the way the devlog archives its detailed file; never by a later sweep. `tests/architecture/defectRegisterRollover.test.ts` holds all of that. An "entry above" or "entry below" written before the split may name an entry that now lives in the archive, and a rollover can strand a pointer in either direction; the archive's header lists the seven that cross today.

## 2026-09-24 — The sustained-raid warning spec stamps a toast with the tick of the frame that drew it, and read 199 against its 200-tick floor on main's CI (found by main's CI on v0.3.231, OPEN)

**Symptom.** Run 36064573424 on `5a6ce866`, browser shard 1/4, attempt 1: `tests/browser/attack-warning-sustained.spec.ts:86` failed "the words came faster than the horn is throttled", `Expected: >= 200`, `Received: 199`. The spec had passed on this commit's other shards and on the runs of the three commits before it. Attempt 2 of the same run passed, so it is intermittent, and `npm run ci:status` read the commit GREEN ONLY ON A RE-RUN and exited 1, which is what it was changed to do the same day.

**Investigation.** Read, not reproduced. The spec stamps each alert toast with `getHudState().tick`, read in a MutationObserver when the toast's node is added. The HUD adds toasts in its animation-frame callback, after the game's own frame callback has stepped the simulation, so a stamp is the tick at the end of the frame that drew the toast, not the tick the horn fired. Two horns exactly 200 ticks apart read 199 when the first frame ran one tick past its horn and the second ran none, and on a slow runner a frame covers several ticks, so the gap can move by more.

**Root cause, as far as it is known.** The instrument, most likely: it measures when the toast was drawn, not when the horn fired. Not checked: whether the horn itself ever fires less than 200 ticks after the last one.

**Checked from now on.** Nothing yet. The fix is to stamp the horn's own tick, from game state or an attribute on the toast, not the tick at insertion, and to add no tolerance. The stacked-click lane found it and left it alone, because the spec belongs to the attack-warning work (v0.3.229); the coordinator assigns it.

## 2026-09-24 — A browser spec on the stacked-entity click cycle failed on CI about one run in five, with the game's selection right and the selection panel one click behind (found by main's CI and the re-run that hid it, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** `tests/browser/game-selection-click.spec.ts:110`, "only advances an overlapping exact-click stack at the same presented hit point", failed on main's CI in run 34041883943 on `bcdc88c3` (2026-09-06, both attempts, beside other specs), in run 35962265457 on `f92c1157` (2026-09-24) and in attempt 1 of run 36019109919 on `221329b4`, which a re-run turned green. The last two read `Expected: "Militia"`, `Received: "Sheep"`, `Timeout: 5000ms` at line 271: five seconds after the fourth click, the selection panel still named the third click's selection.

**Investigation.** Two diagnostic CI runs on a branch that ran only this spec, 15 times on each of three shards, failed 6 of about 34 (run 36029523854) and 11 of about 45 (run 36032537612). In every failure the bridge held the right selection and the panel showed the one before. An animation-frame counter showed why: the page got 5-7 frames between the fixture click and the fourth click, and in one failure 1 frame in more than 5 seconds, against about 12 a second once it recovered. The panel is redrawn only in its animation-frame callback. The spec's search for a click point made about 240 selection calls in one `page.evaluate` (6.4-7.1 s on CI), and each call drew a full frame before it hit-tested. It never failed locally: the first session ran it 47 times (GPU, SwiftShader, six workers, CI's pinned voxel) and a probe of it 42 times across animation phases, and the second ran it 40 times on SwiftShader, 20 of them with the browser's GPU process held to four CPUs like CI's runner (Chrome starts its GPU process on every CPU whatever its parent's mask, so the first try at that measured nothing, and a runner that walks the process tree was needed). On one core it could not finish inside its own timeout at all, so no local arm fails it for the CI reason. Measured locally at four CPUs on one build, the search by clicks drew 255 and 258 frames in 10.5 and 9.8 s; reading the stack drew 1 frame in 25 ms and found the same 43 qualifying points.

**Root cause.** Two parts, one in the game. (1) Every hit test drew a full frame before it read the hit state, even when the snapshot it reads was already on screen: `AoeVoxelGameView` called `renderer.frame` on every click and right-click. (2) The spec searched by clicking, so each of its 240 calls changed the selection, made a snapshot wait, and drew; on CI's SwiftShader that queued seconds of rendering, and its panel checks gave the page five seconds of wall clock rather than a number of frames.

**Fixed.** The view draws before a hit test only when the renderer is not interaction-ready: when a snapshot is waiting, and at boot, after a bridge swap or while the context is lost or restoring, where that draw is what performs the restore (spec, "entity selection by visible voxel silhouette"). With the GPU process at four CPUs, right-click spam at 10 a second ran at 19.8-20.3 frames a second after the fix against 16.8-17.9 before; that is an efficiency win, not a change a player sees, so it has no changelog line. The spec reads the stack a click would walk (`getClickStackAtWorldPosition`, which selects nothing) and asserts its search drew at most one frame; each click is then checked on the bridge first and on the panel two rendered frames later, bounded by the same five seconds. The moving-villager search in the same file now reads the stack too and clicks once.

**Checked from now on.** `tests/app/clickHitTestDrawsOnlyWhatWaits.test.ts` holds the view's rule, red with the unconditional draw put back. The spec's frame assertion is red on a build with it put back ("reading the click stack at 53 points drew 53 frames"), and its panel check is red on a build whose panel redraws on every fourth frame, which the old five-second check passes. Stability: 60 of 60 locally on SwiftShader, 40 of 40 with the GPU process at four CPUs, and on CI 240 of 240: two runs of a branch that ran this test and the moving-villager test 20 times each on three shards (runs 36069113956 and 36069127456, 120 runs of each test, 4.5-4.8 min a shard), where the first session's diagnostic runs of the old spec had failed 17 of about 79. The fourth shard of each run played the real browser shard 3/4, where it had failed on main, and passed 53 of 53 both times.

**Bounds.** One fixture, paused, at 800x600. A search over a running world draws one frame per tick advanced, and nothing measures that. The panel check assumes the selection panel redraws on the first frame whose selection differs, which `selectionPanel.ts` does today. Whether a burst of frames starves CI's page is measured by repeated CI runs only, not by any gate.

## 2026-09-24 — ci:status called a run green when its first attempt had failed and a re-run passed, so a flaky browser spec stayed out of this register (found while chasing that spec, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** In run 36019109919 on `221329b4`, attempt 1 failed "ubuntu-latest — browser suite 3/4" on `game-selection-click.spec.ts:110` and attempt 2 passed. `npm run ci:status` read GREEN and exited 0. Run 34079856007 on `143ad511` (2026-09-07) had the same shape with another spec.

**Root cause.** GitHub re-runs a failed run in place: the run keeps its id, `run_attempt` goes up by one, and the run's conclusion becomes the newest attempt's. Both lists `ci:status` read, the runs for a commit and the workflow history, report only that conclusion, and gh 2.32's `gh run list --json` has no attempt field.

**Fixed.** `ci:status` asks for `run_attempt` and reads the jobs of every earlier attempt (`scripts/ci-status-attempts.mjs`). A commit's own run that passed only on a re-run reads GREEN ONLY ON A RE-RUN, names the earliest failing attempt's jobs and failing step, and exits 1. An earlier attempt in which no job got a runner is the account, not the code, and does not read red. A re-run of a green attempt is not a flake. An earlier attempt that cannot be read is unknown, and reads red. The history line names every run in its window that passed only on a re-run, without making the history red.

**Found by the review of this fix, and fixed with it.** Three more ways a red verdict was read as something else, each shown by the reviewer's probe. (1) An attempt that failed, then a re-run that could not start (no runner): the newest attempt read COULD NOT RUN and exited 0. It now reads RED, on the commit's line and on the history's. (2) A later run of the workflow on the same commit, such as a `workflow_dispatch` after a failed push run: the command read only the newest run, so the later green hid the red. It now reads every run of the workflow on the commit, and a red one before a green one reads GREEN ONLY ON A LATER RUN and exits 1. (3) The fake `gh`'s field check matched a key as a word anywhere in the request, and `conclusion`, `name` and `steps` also appear inside the jobs jq's `failedSteps` filter, so dropping one of them from the projection left every case green. It now matches projection keys only. An earlier attempt whose failed jobs were all cancelled stays red, since a job that hits its time limit also ends cancelled, but it is no longer called a flake. A second review of those fixes found two more, also fixed: a red attempt or run already known read STILL RUNNING and exited 0 while a re-run or a later run was still going, though every way that run can end leaves the commit red; and an older run whose re-run could not start was skipped without reading the attempt before it. Pull-request runs on the same commit are no longer read, because they test a merge of their branch rather than the commit. A third check found one more, an older run being re-run in place skipped before its failed attempt was read, and it is fixed the same way.

**Checked from now on.** `tests/scripts/ciStatusReruns.test.ts`: fifteen cases on fixtures captured from the live repo on 2026-09-24; the ones constructed from captured parts say so. The fake `gh` refuses to return a field the script's projection no longer asks for; without it, dropping `attempt: .run_attempt` from the jq left every case green, and with the old word match, dropping `conclusion` from the jobs jq did too. Fifteen mutants, each red, and that control are in `gate-proofs.md`.

**Bounds.** The two watched workflows, and only attempts and runs GitHub still lists. It says that a commit's gate was flaky, not which spec is flaky across runs. A cancelled job is red whether it hit its time limit or was cancelled by hand: its check-run annotation says which ("The job has exceeded the maximum execution time of 45m0s" on run 35896557214), and the command does not read that annotation for a cancel.

## 2026-09-24 — A quick double-click on a stacked unit that was already selected walked the stack instead of selecting every unit of its type (found by the review of v0.3.224, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** The 2026-09-23 double-click entry recorded it as not covered and a separate defect. Click a unit that stands in front of something else, then double-click it: the pair's first click walked the stack to the entity behind, and the second walked on. On the review's probe (a militia in front of a house in front of a sheep), the pair selected the house and then the sheep. On the real page, on the boot scenario's stacked villager at 1280x720, it ended on one villager where three were expected.

**Root cause.** The pair's first click repeats the earlier click at the same point, so the stack cycle takes it, and a click that walked the stack cleared the double-click candidate. The second click, 100 ms later, found no candidate and walked again. The 2026-09-11 fix made the double-click outrank the cycle only when the pair's first click had selected the unit.

**The first fix was half right.** It took the candidate from the unit the click walked FROM. The review of it found the other order: a slow click that had walked on to the Town Centre, then a quick pair, whose first click walks BACK to the villager. That left the Town Centre as the unit walked from, no candidate, and the pair ended on the Town Centre. With a militia in front of a house in front of an owned sheep, a pair after walking to the sheep selected every sheep. DE selects every villager and every militia.

**Fixed.** Every pointer click leaves as the double-click candidate the type of the player's own unit (or owned sheep) on top at the cursor, which is what a plain click there selects, whether or not the click walked the stack. It is timed on the click's own input timestamp (spec §9.4). A second click inside the 300 ms window selects every unit of that type on screen, and a slower one still walks on. A click on anything else, or one with no input time, leaves no candidate. That last part changes a gesture: a unit, then a building, then the same unit inside 300 ms used to select every unit of its type, because a click on a building left the earlier candidate in place. In DE a click in between breaks the pair, and now it does here.

**Checked from now on.** `tests/input/voxelSelectionController.test.ts`, with the fixture's villager in front of its Town Centre: a pair after a single click selects all three villagers; a pair after a slow walk to the Town Centre selects all three; a click on the Town Centre between two clicks on the villager breaks the pair; and a second click 400 ms after the first still walks. `tests/browser/de-command-surface.spec.ts`, "double-clicking a stacked villager that is already selected selects every villager on screen", with real pointer input on the boot scenario. The original controller fails three of the unit cases, the first fix fails the walk-back case, and keeping the earlier candidate across a click on something else fails the break case. The browser case failed on the original behaviour (`Expected: 3`, `Received: 1`).

**Bounds.** The controller with a stack handed in, and one stacked villager on one scenario at one viewport on the real page. A double-click on a building is not a gesture here at all (spec §9.4 names units and owned sheep), so neither gate asks for one.

## 2026-09-24 — In a team game an ally's ground was lit and its whole base missing: no unit, building, tree or mine the ally could see was drawn (found by the de-look scoping worker, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** Found in passing by the de-look scoping worker on 2026-09-23, not reported by the owner. In a 3-player match with `?teams=1,1,2&players=3` the ally's ground was lit, but none of its units, buildings, trees or mines were drawn. Reproduced in the real game (vite preview, the worker's probe `alliedEntitiesProbe.mjs` through the browser test API) at tick 3000: owner 2 had its Town Centre at (62, 9), 3 buildings and 6 units, and the renderer was handed 9 entities of the human's and 3,198 unowned ones (tiles, trees, animals) — zero of owner 2's.

**Investigation.** The projector projects every entity; which live entities reach the renderer is decided afterwards by `renderStateOps.isCurrentlyVisible`, which asked `isFootprintVisible(visibility, humanPlayerId, ...)` — the human's OWN vision. The frame's lit cells (`visibleCells` / `exploredCells`) took the union with `getSharedVisionOwners` since v0.3.63, and so did projectiles; nothing else did. A search for the human's id passed to the visibility map found eleven more sites: fog memory recording and cleanup (`fogMemorySystem`), fog memory display (`fogMemoryOps`), the four click finders (`selectionFinders`), and the click and right-click visibility checks (`visibilityQueries`, "Target not visible."). Three more handed one owner to the animation filters — the render state's swing index, and the projector's swing index and death feed — which take the id as a parameter, so no search for visibility calls finds them. The replay bridge had shared vision hard-coded to none. Making an ally clickable exposed four more defects, all found by playing it or by the reviews: the ground route of a right-click (`contextRouter`, taken when the pointer hits no drawn entity) called anything `owner !== attackerOwner` hostile, so a right-click on the cell an ally's villager stood on was an attack order; a Monk's right-click on an ally's unit armed a conversion that the applier then abandoned, leaving the Monk standing, on both routes; a conversion armed on an enemy only an ally sees never progressed, because the applier asked the Monk owner's own vision (Block Printing's range outreaches a Monk's own sight, so this stall is reachable in play); and the selection panel named an ally's faction "Enemy".

**Root cause.** "What can the human see?" had no single answer. Each site asked the visibility map about one owner, and shared vision was bolted onto the one site that drew ground. **Fixed.** `src/game/simulation/bridge/humanSight.ts` answers the question once: a player's sight is itself plus `readSharedVisionOwners` (allies from the first frame, everyone with Spies), and `isCellSeen` / `isFootprintSeen` / `isFootprintKnown` / `isWitnessedBySight` take that set. Every site above goes through it, the replay bridge included, and the witness filters take a sight LIST, so handing them one owner no longer typechecks. The ground-route hostile check is `isEnemyOwner`; a Monk's right-click on an ally is a walk on both routes, its ground route may target an enemy that only an ally sees, and a conversion (of a unit, or of a building with Redemption) progresses on the Monk owner's sight; the selection panel reads "Ally". Other rules of play (towers, automatic targets, the AI's searches) still ask the acting owner's own vision, and the visibility map and witness lists are still written per owner. What follows the sight in world state is the human's fog memory (`aoe2.lastSeenStatic`), a ground right-click's target and a conversion's progress. All of it reads teams and technologies from world state, so replays re-simulate it exactly; a team-game recording made before v0.3.230 was made under the old rules, its fog memory differs and its right-clicks and conversions are re-decided, so it may not re-simulate to its recorded state at all.

**Measured.** Fog memory now records what the ally sees, and that slot is re-serialised whole on every tick it changes (it was once 358 MB of a 537 MB bundle). Recorded headlessly for 13,000 ticks of `aoe2-prototype` with three all-AI players (`tmp/probes/fogMemoryBundleSize.mts`, since the playtest recorder takes no teams): the slot is 4.37 MB over 137 dirty ticks, largest write 42.1 KB, 211 remembered entities, against 1.17 MB, 77 ticks, 19.9 KB and 99 entities for the same match unallied and for the unfixed build allied or not (all three identical). ## 2026-09-24 — A player under attack could miss it: the minimap mark was a 3-pixel dot that went dark for 40% of a raid and expired in a pause, no alert said it in words, and loading a save silenced the next warning (found by a read-only scoping of the 2026-09-11 play screenshots, FIXED and gated v0.3.229)

**Symptom.** In the 2026-09-11 play session (`?speed=normal`, match M1 raided to 0 population), the attack warning was hard to find. Measured on its 1600x900 screenshots, the minimap region of `M2-12-attack.png` holds 16 near-white alert pixels out of 1.44 million, and `M2-14-floatbars.png`, taken during the same AI raid, holds 0. No alert said anything in words: the horn was the only other cue, and a muted tab loses it. The coordinator relayed the scoping to this lane; not owner-reported as far as this entry knows.

**Investigation.** The scoping's four claims were checked against the code before building on them, and all four held. (1) `drawAttackWarning` sized the core as `max(hw * 1.3, 3)` canvas pixels, and at the real minimap (220x160 over the 60x36 map, `hw` 2.2) the 3-pixel floor was what got drawn: the before-captures hold 18 white minimap pixels at 1600x900 and 12 at 800x600, where the minimap is shown at 164 pixels over its 220-pixel backing. (2) The mark lived `ATTACK_WARNING_FLASH_MS` = 8,000 ms of wall clock, while `ATTACK_WARNING_THROTTLE_TICKS` = 200 re-raised it: 13.3 s at normal speed, 20 s at slow. So a raid that kept hitting left the minimap dark from 8 s after each horn until the next: 40% of the time at normal, 60% at slow. Measured by putting the shipped rule back: at normal the minimap went dark at tick 140 (the horn at 20 plus 8 s at 15 ticks a second) and stayed dark until 220, and in the browser 323 frames of a sustained raid drew no mark. (3) The wall-clock life also ran while paused, so a paused game lost the mark after 8 s: frame 480 at 60 frames a second. (4) `attack-warning.spec.ts` asserted `marked > 0`, which is green on exactly the frames above. Found while building, and a fifth: the audio controller never forgot the world it had watched. Loading an earlier save kept the old horn's tick and the last attack tick seen, so the same raid met again after the load came with no horn and no words — reproduced through the real menu by saving at tick 100, taking the tick-1444 raid, and loading. Two of this change's own were caught before it shipped. Its after-capture showed the first larger mark filling a dark disk the size of its ring, which hid the whole early-game base on the minimap, raiders included. And the independent review found that the first load fix, which reset the controller whenever the bridge object changed, re-announced a replayed raid on every replay step, because a replay builds a new bridge per step and its world carries the recent swings. The same review found the throttle measured from the tick of the frame that saw a hit while the hold counted from the hit itself, so a frame that stepped several ticks could light the mark again with no horn.

**Root cause.** The mark was built as a transient flash rather than as state: its life was a wall-clock timer unrelated to the tick-counted throttle that re-raised it, it was raised only on the throttled horn rather than on every hit, and its size was a multiple of one map cell, which the real map makes tiny. The gate asked whether any pixel was lit, never whether a player could find it. The controller's memory had no notion of a world ending.

**Fixed (v0.3.229).** Every hit on the human's economy refreshes the mark and restarts its hold; the mark holds `ATTACK_WARNING_HOLD_TICKS` = the throttle's 200 simulation ticks after the last hit, so it is dark only when the next hit is sure to sound the horn again, and a pause keeps it. The horn's throttled decision also shows an alert toast, "You are under attack!", outside the mute switch, up for 5 s and styled apart from a rejection. The core is at least 7 SCREEN pixels in radius and the ring's base at least 20 at every minimap size, and only the core and its thin halo are filled, so the ground and units between the core and the ring stay visible. The throttle is measured between hit ticks, the hold's own clock. When a save is loaded, the load path dispatches `WORLD_LOADED_EVENT` and the audio mount resets the controller's memory of the old world; a replay step does not. Spec §14.5 (the attack warning, at the end of the audio-cues bullet) and §14.3's attack-alerts line.

**Checked from now on.**

- `tests/browser/attack-warning.spec.ts` requires at least `ALERT_SCREEN_PIXEL_FLOOR` = 100 screen pixels of the white core on the real match's raid (143 measured) and the toast's exact text, and replays the raid after loading a tick-100 save through the menu, requiring the second toast. Red by mutation: core floor back to 3 (26 pixels); the toast call removed; the mount's reset removed ("came with no words", 1 toast of 2).
- `tests/browser/attack-warning-sustained.spec.ts` runs `raid-warning-fixture` at `?speed=normal` (15.0 ticks a second measured) and samples the minimap on every frame: no frame below the floor from the first lit frame through a raid longer than both the old flash and the throttle, one toast per throttle window, then F3 and 9.5 s of paused frames with the mark still up. Red by mutation: the shipped rule (323 dark running frames), the wall-clock expiry alone (124 dark paused frames, running half green), no toast (0 of 2).
- `tests/browser/attack-warning-replay.spec.ts` steps a recorded raid forty ticks with the step button, rendering frames between steps, and allows at most one alert toast. Red by mutation: the bridge-identity reset put back (37 toasts over 40 steps).
- `tests/ui/raidWarning.test.ts` drives the real controller and mark on a clock that is the module's own (`performance.now` mocked) at all three speeds, paused, across a load, and with one frame stepping ten ticks over a blow; `tests/ui/gameAudio.test.ts` holds that a load forgets the old world's tallies and announces nothing the loaded world already holds; `tests/ui/attackWarning.test.ts` holds the tick-based hold; `tests/ui/minimap.test.ts` holds the screen-pixel sizing at 220 and 164 pixels wide and that nothing filled is wider than half the ring. Each red by its own mutation, listed in the 2026-09-24 devlog section.
- Bounds: one fixture and one speed in the browser, one save and one load, and one viewport, 800x600, where the minimap is shown at 164 pixels over its 220-pixel backing; the full-size minimap by draw calls and the before/after captures only. An enemy tower or castle shooting a villager still raises nothing, as before: only a unit is an attacker in the feed.
## 2026-09-24 — Three parts of the verification plumbing said "checked" or "green" about work they never checked (found by a read-only audit on 2026-09-23, FIXED and gated)

## 2026-09-03 — A decided match never ends, because the AI can only aim at a Town Center (PARTLY FIXED: 1 of 4 seeds resolved then; 3 of 4 after the 2026-09-04 garrison entry above, OPEN)

**Symptom.** The standing acceptance-test failure "matches do not resolve": at 60,000 ticks — 100 minutes of game time — every seed is still running. Measured on four seeds after the wood fix, all four still ran to the horizon.

**Investigation.** `gold-rush` gave the clean case. From tick 48,000 to 60,000, sampled every 4,000 and byte-identical at every sample, owner 2 held:

    pop 0/5   villagers 0   army 0   buildings: blacksmith x1, house x1

Zero units. No Town Center, so it can never produce a unit again. It is functionally dead and cannot recover. Meanwhile owner 1 stood at 130 population with an army of 90, building farms.

**Root cause.** Two rules that are each correct and together deadlock.

`conquestOutcomeSystem` ends a match only when a player has NO UNITS **AND NO BUILDINGS** — which is right; a player with a building standing has not been conquered.

`pickAttackTarget` chooses the AI's march target from `townCentersByOwner`, a map keyed on owners that still HAVE a Town Center. Raze it and that owner drops out of the target list entirely: `targetOwner` becomes null, the attack phase's push branch has nothing to march on, and the surviving buildings are attacked only if they happen to fall inside somebody's vision. The AI is structurally unable to finish what it started.

So the winner cannot satisfy the condition the loser is being kept alive by.

**Fix, and the first version of it was WRONG in a way worth recording.** The obvious shape — give `pickAttackTarget` a fallback map and let it return the surviving building in `targetOwner` / `targetTownCenterId` / `targetTownCenterPosition` — passed its own unit gate, resolved `gold-rush`, and REGRESSED THE AGE-UP: `aiPlayer.test.ts` :: "ages up through the ages within a generous tick budget" went red, the AI reaching Feudal at 2,061 and never Castle within 8,000 ticks. Cause: `targetTownCenterPosition` is not private to the attack phase. `aiFerryPhase` reads it too, and when it is set with the target not land-reachable it stands down the ordinary march AND discretionary building to fund a Transport Ship — so filling that field for a Town-Center-less enemy stalled the economy. Isolated by disabling only the fallback and re-running: 24 of 24 passed.

The shipped fix is therefore SURGICAL. `pickAttackTarget` is byte-identical to what it was. A separate `lastResortTargetId` / `lastResortTargetPosition` pair is computed alongside it and read by the attack phase and nothing else, in its final push branch, only when no enemy Town Center is left. Deterministic — owners and entity ids both sorted, so a replay picks the same building. `gold-rush` still resolves at exactly tick 41,967, and the age-up test is green.

**The lesson, which is about the FIELD and not the feature.** A context field named for one thing (`targetTownCenterPosition`) had a second reader with different semantics, and reusing it to mean "the place to march" was correct for the phase being edited and wrong for the phase that was not. The cost of finding that was one full gate run; the cost of not finding it would have been a shipped economy regression that the resolution measurement would have applauded.

**Effect, measured on four seeds at 60,000 ticks, before and after.** `gold-rush` goes from running-at-60,000 to **victory at tick 41,967**. The other three still run, and the reasons are DIFFERENT defects rather than this one failing:

- `arena` and `coastal`: owner 2 still HAS a Town Center, so the fallback never engages. Owner 1 has an army of 105 and does not finish it — on arena that base sits inside the script's own stone wall ring, which points at attack EXECUTION (pathing to or breaching a walled base) rather than at targeting.
- `fortress`: neither side has an army to attack with (2 and 0 in the Imperial age). That is the villager-target-versus-population-cap chain in the entry above, not this one.

**How it is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/aiAttacksAnyEnemy.test.ts` :: `an enemy with no Town Center is still a target` — four cases: the fallback fires when the enemy has lost its Town Center; a Town Center still outranks a much closer bare building; the two-argument form is unchanged; and an ALLY without a Town Center is never targeted. Red-checked by reintroducing the defect (iterating an empty list instead of the fallback map): the first case fails with `expected null to be 2`, and all four pass again on revert.

**Bound of that gate, named.** It is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE. It does not prove the army reaches the building, kills it, or that the match ends — arena and coastal are live proof that a correct target is not sufficient. The end-to-end claim rests on the `gold-rush` seed alone, at one horizon.

**What this predicts.** The remaining two causes are both "the army exists and does not close": walls it will not breach, and an army too small to matter. Both are about what the military DOES rather than what it is pointed at, and neither is touched here.

**What it actually was, found 2026-09-04.** The prediction above was half right and half wrong, and the wrong half is the useful one. It reads `arena` and `coastal` as "the army exists and does not close" — walls, or too few units. The army was not closing because it was never ORDERED to: the attack phase's first preference is the target enemy's villager, and a GARRISONED villager is alive with no position, so the order silently no-opped and the branch `continue`d before the fallbacks this entry added. Those fallbacks were correct and were never reached. See the 2026-09-04 entry above; with that fixed, three of four seeds resolve.

**And the bound named above is exactly where it hid.** This gate is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE, and it says so. The defect was one line earlier, in a preference that never got as far as choosing.

## 2026-08-30 — Gather over-subscription counts EVERY owner's villagers (LATENT, not fixed, OPEN)

**What it is.** `villagerEconomySystem` builds `gatherTargetCounts` by walking `activeWorld.query('gatherer')` with no owner filter, so every gatherer in the match counts toward every resource's occupancy. ## 2026-08-30 — The behind-building silhouette reads as a unit standing ON the building (OPEN)

**Symptom.** A unit hidden behind a building is cued by a flat-coloured mirror of its body drawn over the building. It is fully opaque, and at a Town Center that reads as a villager standing on the roof rather than as an x-ray of one behind the wall — noticed hands-on on 2026-08-29 ("three of them across the TC's upper tier read as villagers standing on the roof") and confirmed in the `occlusion-showcase-fixture` capture, where the blue figure sits on the roof with nothing to say it is a cue.

**Investigation.** The obvious fix — draw the mirror on a transparent material so the building reads through it — was implemented, measured, and REVERTED. It failed on three independent axes, each measured rather than argued:

1. *It is close to a no-op under the art style the game actually ships.* The default is `moebius`, whose tone-band quantiser snaps **24.1% of the cue's pixels byte-identical to the fully opaque render**; mean change against opaque is 46.6 there against 125.6 under `painted`. One opacity value cannot serve a banding and a non-banding pipeline.
2. *It introduces self-blending seams.* The mirror copies a unit's own overlapping parts, and a translucent instanced part that overlaps itself blends twice — solved per-pixel as 452 px at the declared 0.55, 68 at 0.7975 (1−0.45²) and 6 at 0.9089 (1−0.45³). About 14% of the body draws at the wrong opacity, as streaks down the arm and torso. The engine has the cure and does not export it (see `docs/engine-feedback/current.md`).
3. *It degrades the half of the cue that matters most.* The enemy colour is a coral in the same hue family as every roof in the palette, so blending erases it: mean RGB distance from the background over a bright roof fell from 204.2 opaque to **82.0**, while the friendly blue — orange's complement — held at 196.9. The cue exists to answer "are those mine?", and it was the *not mine* answer that went.

Raising opacity to 0.78 fixed the measured colour bar but none of the three causes.

**Root cause of the DEFECT (still open).** Uniform alpha is the wrong technique for this cue in this renderer. What would work is a treatment that does not depend on blending: a contour, a hatch, or a colour chosen for contrast against the building palette rather than for ownership alone. The enemy coral in particular should be re-picked against the roof palette, which is a change that needs no engine feature and no transparency.

**How it is checked from now on.** It is not, and that is the honest state — no gate covers "does this cue read as behind rather than on top", because it is a judgement about a rendered image. What the attempt DID leave behind is the measurement method: capture background / opaque / candidate under both art styles, solve the per-pixel alpha, and measure mean RGB distance from the local background for BOTH ownership colours over both a bright and a dark building. Any future attempt that does not do that is guessing, and the first attempt's 0.55 was a guess that looked right in one capture of one art style with one owner.

**What this predicts.** Every other overlay that might reach for transparency has the same two traps waiting — the moebius quantiser and self-blending — and every two-colour ownership cue should be checked in BOTH colours against the palette it will sit on, not just the one the showcase fixture happens to spawn.
