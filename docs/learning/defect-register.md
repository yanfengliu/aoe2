# Defect register

The standing list of what the gates could not see. One entry per defect that reached a build: the symptom as it presented, what the investigation found, the root cause, and the check that covers the defect's whole CLASS from now on.

Unlike a lesson, an entry stays after it becomes a gate. The register is not a to-do list — it is the record of where defects came from, which is the best available guide to where the next one is.

**What lives here, and what rolled over.** This file holds the 12 most recent CLOSED entries plus every OPEN one, whatever its age, newest first. Everything older and closed is in [`defect-register-past.md`](defect-register-past.md), moved verbatim. An OPEN entry never rolls over — it leaves this file by being closed, not by ageing, and a heading that says the defect is still live is not a closure however it spells it: "(LATENT, not fixed)", "(PARTLY FIXED: ...)" and "(... still unfixed)" all keep the entry here, and each of those three had been archived once before the gate learned to refuse them. The rollover is done by the task whose new entry pushes this file past 12 closed entries, in that task's own commit, the way the devlog archives its detailed file; never by a later sweep. `tests/architecture/defectRegisterRollover.test.ts` holds all of that. An "entry above" or "entry below" written before the split may name an entry that now lives in the archive, and a rollover can strand a pointer in either direction; the archive's header lists the references that cross the split.

## 2026-10-14 — The train button quoted an Italian Fishing Ship at 75 wood while the Dock charged 64, and every civilization and technology price the card stated was a second copy that could drift (agent-observed, found by work 120 M3, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** Agent-observed, not reported by a player. Needs an open production queue slot." and one click on that button took 64 wood. The census written for the class, run against the old reads put back one at a time, named the rest. Every civilization unit discount and Shipwright: "Goths dark-age pass 0 barracks train-militia: the card quoted 50 food, 20 gold, the click charged 43 food, 17 gold", "Britons imperial-age pass 3 dock train-fishing-ship: the card quoted 75 wood, the click charged 60 wood" (20 of the 30 civilizations). Every civilization research discount: "Chinese feudal-age pass 0 barracks research-man-at-arms-upgrade: the card quoted 100 food, 40 gold, the click charged 95 food, 38 gold", and the Italians' Feudal Age at 500 food against 425. Spies' tooltip stated its rule ("200 gold per enemy villager (halved by Atheism)"), not its price, and its validator gated it at the 200-gold floor, so with three enemy villagers and 300 gold the button looked affordable and the accepted click did nothing. After Guilds the Market's refusal quoted the 30% fee ("this trade costs 130") while the trade charges 15% (115), and its validator refused a buy the trade would take. A refused train's toast named the first short resource by the base table: an Inca Militia (43 food and 20 gold in the Dark Age) refused with 45 food and 10 gold said "Not enough food.". The replay bridge priced build cards and the tribute fee from the base table. Build cards in the live game were right: they read the bridge's `getConstructionCost`.

**Investigation.** Each price had as many computations as places that state or gate it. The charges read the per-owner rules inline: `effectiveTrainingCost` in `enqueueTraining`, `effectiveResearchCost` or `spiesCost` in `enqueueResearch`, `marketFeeRateFor` in the Market trade. The validators read some of them (`queue.research` read `effectiveResearchCost` for Spies too; `market.action` a constant 30% fee threaded in as a dep, which the trade itself had stopped reading, `void marketFeeRate;`). The rejection toast read `trainingCost`, with a comment saying the base table was "fine here".

**Root cause.** The civilization layer was added to the charges and their gates (v0.1.81 onward) and never to the HUD's price text, because nothing tied a quoted price to the computation that charges it: the "must be used at every training-cost site" comments at the charge sites were prose, and the HUD imported the base tables directly.

The charges, the three validators, the unavailable reasons, the toasts and the agent's research prices read them, and the constant fee deps are gone. The price of every drawn train, research and build command travels to the HUD in `SelectionState.commandCosts`, computed beside `unavailableCommands`. The tooltip formatters take the price they are given and state none when given none. The panel's render signature covers the selection state, so a quote follows a price that moves while the selection stands still. The replay bridge reports the recorded owner's building price and tribute fee. Left for a separate card, because the first two change self-play and want the coverage lab's two-way census to measure them: the AI's own affordability checks still read the base training table (six sites: `aiSystemProductionPhase.ts` four, `aiTradePhase.ts` and `aiFerryPhase.ts`), so a discounted AI waits for the base price before it asks; the AI prices Spies at its 200-gold floor (`aiSystemProductionPhase.ts` lines 164 and 191), so an Imperial AI's Castle can stall on a Spies order the validator refuses and never reach the technologies after it; a repair is priced from the base training table (`unitRepair.ts` line 28); and a research tooltip's time is the base research time, without the team research-speed bonuses. ## 2026-10-14 — A relic in a Monastery paid 10 gold a second, twenty times Definitive Edition's 0.5, and the Aztec bonus was wrong by the same factor (agent-observed by work 120 M4's implementation worker, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** Agent-observed, not reported by a player: work 120 M4's worker, reading the Aztec team bonus, saw relic gold paid at 10 gold a second. Work 127 reproduced it on the bridge through the real relic path: in `monk-relic-fixture` the Monk was ordered onto the relic and then into the Monastery, as a right-click does it, and each of the next ten minutes (600 ticks each) paid 600 gold, where spec Appendix A and Definitive Edition give 30. On an Aztec side three relics paid 24,000 gold in ten minutes, 13.3 a second each, where DE gives 1,200.

**Investigation.** The simulation runs 10 ticks a second: `TPS = 10` in `prototypeScenario.ts`, and the bridge's step turns 1,000 ms into exactly 10 ticks (now the first case of the new test file). `relicGoldSystem.ts` added each Monastery's relic count to its owner's gold on every tick, and the count again on every third tick for a side with the Aztecs. DE's rate, from two sources: AoE2ScenarioParser's attribute list gives resource 191, "Relic gold generation rate in gold per minute", a default of 30 (0.5 a second) and 15 after Atheism; Forgotten Empires' gold-and-relics page gives 2 seconds a gold per relic and 1.5 for the Aztecs, so 40 a minute and 2,400 an hour. Spec §6.9 said only "passive gold", Appendix A gave 0.5 a second as a target, and the team-bonus line restated the per-tick rule. Nothing tested the rate in seconds: `monastery.relic.test.ts` and the browser spec `game-progression-production.spec.ts` both pinned "+1 per tick per stored relic". No self-play gate saw it either: in the castle and lab gate matches on the boot map no relic is ever stored in 45,000 ticks, so relic income there is zero at either rate.

**Root cause.** The rate was written per tick, one gold a tick, and never converted to seconds when the target was written down, so the code and the target disagreed by the tick rate times two. The Aztec bonus was written as a third more of that per-tick rate, so it was wrong by the same factor.

**Fix.** Each relic is paid on an integer schedule. By the end of tick n of the match a relic held since the start has earned floor(n × gold-per-minute / 600) gold, and each tick pays the whole gold that total crosses: one gold on every 20th tick, and on every 15th for a side with the Aztecs, the owner's own civilization or an ally's. There is no float and no new saved state: the schedule reads the tick number and the saved `relicsInMonastery` counts only.

**Bound: saves and replays.** A game saved part way through a period loads onto the same schedule, because the world's tick is in the save: nothing is lost or paid twice, and a case gates it. A replay re-derives the schedule the same way. A replay recorded before this change plays out differently from the first relic payout, and a game saved before it keeps the gold the old rate paid. What the integer schedule does not keep is DE's running fraction of a gold: a relic that arrives or leaves part way through a period is paid on the game's payout ticks, so one stay can be paid up to one gold more or less than DE would pay it. DE's Atheism also halves enemy relic gold (resource 191 drops to 15); `technologies.csv` lists only the countdown and Spies halves, and that half is not modelled.

**Checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/relicGoldRate.test.ts`, eight cases: 10 ticks to a second of game time; a relic a Monk carries in pays 30 gold in each of ten minutes; any 20 consecutive ticks pay exactly one gold per relic, and any 15 on an Aztec side, from every start tick, so every phase; two relics in one Monastery pay 600 in ten minutes, and three relics in two Monasteries 900 while one relic pays 300; an Aztec owner 1,200; an Aztec ally on a Britons side 1,200, the Aztecs' enemy still 900 and the Aztecs 400; two relics that leave after 340 ticks are paid 17 each; and a game saved at tick 7 pays the same 90 over 600 ticks as one never saved. The expected numbers are worked by hand from the two periods. Mutations, each restored by bytes: the old file turns 7 of the 8 cases red, all but the tick-rate case (the ten minutes read "expected [ 600, 600, … ]", the removal case "expected 1280 to be 64"); no Aztec bonus turns 3 red (`expected [ 900, 300 ] to deeply equal [ 1200, 300 ]`); the Aztec rate on every side 7; the bonus for an Aztec owner but not an ally 1 (`expected [ 900, 300, +0 ] to deeply equal [ 1200, 300, +0 ]`); a schedule from a counter the save does not keep 1 (`expected 87 to be 90`); one payout per Monastery instead of per relic 6; 31 gold a minute instead of 30, 7; and 39 a minute for the Aztecs instead of 40, 3.

The two checks that pinned the old rate now expect one gold per 20 ticks. `monastery.relic.test.ts`'s case is red with the old file (`expected 20 to be 1`), and so is the browser spec on a build of it (`Expected: 1`, `Received: 20`), green on the fixed build. The AI's relic case in `aiPlayerMonksAndWonder.test.ts` went red on the new rate for a reason that was not the AI's: it waited for the relic to leave the map and for owner 2's gold to climb above its 200 starting gold. On the live bridge the AI's Monk stores the relic at tick 32, after the AI has spent its starting gold, and the relic then pays 1 gold by tick 40 and 10 by tick 220, so the old proxy would need about 4,000 ticks; at the old rate it fired at about tick 233. With the coordinator's approval it now reads the deposit directly (no relic on the map, and the relic slot holding one relic in a Monastery owner 2 owns) and the payout as gold rising on some tick of the 20 after the deposit. Each part is red alone: a deposit that stores nothing, `expected [] to have a length of 1 but got +0`; a relic system that pays nobody, `expected false to be true`. The payout reading would break, for a reason that has nothing to do with relics, if the AI spent gold on the very tick the relic pays; the case's header says so.

## 2026-10-14 — A gatherer holding more than the new resource lets it carry took a negative swing, so the node gained food (found by the independent review of work 120 M4, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** Found by the coordinator's independent review of work 120 M4 at `64d07754`, not by a player. A farmer with Heavy Plow ("Farmers carry +1 food") fills its hands with 11 food. Right-clicked onto a berry bush, which lets a forager carry 10, before it had dropped its load off, its first swing took -1: the bush gained a food and the hands lost one, for every civilization. Reproduced through the right-click path before the fix (`tests/simulation/gatherSwingNeverNegative.test.ts`, both cases red): 29 civilizations whose farmers carry, each "the bush went from 400 to 401" and "the hands went from 11 to 10" (an Aztec's from 14 to 13; a Mayan bush went to 400.87, its 1.15 duration dividing the -1). On the M4 branch a Portuguese forager's wood beside that swing came to -0.3333, paid as -1 wood (5 to 4). That payment marked the stockpile changed only when it paid more than 0, so the save never saw the -1: a save gave the wood back, and the review's `selfCheck` diverged on a recording with two foragers.

**Investigation.** The swing was `min(per-swing amount, what the node has left, carry - what the gatherer holds)` in `villagerEconomySystem.ts`, and the last term is below 0 whenever a gatherer holds more than the new resource lets it carry. Heavy Plow's +1 on farms is the case measured; a carry bonus on one kind of food does the same by that arithmetic. A load of another resource relabelled as the new one when gathering starts is a separate defect, and the coordinator gave it its own card.

**Root cause.** A minimum over three terms, one of which can go below 0, with nothing holding the result at 0, and no test in which a gatherer arrived holding more than the new carry. Work 120 M4 then made the hole cost wood as well as food, and its save flag was guarded on a payment above 0.

**Fixed (v0.3.255).** The swing is `max(0, ...)`: the overfull gatherer takes nothing and goes to drop its load off. The in-place payment marks the stockpile changed whenever it pays anything (`paid !== 0`).

**Checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/gatherSwingNeverNegative.test.ts`: the reviewer's Portuguese farmer through the right-click path (the wood stays at 5, the bush at 400 and the hands at 11), and a census of every civilization but the Khmer, whose farmers hold nothing, reading the bush, the hands and the wood on every tick after the order. Removing the clamp turns both red (`expected 4 to be 5`, and 59 failures across the 29). The save flag's own gates are in the work 120 M4 entry below. Bounds: one overflow, Heavy Plow's +1 against a forager's carry, with the Aztecs' +3 on both sides of it.

## 2026-10-12 — Main's CI went red on some runs and not others, each time on a test running near its own time limit, and nothing said how near any other test ran (agent-observed by work 123, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** Agent-observed. On 2026-10-11 main's CI was red on six of seven runs. Each red run had a test at or past its own limit on one runner and not on the others. On windows-latest the 600-tick replay comparison ran 32.0-35.1 s of 30 s and the 700-tick lifecycle comparison 30.6-35.7 s. On the SwiftShader browser shard the 1920px resource readouts timed out. de-ground.spec.ts:139 passed at 89.8 s of 90 s. The job logs held per-file totals and five browser tests per shard, so a test creeping towards its limit was invisible until a slow runner timed it out.

**Investigation.** Work 123 read 84 CI job logs from 14 runs (`gh api actions/jobs/<id>/logs`). Each job's runner ran its tests at 0.57-1.32x of their medians, one factor per job: the runner, not the test. A probe that reran the same specs in a fresh process after a full shard matched the shard's times, which rules out a slowdown building up across a shard. Ten browser tests and three unit tests had been above two thirds of their limits on some run. Three more probes on 2026-10-13 ran the specs nearest the line on 12 runners of four CPU models, all with 4 cores. The slowest model, the AMD EPYC 7763, ran one list in 637 s where an AMD EPYC 9V45 took 362 s, 1.76x; two 7763 runners agreed within 2%.

**Root cause.** There was no per-test headroom check, only the timeouts, which fire on the slowest runner only. The cost behind the offenders was of six kinds:
- **Simulation:** a slow product path, boxed-in sheep replanning one A* per map cell (the entry below).
- **One long case:** whole-world snapshots compared tick by tick in a single case.

*What it actually was.* The AI has owned a Market since v0.1.91 and had exactly one reason to use it: `marketActionForAgeUpShortfall`, which fires when it qualifies for the next age and cannot afford it. *The shipped fix: the PLAN outranks the QUEUE.* Three parts, one idea, all of them Imperial-only.

`blockedBuildTarget` in `ai.ts` names the highest-priority building the owner wants and CANNOT pay for. That is a different building from the one `pickNextBuildTarget` returns whenever a cheap entry is affordable and a dear one above it is not, and the difference is the whole point: measured, the build order reads as blocked on a 60-wood FARM in 63 of 122 Imperial samples while the 200 wood for a Siege Workshop never forms, so nothing in the AI could name the Siege Workshop as the thing it was waiting for.

`marketActionForUnaffordableWant` in `aiMarketPlanning.ts` aims the trade at the plan — that blocked building, plus the technologies standing unbought at the owner's idle producers — before it aims at the unit queue.

`researchOutranksQueue` puts an affordable upgrade ahead of the next unit. Both loops push against the same unspent stockpile and the handler charges them in push order, so whichever runs second gets only what the first left; the unit loop asks every decision tick and an upgrade is bought once.

## 2026-09-03 — A decided match never ends, because the AI can only aim at a Town Center (PARTLY FIXED: 1 of 4 seeds resolved then; 3 of 4 after the 2026-09-04 garrison entry above, OPEN)

**Symptom.** The standing acceptance-test failure "matches do not resolve": at 60,000 ticks — 100 minutes of game time — every seed is still running. Measured on four seeds after the wood fix, all four still ran to the horizon.

**Investigation.** `gold-rush` gave the clean case. From tick 48,000 to 60,000, sampled every 4,000 and byte-identical at every sample, owner 2 held:

    pop 0/5   villagers 0   army 0   buildings: blacksmith x1, house x1

Zero units. No Town Center, so it can never produce a unit again. It is functionally dead and cannot recover. Meanwhile owner 1 stood at 130 population with an army of 90, building farms.

**Root cause.** Two rules that are each correct and together deadlock.

`conquestOutcomeSystem` ends a match only when a player has NO UNITS **AND NO BUILDINGS** — which is right; a player with a building standing has not been conquered.

`pickAttackTarget` chooses the AI's march target from `townCentersByOwner`, a map keyed on owners that still HAVE a Town Center. Raze it and that owner drops out of the target list entirely: `targetOwner` becomes null, the attack phase's push branch has nothing to march on, and the surviving buildings are attacked only if they happen to fall inside somebody's vision. The AI is structurally unable to finish what it started.

So the winner cannot satisfy the condition the loser is being kept alive by.

**Fix, and the first version of it was WRONG in a way worth recording.** The obvious shape — give `pickAttackTarget` a fallback map and let it return the surviving building in `targetOwner` / `targetTownCenterId` / `targetTownCenterPosition` — passed its own unit gate, resolved `gold-rush`, and REGRESSED THE AGE-UP: `aiPlayer.test.ts` :: "ages up through the ages within a generous tick budget" went red, the AI reaching Feudal at 2,061 and never Castle within 8,000 ticks. Cause: `targetTownCenterPosition` is not private to the attack phase. `aiFerryPhase` reads it too, and when it is set with the target not land-reachable it stands down the ordinary march AND discretionary building to fund a Transport Ship — so filling that field for a Town-Center-less enemy stalled the economy. Isolated by disabling only the fallback and re-running: 24 of 24 passed.

The shipped fix is therefore SURGICAL. `pickAttackTarget` is byte-identical to what it was. A separate `lastResortTargetId` / `lastResortTargetPosition` pair is computed alongside it and read by the attack phase and nothing else, in its final push branch, only when no enemy Town Center is left. Deterministic — owners and entity ids both sorted, so a replay picks the same building. `gold-rush` still resolves at exactly tick 41,967, and the age-up test is green.

**The lesson, which is about the FIELD and not the feature.** A context field named for one thing (`targetTownCenterPosition`) had a second reader with different semantics, and reusing it to mean "the place to march" was correct for the phase being edited and wrong for the phase that was not. The cost of finding that was one full gate run; the cost of not finding it would have been a shipped economy regression that the resolution measurement would have applauded.

**Effect, measured on four seeds at 60,000 ticks, before and after.** `gold-rush` goes from running-at-60,000 to **victory at tick 41,967**. The other three still run, and the reasons are DIFFERENT defects rather than this one failing:

- `arena` and `coastal`: owner 2 still HAS a Town Center, so the fallback never engages. Owner 1 has an army of 105 and does not finish it — on arena that base sits inside the script's own stone wall ring, which points at attack EXECUTION (pathing to or breaching a walled base) rather than at targeting.
- `fortress`: neither side has an army to attack with (2 and 0 in the Imperial age). That is the villager-target-versus-population-cap chain in the entry above, not this one.

**How it is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/aiAttacksAnyEnemy.test.ts` :: `an enemy with no Town Center is still a target` — four cases: the fallback fires when the enemy has lost its Town Center; a Town Center still outranks a much closer bare building; the two-argument form is unchanged; and an ALLY without a Town Center is never targeted. Red-checked by reintroducing the defect (iterating an empty list instead of the fallback map): the first case fails with `expected null to be 2`, and all four pass again on revert.

**Bound of that gate, named.** It is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE. It does not prove the army reaches the building, kills it, or that the match ends — arena and coastal are live proof that a correct target is not sufficient. The end-to-end claim rests on the `gold-rush` seed alone, at one horizon.

**What this predicts.** The remaining two causes are both "the army exists and does not close": walls it will not breach, and an army too small to matter. Both are about what the military DOES rather than what it is pointed at, and neither is touched here.

**What it actually was, found 2026-09-04.** The prediction above was half right and half wrong, and the wrong half is the useful one. It reads `arena` and `coastal` as "the army exists and does not close" — walls, or too few units. The army was not closing because it was never ORDERED to: the attack phase's first preference is the target enemy's villager, and a GARRISONED villager is alive with no position, so the order silently no-opped and the branch `continue`d before the fallbacks this entry added. Those fallbacks were correct and were never reached. See the 2026-09-04 entry above; with that fixed, three of four seeds resolve.

**And the bound named above is exactly where it hid.** This gate is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE, and it says so. The defect was one line earlier, in a preference that never got as far as choosing.

## 2026-08-30 — Gather over-subscription counts EVERY owner's villagers (LATENT, not fixed, OPEN)

**What it is.** `villagerEconomySystem` builds `gatherTargetCounts` by walking `activeWorld.query('gatherer')` with no owner filter, so every gatherer in the match counts toward every resource's occupancy. ## 2026-08-30 — The behind-building silhouette reads as a unit standing ON the building (OPEN)

**Symptom.** A unit hidden behind a building is cued by a flat-coloured mirror of its body drawn over the building. It is fully opaque, and at a Town Center that reads as a villager standing on the roof rather than as an x-ray of one behind the wall — noticed hands-on on 2026-08-29 ("three of them across the TC's upper tier read as villagers standing on the roof") and confirmed in the `occlusion-showcase-fixture` capture, where the blue figure sits on the roof with nothing to say it is a cue.

**Investigation.** The obvious fix — draw the mirror on a transparent material so the building reads through it — was implemented, measured, and REVERTED. It failed on three independent axes, each measured rather than argued:

1. *It is close to a no-op under the art style the game actually ships.* The default is `moebius`, whose tone-band quantiser snaps **24.1% of the cue's pixels byte-identical to the fully opaque render**; mean change against opaque is 46.6 there against 125.6 under `painted`. One opacity value cannot serve a banding and a non-banding pipeline.
2. *It introduces self-blending seams.* The mirror copies a unit's own overlapping parts, and a translucent instanced part that overlaps itself blends twice — solved per-pixel as 452 px at the declared 0.55, 68 at 0.7975 (1−0.45²) and 6 at 0.9089 (1−0.45³). About 14% of the body draws at the wrong opacity, as streaks down the arm and torso. The engine has the cure and does not export it (see `docs/engine-feedback/current.md`).
3. *It degrades the half of the cue that matters most.* The enemy colour is a coral in the same hue family as every roof in the palette, so blending erases it: mean RGB distance from the background over a bright roof fell from 204.2 opaque to **82.0**, while the friendly blue — orange's complement — held at 196.9. The cue exists to answer "are those mine?", and it was the *not mine* answer that went.

Raising opacity to 0.78 fixed the measured colour bar but none of the three causes.

**Root cause of the DEFECT (still open).** Uniform alpha is the wrong technique for this cue in this renderer. What would work is a treatment that does not depend on blending: a contour, a hatch, or a colour chosen for contrast against the building palette rather than for ownership alone. The enemy coral in particular should be re-picked against the roof palette, which is a change that needs no engine feature and no transparency.

**How it is checked from now on.** It is not, and that is the honest state — no gate covers "does this cue read as behind rather than on top", because it is a judgement about a rendered image. What the attempt DID leave behind is the measurement method: capture background / opaque / candidate under both art styles, solve the per-pixel alpha, and measure mean RGB distance from the local background for BOTH ownership colours over both a bright and a dark building. Any future attempt that does not do that is guessing, and the first attempt's 0.55 was a guess that looked right in one capture of one art style with one owner.

**What this predicts.** Every other overlay that might reach for transparency has the same two traps waiting — the moebius quantiser and self-blending — and every two-colour ownership cue should be checked in BOTH colours against the palette it will sit on, not just the one the showcase fixture happens to spawn.
