# Defect register

The standing list of what the gates could not see. One entry per defect that reached a build: the symptom as it presented, what the investigation found, the root cause, and the check that covers the defect's whole CLASS from now on.

Unlike a lesson, an entry stays after it becomes a gate. The register is not a to-do list — it is the record of where defects came from, which is the best available guide to where the next one is.

**What lives here, and what rolled over.** This file holds the 12 most recent CLOSED entries plus every OPEN one, whatever its age, newest first. Everything older and closed is in [`defect-register-past.md`](defect-register-past.md), moved verbatim. An OPEN entry never rolls over — it leaves this file by being closed, not by ageing, and a heading that says the defect is still live is not a closure however it spells it: "(LATENT, not fixed)", "(PARTLY FIXED: ...)" and "(... still unfixed)" all keep the entry here, and each of those three had been archived once before the gate learned to refuse them. The rollover is done by the task whose new entry pushes this file past 12 closed entries, in that task's own commit, the way the devlog archives its detailed file; never by a later sweep. `tests/architecture/defectRegisterRollover.test.ts` holds all of that. An "entry above" or "entry below" written before the split may name an entry that now lives in the archive, and a rollover can strand a pointer in either direction; the archive's header lists the twelve that cross today.

## 2026-09-26 — Found by the independent review of v0.3.239: unique melee units strike with pierce damage, the accuracy and wind-up tables disagree with DE on nineteen units, and four content differences from DE are open (OPEN)

**Symptom.** Measured on `v0.3.239`'s tree, in the tables, not yet in a match. Twenty-three units that strike at contact deal pierce damage, because `MELEE_UNITS` holds only the stock lines: the Eagle Warrior line, the nine unique melee lines (Jaguar Warrior, Cataphract, Woad Raider, Huskarl, Tarkan, Samurai, War Elephant, Teutonic Knight, Berserk and their elites), the Petard and both Demolition Ships. A Teutonic Knight hits a Huskarl for 6 where melee gives 12, a Woad Raider hits a Battering Ram for 1 where melee gives 11, and a Huskarl stands in a formation's second rank. The accuracy and wind-up tables (`projectileRules.ts`) disagree with DE on nineteen units, measured with the game's own `unitAccuracy` and `projectileLaunchDelayTicks` against the pinned dataset's `AccuracyPercent` and `AttackDelaySeconds`, wind-up in the game's whole ticks of 0.1 s. Two also disagree with `units.csv`: the Hand Cannoneer never misses and looses at once (`units.csv` 65% and 0.45 s; DE 75% and 4 ticks), and the Longboat looses after 4 ticks where `units.csv` gives 9 s and DE 0. On the other seventeen `units.csv` agrees with the game, so a differential over `units.csv` cannot see them. Accuracy: the Heavy Cavalry Archer 50% (DE 80%), the Elite Longbowman 70% (80%), the Elite Plumed Archer 80% (90%), the Bombard Cannon 92% (100%) and the Elite Janissary 50% (65%). Wind-up: the Longbowman line 4 ticks (DE 5), the Trebuchet 6 (9), the Mangudai 10 and its elite 0 (both 5), the Throwing Axeman 12 (10), the Mameluke 6 and its elite 0 (4 and 2), the Janissary 3 (4), the Turtle Ship line 0 (2), and by one tick the Cavalry Archer line 10 (9) and the Arbalest 4 (3). Both Cannon Galleons' 100% agrees with DE where `units.csv` says 50%. The fire ships are not compared: DE gives them no accuracy.

**Also open, from the same review and the v0.3.239 work, each recorded in the spec:** DE gives melee damage to the Mangonel line, the Bombard Cannon, the Cannon Galleon line, the Throwing Axeman, the Mameluke and the Fire Ship, which deal pierce here (§10.2); DE's Furor Celtica gives +40% to "Siege Weapons" where this game gives +50% to Siege Workshop units, and DE's Shinkichon now serves Rocket Carts and Turtle Ships (§9.2.2); the Cannon Galleon and Turtle Ship lines' shots fly at arrow speed, and the AoE2 wiki reports DE's Cannon Galleon shot at 3 tiles a second, not checked against the game files (§10.4); DE's musket balls, thrown axes and scimitars and fire-ship flames have no look and are drawn as arrows (§10.4); and a target that walks inside a player's unit's minimum range after the unit took it on its own is held, not dropped, because the taken target and the player's order look the same (§10.4).

**Found by the third review, older than v0.3.239:** an AI-owned Trebuchet that has unpacked can be ordered at a target beyond its range and hold that order for good, because only a move order packs it (`playerCommandsSystem.ts`) and the attack step holds a stationary Trebuchet at an out-of-range target (`attackCommandStep.ts`). It already happened when its target died; an order replaced for being inside the minimum is one more way in. The AI never trains a Trebuchet, so only a converted one or a forced AI seat reaches it.

**Why it is not fixed with v0.3.239.** Each changes combat for whole lines and needs its own play check, and each is a different table from the tiers that entry closed. Under the local rule DE wins, so each is a defect, not a choice.

**How it would be checked.** The melee set by a differential over `units.csv`, a unit striking with melee damage exactly when its range is 0 and it has an attack, with DE's melee-damage projectiles as a separate attack type from `firesProjectile`. Accuracy and wind-up by re-sourcing the `accuracy` and `attack_delay` columns of `units.csv` from DE first, then a differential over them at tick resolution, each remaining exception named with its source and red once dissolved, as the minimum range is checked in `tests/content/baseStats.test.ts`; a differential over the columns as they stand would pass on all seventeen. A dropped automatic target by a census case whose target walks inside the minimum, once an order records whether a player gave it.

## 2026-09-26 — The Siege Onager, the Capped Ram and the Elite Skirmisher were left out of tables that named the rest of their line, and seven units fired inside the minimum range units.csv gives them (found on 2026-09-24 while fixing the Siege Onager's splash, FIXED and gated v0.3.239)

**Symptom.** Read in code by the v0.3.236 task and flagged in its entry's bounds, then reproduced in play before any fix. Over the same six cells, the Onager's stone flew 12 ticks and the Siege Onager's shot 9, drawn as an arrow; the Cannon Galleon and Turtle Ship lines' shots were arrows too (the new `projectile-look-showcase-fixture`, tick 5). A Celt's Onager at 90 hit points (Furor Celtica's +50% on 60) came out of the Siege Onager upgrade at 70, not 105; a Celt's Battering Ram at 263 came out of the Capped Ram upgrade at 200, not 300, and got the bonus back from the Siege Ram upgrade (405); a Briton's Skirmisher came out of the Elite Skirmisher upgrade at range 5, not 6 (the new `upgrade-keeps-line-bonuses-*` fixtures). The Mongols' Drill and the Koreans' Shinkichon left out the same tiers. The Capped Ram's blows met pierce armour, 1 damage to a Skirmisher where melee gives 3, and it stood in a formation's second rank. On the new minimum-range census, 12 of 33 ordered cases failed: a Skirmisher shot a unit in its own cell, a Trebuchet a unit 3 cells away and a building 1 away, both Cannon Galleons a unit 2 away and a building 1 away, and both Scorpions a building 1 away and killed a unit next to them.

**Investigation.** A probe asked every exported predicate that sorts units into a class, 47 of them (projectile, look, speed, minimum range, melee, attack type, armour classes, domain, stance, target priority, every unique technology's `applies`), whether each of the 46 upgrades in `UNIT_LINE_UPGRADES` changes its answer. Every claim was then checked against Definitive Edition, update 185872, from the aoe2techtree dataset pinned at `3bb43b14`: its `MinRange` agrees with all eleven "min-max" rows of `units.csv`; its help texts give Yeomen to "Foot Archers and Skirmisher-line", Drill to "Siege Workshop Units", and say each of the eleven units "cannot attack at close range"; and they call the Cannon Galleon a "Siege Gunpowder Warship" and the Turtle Ship a "Siege Warship". The AoE2 wiki says the Turtle Ship "shoots cannonballs".

**Root cause.** The three tiers reached the roster on 2026-08-23 (spec §9.2) and reached only the tables someone remembered. Hand lists of a line's members sat beside the upgrade table that already says which units are one line, and nothing compared them. An upgrade rebuilds each unit it replaces from its new type (`upgradeOwnedUnits`), so a tier a table leaves out loses the table's effect the moment the research completes. The seven minimum ranges were never transcribed: the table held the four the mangonel and Bombard Cannon work needed, and the base-stat differential read only the maximum of a "min-max" range.

After the reviews, no unit is handed a target already inside its minimum except by its player's order. Automatic targeting passes over a unit or building inside it and takes the best one it can fire at, by priority and then distance, as a tower does. The AI does not order a unit at such a target, and replaces a kept order whose target has come inside as soon as it has a target the unit can fire at, since every order an AI-owned unit carries is the AI's own; until then the order stands. ## 2026-09-26 — A blast landed where DE's does not: on a building's north-west cell, on a demolition ship's target and its own side, and never around an animal (recorded in spec §10.7 by the review of v0.3.236, reported by the owner, FIXED and gated v0.3.238)

**Symptom.** Spec §10.7 recorded three differences from Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition in where a blast lands, found on 2026-09-24 by the independent review of the Siege Onager fix, and the owner suspected a fourth. A Mangonel's stone on a House came down on the House's anchor, the north-west cell of its footprint, so its splash reached units at the House's north and west sides and never its south and east. A Demolition Ship's blast was centred on its target. An attack on a wolf or a boar took the damage off the animal at once, with no shot and no blast, even from an archer or the mangonel line. And every blast hurt friend and foe alike, where the AoE2 wiki's Demolition Ship page says its blast "does not harm friendly units".

**Investigation.** Each claim was checked against DE before anything changed, `design/stats/` first: of its rows only the Demolition Ship's help text speaks to any of them ("Pilot near enemy ships and detonate"). Then references, from a cloud session whose network reaches GitHub but not the AoE2 wiki, Liquipedia, the official forums, Forgotten Empires' site, Reddit or Steam (each refused by the egress policy): DE's own data, the `empires2_x2_p1.dat` of DE update 147949 as dumped by HSZemi/aoe2dat (its last dump, 2025-06-26); the DE modding guide's source (Divy1211/aoe2de_ugc_guide at `1f0f7e8d`); openage's reverse-engineering notes (SFTtech/openage at `abfc45a2`); the field tooltips of Advanced Genie Editor (Tapsa/AGE at `0bd1b417`); and, for the pages the policy blocks, a search engine's text of them. Three held outright; the fourth held on every written source that speaks of the Demolition Ship, against the plain reading of DE's data, and is a reversible default.
- (1) The guide's attribute 11: a unit fires "at the exact centre of its target", and a mangonel's blast is centred on "the targeted point". DE's data gives every building a collision half-size around its position (House 1, Dock 1.5, Town Centre and Castle 2, Watch Tower 0.5), so a building's position is the centre of its footprint.
- (2) The help text above, and the wiki (the owner's quote, and the search engine's text of the page): the ship deals full damage even when it explodes before reaching its target, including when its owner deletes it, so the blast is the ship's own and not a blow on its target's cell. DE's data gives it no projectile and a range of 0. No source read places DE's blast on the target, and none shows DE's code for the ordinary case, where the ship stands beside its target.
- (3) DE gives a unit one Combat task, whose owner type 5 is "Gaia, neutral and enemy objects only", and one projectile (`ProjectileUnitID`), whatever it attacks; boars and wolves are units there, inside every blast's reach.
- (4) For: the wiki (the owner's quote, and the search engine's text), that its blast "does not harm friendly units"; the search engine's text of Forgotten Empires' "Powergaming 101 – Utilizing Demolition Rafts", that a raft's explosion damages "all enemy units" in its radius; openage's `doc/reverse_engineering/game_mechanics/accuracy.md`, which names no game version, that "petards and demolition ships do not injure friendlies", where the Onager's blast "hits all units regardless of their owner". Against: the guide's attribute 44 labels Blast Attack Level 2 "Damage nearby and allied units", with the example that "mangonels (blast attack: 2) can damage your own units", and DE's data marks the blasts that spare a side with flag 4 ("Damage enemy units only") or 8 ("Ignore friendly fire"), on the Hoplite and the Strategos (6), the Fire Archer (6) and the Grenadier (11); the demolition line is 66, level 2 with flag 64 ("Attenuate damage as distance from the centre of attack increases (infantry only)") and neither. What decided it: every written source that speaks of the ship says it spares friends, only the data's flags read plainly say otherwise, and no copy of DE runs here to settle it. The Battle Elephant's trample is level 2 with neither flag as well, and the guide's attribute 22 says "All enemy units inside this radius take damage" from elephants, Druzhina Halberdiers and Logistica Cataphracts, which suggests level 2 with no flag does not by itself give a blow delivered in person friendly fire; but attribute 22 is Blast Width, which sets the Mangonel's radius too, and read that literally the Mangonel would spare its own side, which attribute 44's example denies, so it cannot settle the point. The rule taken is that a blast delivered in person spares its side and a shot's does not.

Reproduced on a headless bridge with the tests below, written before the fix: 20 cases failed. Nine building shots were aimed at the anchor, `(24, 10)` where the House's centre is `(24.5, 10.5)`, and so on for the 3x3 and 4x4. A Siege Onager's stone on a House hurt the Villagers at `(23, 9)`, `(24, 9)`, `(25, 9)`, `(23, 10)` and `(23, 11)` and spared the seven on its south and east. Both demolition ships' charges at the Dock hurt an enemy witness 2.83 cells from where they went off, out of reach, and their charges at a unit went off at `(36, 17)`, away from where the layout had put the witnesses; a trace of the live bridge (`getDebugSnapshot`, in a probe since deleted) showed the approach step up a row and run east along it to the target's north side. An Archer's attack on a boar loosed no arrow. The Siege Onager's on a boar flew no shot; the Mangonel's and the Onager's failed first on the still field, the boar having charged on the tick the damage came off it. And the blast's unit cases for its own side and for animals.

**Root cause.** (1) `deliverUnitAttackOnBuilding` aimed the shot at `target.position`, which for a building is its anchor. (2) `resolveUnitAttackOnUnit` and `deliverUnitAttackOnBuilding` centred a blast delivered in person on `target.position`. (3) The attack step's wildlife branch (`attackCommandStep.ts`) predates the projectile model (§10.4) and was never routed through the delivery functions, so it subtracted `attackDamage` itself; and the blast queried units only, so a shot at an animal could not have blasted it either. (4) `applyUnitBlast` applied the mangonel line's model, "friend and foe alike", to every unit with a radius, and the demolition line was given one.

**Fixed.** A shot at a building aims at the centre of its footprint (`footprintCentre`). A detonation's blast is centred on the unit that detonates (`inPersonBlastCentre`). An attack on an animal goes through `deliverUnitAttackOnAnimal`: a shooter's is a shot with target kind `wildlife`, which rolls to hit and lands on its impact tick, a melee blow lands at once as before, and a detonating unit's charge, a Demolition Ship's or a Petard's, goes off and spends it as it does at anything else (before, it struck the animal again every reload and survived). Every blow on an animal is dealt by `animalDamage.ts`, which turns the animal on its attacker, and every blast reaches the animals in its radius. A blast a unit delivers in person spares its owner's units and its allies' (`blastSparesOwnSide`, read with the teams); a shot's blast hurts everyone in reach. The census fixture's land building became a one-cell Outpost, because every cell beside a 2x2 or larger building is more than 1.5 from its centre and a mangonel-line blast there reaches nobody a witness could stand for; `mangonel-vs-building-splash-fixture` became an Outpost for the same reason.

`blockedBuildTarget` in `ai.ts` names the highest-priority building the owner wants and CANNOT pay for. `marketActionForUnaffordableWant` in `aiMarketPlanning.ts` aims the trade at the plan — that blocked building, plus the technologies standing unbought at the owner's idle producers — before it aims at the unit queue.

`researchOutranksQueue` puts an affordable upgrade ahead of the next unit. Both loops push against the same unspent stockpile and the handler charges them in push order, so whichever runs second gets only what the first left; the unit loop asks every decision tick and an upgrade is bought once.

## 2026-09-03 — A decided match never ends, because the AI can only aim at a Town Center (PARTLY FIXED: 1 of 4 seeds resolved then; 3 of 4 after the 2026-09-04 garrison entry above, OPEN)

**Symptom.** The standing acceptance-test failure "matches do not resolve": at 60,000 ticks — 100 minutes of game time — every seed is still running. Measured on four seeds after the wood fix, all four still ran to the horizon.

**Investigation.** `gold-rush` gave the clean case. From tick 48,000 to 60,000, sampled every 4,000 and byte-identical at every sample, owner 2 held:

    pop 0/5   villagers 0   army 0   buildings: blacksmith x1, house x1

Zero units. No Town Center, so it can never produce a unit again. It is functionally dead and cannot recover. Meanwhile owner 1 stood at 130 population with an army of 90, building farms.

**Root cause.** Two rules that are each correct and together deadlock.

`conquestOutcomeSystem` ends a match only when a player has NO UNITS **AND NO BUILDINGS** — which is right; a player with a building standing has not been conquered.

`pickAttackTarget` chooses the AI's march target from `townCentersByOwner`, a map keyed on owners that still HAVE a Town Center. Raze it and that owner drops out of the target list entirely: `targetOwner` becomes null, the attack phase's push branch has nothing to march on, and the surviving buildings are attacked only if they happen to fall inside somebody's vision. The AI is structurally unable to finish what it started.

So the winner cannot satisfy the condition the loser is being kept alive by.

**Fix, and the first version of it was WRONG in a way worth recording.** The obvious shape — give `pickAttackTarget` a fallback map and let it return the surviving building in `targetOwner` / `targetTownCenterId` / `targetTownCenterPosition` — passed its own unit gate, resolved `gold-rush`, and REGRESSED THE AGE-UP: `aiPlayer.test.ts` :: "ages up through the ages within a generous tick budget" went red, the AI reaching Feudal at 2,061 and never Castle within 8,000 ticks. Cause: `targetTownCenterPosition` is not private to the attack phase. `aiFerryPhase` reads it too, and when it is set with the target not land-reachable it stands down the ordinary march AND discretionary building to fund a Transport Ship — so filling that field for a Town-Center-less enemy stalled the economy. Isolated by disabling only the fallback and re-running: 24 of 24 passed.

The shipped fix is therefore SURGICAL. `pickAttackTarget` is byte-identical to what it was. A separate `lastResortTargetId` / `lastResortTargetPosition` pair is computed alongside it and read by the attack phase and nothing else, in its final push branch, only when no enemy Town Center is left. Deterministic — owners and entity ids both sorted, so a replay picks the same building. `gold-rush` still resolves at exactly tick 41,967, and the age-up test is green.

**The lesson, which is about the FIELD and not the feature.** A context field named for one thing (`targetTownCenterPosition`) had a second reader with different semantics, and reusing it to mean "the place to march" was correct for the phase being edited and wrong for the phase that was not. The cost of finding that was one full gate run; the cost of not finding it would have been a shipped economy regression that the resolution measurement would have applauded.

**Effect, measured on four seeds at 60,000 ticks, before and after.** `gold-rush` goes from running-at-60,000 to **victory at tick 41,967**. The other three still run, and the reasons are DIFFERENT defects rather than this one failing:

- `arena` and `coastal`: owner 2 still HAS a Town Center, so the fallback never engages. Owner 1 has an army of 105 and does not finish it — on arena that base sits inside the script's own stone wall ring, which points at attack EXECUTION (pathing to or breaching a walled base) rather than at targeting.
- `fortress`: neither side has an army to attack with (2 and 0 in the Imperial age). That is the villager-target-versus-population-cap chain in the entry above, not this one.

**How it is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/aiAttacksAnyEnemy.test.ts` :: `an enemy with no Town Center is still a target` — four cases: the fallback fires when the enemy has lost its Town Center; a Town Center still outranks a much closer bare building; the two-argument form is unchanged; and an ALLY without a Town Center is never targeted. Red-checked by reintroducing the defect (iterating an empty list instead of the fallback map): the first case fails with `expected null to be 2`, and all four pass again on revert.

**Bound of that gate, named.** It is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE. It does not prove the army reaches the building, kills it, or that the match ends — arena and coastal are live proof that a correct target is not sufficient. The end-to-end claim rests on the `gold-rush` seed alone, at one horizon.

**What this predicts.** The remaining two causes are both "the army exists and does not close": walls it will not breach, and an army too small to matter. Both are about what the military DOES rather than what it is pointed at, and neither is touched here.

**What it actually was, found 2026-09-04.** The prediction above was half right and half wrong, and the wrong half is the useful one. It reads `arena` and `coastal` as "the army exists and does not close" — walls, or too few units. The army was not closing because it was never ORDERED to: the attack phase's first preference is the target enemy's villager, and a GARRISONED villager is alive with no position, so the order silently no-opped and the branch `continue`d before the fallbacks this entry added. Those fallbacks were correct and were never reached. See the 2026-09-04 entry above; with that fixed, three of four seeds resolve.

**And the bound named above is exactly where it hid.** This gate is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE, and it says so. The defect was one line earlier, in a preference that never got as far as choosing.

## 2026-08-30 — Gather over-subscription counts EVERY owner's villagers (LATENT, not fixed, OPEN)

**What it is.** `villagerEconomySystem` builds `gatherTargetCounts` by walking `activeWorld.query('gatherer')` with no owner filter, so every gatherer in the match counts toward every resource's occupancy. That map is what `MAX_GATHERERS_PER_RESOURCE` (2) is checked against, and its stated purpose is to stop ONE player piling villagers on one node. Counting the opponent's villagers is wrong for that purpose: an enemy working a neutral resource can make it read over-subscribed for you, and your villagers fan out away from a resource that has room.

**Why it is not fixed.** Measured before touching it, which is the point of this entry: across `aoe2-prototype`, `default-seed` and `corpus-seed-b`, sampling every 100 ticks for 12,000 ticks each, **not one tick had a resource targeted by two different owners** — 0 of 360 samples, max owners on any single resource 1. Every current map keeps the players' resource rings apart, so the defect cannot fire. Fixing it means threading a per-owner count through five call sites in two files for no measurable behaviour change today.

**The trigger to watch for.** It becomes live the moment two players' gatherers can reach the same node: a map with a contested middle (gold or stone between bases), overlapping resource rings on a small map, or allied players sharing a base. Any of those should come with the fix — key the counts by owner — and this entry is the note that the machinery is already wrong when they arrive.

**What this predicts.** A shared counter with no owner dimension is a latent cross-player coupling wherever it appears. `gatherTargetCounts` is one; the same shape is worth looking for in any per-tick map keyed only by entity id.

## 2026-08-30 — The behind-building silhouette reads as a unit standing ON the building (OPEN)

**Symptom.** A unit hidden behind a building is cued by a flat-coloured mirror of its body drawn over the building. It is fully opaque, and at a Town Center that reads as a villager standing on the roof rather than as an x-ray of one behind the wall — noticed hands-on on 2026-08-29 ("three of them across the TC's upper tier read as villagers standing on the roof") and confirmed in the `occlusion-showcase-fixture` capture, where the blue figure sits on the roof with nothing to say it is a cue.

**Investigation.** The obvious fix — draw the mirror on a transparent material so the building reads through it — was implemented, measured, and REVERTED. It failed on three independent axes, each measured rather than argued:

1. *It is close to a no-op under the art style the game actually ships.* The default is `moebius`, whose tone-band quantiser snaps **24.1% of the cue's pixels byte-identical to the fully opaque render**; mean change against opaque is 46.6 there against 125.6 under `painted`. One opacity value cannot serve a banding and a non-banding pipeline.
2. *It introduces self-blending seams.* The mirror copies a unit's own overlapping parts, and a translucent instanced part that overlaps itself blends twice — solved per-pixel as 452 px at the declared 0.55, 68 at 0.7975 (1−0.45²) and 6 at 0.9089 (1−0.45³). About 14% of the body draws at the wrong opacity, as streaks down the arm and torso. The engine has the cure and does not export it (see `docs/engine-feedback/current.md`).
3. *It degrades the half of the cue that matters most.* The enemy colour is a coral in the same hue family as every roof in the palette, so blending erases it: mean RGB distance from the background over a bright roof fell from 204.2 opaque to **82.0**, while the friendly blue — orange's complement — held at 196.9. The cue exists to answer "are those mine?", and it was the *not mine* answer that went.

Raising opacity to 0.78 fixed the measured colour bar but none of the three causes.

**Root cause of the DEFECT (still open).** Uniform alpha is the wrong technique for this cue in this renderer. What would work is a treatment that does not depend on blending: a contour, a hatch, or a colour chosen for contrast against the building palette rather than for ownership alone. The enemy coral in particular should be re-picked against the roof palette, which is a change that needs no engine feature and no transparency.

**How it is checked from now on.** It is not, and that is the honest state — no gate covers "does this cue read as behind rather than on top", because it is a judgement about a rendered image. What the attempt DID leave behind is the measurement method: capture background / opaque / candidate under both art styles, solve the per-pixel alpha, and measure mean RGB distance from the local background for BOTH ownership colours over both a bright and a dark building. Any future attempt that does not do that is guessing, and the first attempt's 0.55 was a guess that looked right in one capture of one art style with one owner.

**What this predicts.** Every other overlay that might reach for transparency has the same two traps waiting — the moebius quantiser and self-blending — and every two-colour ownership cue should be checked in BOTH colours against the palette it will sit on, not just the one the showcase fixture happens to spawn.
