# Defect register

The standing list of what the gates could not see. One entry per defect that reached a build: the symptom as it presented, what the investigation found, the root cause, and the check that covers the defect's whole CLASS from now on.

Unlike a lesson, an entry stays after it becomes a gate. The register is not a to-do list — it is the record of where defects came from, which is the best available guide to where the next one is.

**What lives here, and what rolled over.** This file holds the 12 most recent CLOSED entries plus every OPEN one, whatever its age, newest first. Everything older and closed is in [`defect-register-past.md`](defect-register-past.md), moved verbatim. An OPEN entry never rolls over — it leaves this file by being closed, not by ageing, and a heading that says the defect is still live is not a closure however it spells it: "(LATENT, not fixed)", "(PARTLY FIXED: ...)" and "(... still unfixed)" all keep the entry here, and each of those three had been archived once before the gate learned to refuse them. The rollover is done by the task whose new entry pushes this file past 12 closed entries, in that task's own commit, the way the devlog archives its detailed file; never by a later sweep. `tests/architecture/defectRegisterRollover.test.ts` holds all of that. An "entry above" or "entry below" written before the split may name an entry that now lives in the archive, and a rollover can strand a pointer in either direction; the archive's header lists the seven that cross today.

## 2026-09-06 — Select the whole army, attack-move across the map, and nothing happens at all (found by the standing loop, FIXED and gated v0.3.219)

**Symptom.** In a 93-minute Black Forest match at Castle Age, 58 units all reporting idle: `issueAttackMoveCommand` returns `true`, and over 9,200 ticks — about fifteen game minutes — the army's centre moves from (14.0, 10.0) to (15.0, 9.7) and then never changes again. No movement, no message, no console error. A plain move to the same cell does the same; so does a move to an open cell eleven tiles away. Short orders work: +4/+3 moved the centre half a tile, an attack-move of −5/+5 moved it about two tiles. This is why no battle had ever happened in this game.

**Attribution.** Found by the standing loop, playing the game. The owner reported none of it.

**Does the path exist? No — and that was established, not assumed.** An independent 4-connected flood over terrain plus building and resource footprints (never over the pathfinder under test), run on a live bridge the way `scripts/aiArmyBlock.mjs` drives one: on `black-forest` with the human idle and only owner 2's AI building, the open ground falls from 370 cells at tick 0 to 315 by tick 20,000, the human's own component from 368 cells to 205, and **enemy units reachable from it from 4/4 to 0/26**. With both sides AI the human's units split into 50-, 43- and 2-cell pockets by tick 15,000. The map seals itself with the buildings a normal match puts up. The control that proves the search itself was sound: a 38-tile order inside the component is carried out and arrives.

**But a real search bug was hiding behind it.** The fallback for "the ordered cell cannot be reached" scanned `getNearestMoveCandidates(target)` — every cell within `max(mapWidth, mapHeight)` = 60 MANHATTAN cells of the target — and ran a full A* per candidate until one succeeded. The greatest distance between two cells on a 60x36 map is 94. A unit sealed in a pocket more than 60 cells from the ordered cell therefore had **no candidate it could reach at all**: `resolveMovePlanFromCache` returned null and `playerCommandsSystem`'s `if (!movePlan) clearUnitCommand(id)` dropped the order in silence. The unit did not walk one step toward it. A gate fixture reproduces exactly that: the sealed unit went from (2,2) to (2,2).

**And the second half: nothing ever told the player.** `clearUnitCommand` on a null plan is the whole of the old behaviour. An order accepted and dropped is the same defect the placement validator and the command-availability work fixed, one layer out — and a player in that state has no way to learn anything is wrong. The same shape had been seen earlier on the boot map: villagers walled in by their owner's own houses went idle and every subsequent gather, build and move order was accepted and dropped.

**Fix.** `src/game/simulation/bridge/moveDestinationSearch.ts` owns where a walk order ends. A flood out of the unit's OWN cell has no radius to run out of, and it picks the reachable cell with the smallest `(manhattan-to-target, y, x)` — deliberately the same key the old ring scan used (rings by distance, row-major within a ring, first reachable wins), so every order the old scan could already carry out ends in exactly the same cell. `describeWalkOrderReach` is the sentence the player now gets, computed where the order is given and put on the rejection queue the HUD already drains into a toast.

**It is also far cheaper, measured with both arms in ONE process against ONE bridge so the tree could not move between them.** Reachable order: 0.89 ms → 0.02 ms per call. Sealed order across a wall: **162.91 ms → 1.09 ms**. Both arms returned the identical destination and the identical path length in every case, which is what makes the equivalence claim a measurement rather than an assertion. The old cost was one unit for one replan; 58 units in that state would have frozen the game.

**How this class is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/walkOrderUnreachable.test.ts` on the new `walled-pocket-fixture`: a long order across a reachable map is carried out, a short order still works (the control against a fix that refuses too much), an order that cannot be satisfied still walks the unit as far toward it as it can, and both a move and an attack-move that cannot be satisfied produce something the player can observe. Proven red on the unfixed tree with the two controls green, then proven again by two TARGETED mutations that bind each half to its own mechanism: silencing only the message reddens exactly the two message cases; restoring only the give-up fallback reddens exactly the movement case, with the same `(2,2) to (2,2)` text.

**Bounds, named.** ONE map size (60x36) and ONE sealed shape — a 6x6 pocket inside a closed ring of trees. A pocket sealed by BUILDINGS is the same predicate (`blocksWholeCell` covers both) but is not exercised. LAND units only: a ship ordered inland finds no water seed and stays silent. Move and attack-move only; patrol, attack-ground, gather, build and garrison walks share the resolver and are not asserted. **A unit standing on a cell that became blocked underneath it still cannot move at all** — the flood returns null, the command is still cleared, and only the toast covers it; that is the spawn-egress class and is untouched here.

## 2026-09-06 — A normal match seals its own map, so the armies can never meet (found by the standing loop, OPEN)

**Symptom.** No battle has ever happened in this game, on any seed, and the reason is not the AI's willingness to attack.

**Measurement, from the walk-order investigation of the same day.** An independent 4-connected flood over terrain plus building and resource footprints — never over the pathfinder, so this is not the search grading itself — run on a live bridge over `black-forest`, with the human idle and only owner 2's AI building:

| tick | terrain-open cells | open after buildings | human's own component | enemy units reachable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 442 / 2160 | 370 | one, 368 cells | 4 / 4 |
| 10,000 | 451 | 356 | one, 354 cells | 11 / 11 |
| 20,000 | 470 | 315 | one, **205** | **0 / 26** |

With BOTH sides played by the AI, the human's own units are split into 50-, 43- and 2-cell pockets by tick 15,000 — six units in a two-cell pocket they can never leave.

**What it is not.** It is not the pathfinder: a 38-tile order inside a component is carried out and arrives. It is not the walk-order defect fixed the same day in v0.3.219; that fix makes the units walk as far as they can and tells the player why, which is correct behaviour on a map that has sealed itself, and changes nothing about the sealing.

**What it is.** Buildings placed during ordinary play cut the map into pieces no army can cross. That is building placement and map generation — nothing in the AI's build logic asks whether the thing it is about to put down closes the last gap, and `black-forest` starts with only 17% of its cells open, so it has very little slack to spend.

**Why it stays open.** It needs a placement rule (a building may not be the wall that seals a component) and probably a map-generation guarantee about corridor width. Both are design decisions with balance consequences, not repairs. The v0.3.219 gate covers the ORDER-DISPATCH half of this and explicitly does not cover this half.

## 2026-09-06 — An enemy hit a villager twice in the player's own base and the screen said nothing, and the horn's own rule could never fire for a 4x4 building (found by the standing loop, FIXED and gated v0.3.217)

**Symptom.** At tick 3084 of `aoe2-prototype` an enemy militia walks into the human's base and hits villager #2205 twice, 25 -> 21 -> 17 HP. There was no banner, no toast, no minimap mark, no selection change and no message of any kind. AoE2 tells you when your economy is being killed; this told you nothing.

**Attribution.** Found by the standing loop, playing the game. The owner reported none of it.

**Two defects, and the second had never been hit by anything.**

**Defect one: there was no visual at all.** The horn already fired — counted in the live app by its own oscillators, not inferred from the code — so half the cue existed. A horn is the one cue a muted tab, a headphone-less player or a busy moment takes away, and nothing on screen said an attack was happening or where.

**Defect two: the horn's rule could not match a 4x4 building.** The old rule compared each recorded swing's target cell against every own building's and villager's RENDER ORIGIN, within one cell. A swing does not target the origin — `unitAttackAnimationFeed` records `targetRootX + (footprintWidth - 1) / 2`, the VISUAL CENTRE. For a 4x4 building that is **1.5 cells** away from the origin, so `Math.abs(entity.x - attack.targetX) <= 1` was false for every one of them: **the Town Center, the Market, the Castle and the Wonder were silently dropped by the check written to catch exactly this.** A 3x3 building sat at exactly 1.0 and matched on the boundary; villagers matched only because their footprint is 1x1 and the offset is zero. Nobody had hit it because the case that reproduces it is an attack on a 4x4 building specifically, and the play finding above is a villager.

**Root cause, and it ties both.** The attack feed is an ANIMATION feed. It records that a swing landed at a cell and discards the target id it was handed, because an animation only needs the cell. The one consumer that needs to know WHO was hit therefore had to infer it from coordinates, and it inferred it with the wrong convention. A rule reconstructed from geometry, when the identity was available and thrown away, is the shape of this defect rather than the off-by-1.5 itself.

**Fix.** The feed now carries `participants` — `attackerOwner`, `targetOwner`, and `targetIsEconomy` — read from the components at record time, on the ticks that already recorded a hit. `src/ui/hud/attackWarning.ts` owns the rule, `gameAudioController` becomes the single decision point for all three cues (horn, Space's jump target, minimap mark), and `mountGameAudio` no longer builds a list of own entities to compare against.

**The rule is now exact, and each narrowing was argued rather than assumed.** A warning is raised when a unit owned by ANOTHER PLAYER swings at a villager or a building owned by the human. Wildlife is excluded on both ends: a lured boar biting the villager that shot it happens in the opening of every match, on purpose, under the player's eye, and a horn for it is the storm the rule exists to avoid. Soldiers are excluded as targets: a villager losing health is news by definition, because nobody puts a villager in a fight on purpose, while a soldier taking a hit is the fight you went looking for. And the rule keys on who was HIT, never on who swung or on what happens to be standing nearby, so the Town Centre shooting back raises nothing.

**One tempting narrowing was explicitly REJECTED**: suppressing the warning when the camera is already looking at the spot. That reading is exactly backwards — the reproduced defect happened inside the player's own base with the camera on it, so a "you are already looking" rule would have suppressed the very case this exists for.

**Two placement decisions, both forced by existing rules.** The mark is raised OUTSIDE the mute switch, so a silenced player still sees where the raid is — the horn is the cue a real player most easily loses, which is the whole reason the visual was needed. And it is drawn ON the minimap rather than floating over it, because nothing may cover the minimap (local rules, 2026-09-02) and AoE2's own affordance is the minimap itself flashing.

**How this class is checked from now on.** Three gates. `tests/browser/attack-warning.spec.ts` (`npm run test:browser`) reproduces the raid on the real scenario at the real tick and asserts PIXELS on the minimap canvas — near-white ones, because owner 2's tint is itself a red and a "reddish" threshold would pass on the raider's own marker; the boot frame is asserted to carry zero such pixels, which is what makes the raid frame's count mean anything, and the spec fails with "no villager lost HP" if the scenario ever stops producing the raid, so a run that did not exercise the warning cannot report itself as one that did. `tests/ui/attackWarning.test.ts` holds the rule, the throttle and the mark over a synthetic feed, including the cases that must NOT warn. `tests/ui/gameAudio.test.ts` and `tests/ui/minimap.test.ts` were extended for the single decision point and the mark's paint.

**Bounds, named.** The browser gate is one scenario, one raid, and a tick window of 3050–3600. The feed records a SWING and only a unit can be an attacker in it, so an enemy tower or castle shooting a villager still raises nothing — the same gap the horn already had, and the case that matters (a raid, which is units) is covered. A saved game's restored attack entries carry no `participants`, because `hydrateUnitAttacks` drops them rather than re-validate a feed that is ten ticks deep; a load therefore starts silent until the next live swing. Nothing here checks that the mark is legible against every terrain, only that it is white enough to find.

**Known wrong text, not corrected here.** Twelve comments across the shipped source and test files date this work `v0.3.215`. It shipped as v0.3.217; v0.3.215 is the setup-screen fix from earlier in the same batch. The code is right and the comments are not. A thirteenth, on `ATTACK_WARNING_THROTTLE_TICKS`, is wrong about something that matters and has its own entry below. **Corrected in v0.3.218**: all twelve, across nine files, alongside the throttle entry's fix.

## 2026-09-06 — The attack-warning throttle is 40 seconds and its own comment says 20, because it computes at a tick rate this build does not use (found at integration, reading the constant, FIXED and gated v0.3.218)

**Symptom.** `ATTACK_WARNING_THROTTLE_TICKS = 400` in `src/ui/hud/attackWarning.ts`, with the comment "AoE2 spaces its 'town under attack' warnings well apart; ~20s at 20 TPS". This build runs at **10 TPS** — `export const TPS = 10` in `src/game/simulation/prototypeScenario.ts`, and the spec's own numbers agree throughout (§9.6's garrison heal is "4 HP per second at 10 ticks per second"; §11's Horse Collar is "200 ticks (20 s)"). So 400 ticks is **40 seconds of game time at speed 1.0**, twice what the comment says it is.

**Attribution.** Found at integration while checking a changelog sentence that quoted the comment's 20 seconds; nobody played it and no gate saw it. It predates v0.3.217: the constant was `HORN_THROTTLE_TICKS = 400` with the same comment before the attack warning moved it, so the horn has been spaced at 40 seconds for as long as it has existed.

**What is established and what is NOT.** Established: the constant, the tick rate, and therefore the 40 seconds. NOT established: what AoE2: Definitive Edition actually does. The comment asserts ~20s, and a remembered game rule is a hypothesis here (local rules, "Behaviour matches the real Age of Empires II") — so this entry deliberately does not say the value is wrong, only that the code's stated intent and the code's behaviour disagree by a factor of two and one of them has to give.

**Why it is worth an entry rather than a comment fix.** Whichever way it resolves, something user-visible changes or a documented intent does: either the throttle halves to 200 ticks and a raided player is warned twice as often, or the intent is restated at 40 seconds and the divergence from the comment's "AoE2's rule" is recorded as deliberate. That is a product decision against `design/stats/` and DE, not a typo.

**Resolution (v0.3.218). The throttle halved to 200 ticks, and the research result is a NEGATIVE one that has to survive.** DE's own spacing could not be established from any authoritative source. The best statement found is a single post on the official Age of Empires forum (HestiaAoE, 2023-10-14, thread "Under-Attack notifications are annoying and useless"), with no staff badge, citing a video the search could not read; neither the wiki nor the community databases document the alert at all, and no developer statement, data file or official document was found. More importantly the rule that post describes has a **different SHAPE**: it suppresses the alarm only when two attacks fall within ten TILES *and* ten seconds. This throttle is purely temporal, so two raids far apart alarm twice there and once here, and **there is no single DE number to copy**. The value is therefore what the comment always intended — twenty seconds — rather than a measured DE figure, and that is written into the constant's own comment so nobody repeats the search.

**Why 200 and not something smaller.** 200 is the largest correction that leaves the existing storm test still measuring what it claims. A smaller value would have meant rewriting an expectation to fit the change, which is the move this repo does not make.

**Open follow-up, and it is a feature gap rather than a defect:** the spatial half is unimplemented. DE's reported rule needs BOTH a distance and a time; ours has only the time, so a second raid on the opposite side of the base is silenced for twenty seconds when DE would alarm for it. Nothing here establishes that DE's rule is as that post describes, so implementing it would need the fact established first.

**How this class is checked from now on.** `tests/architecture/tickRateComments.test.ts` (`npm test`, so `npm run verify`) reads every `.ts`/`.tsx` file under `src/` and `tests/` and fails when a stated tick rate, a stated milliseconds-per-tick, or the arithmetic between a stated duration and a stated tick count disagrees with `TPS`. It scans **string literals as well as comments**, because this defect's twin was in a test TITLE — `is AoE2's spacing: 400 ticks, ~20 seconds at 20 TPS` — asserting the wrong tick count under the wrong rate. That is how it found a third instance, `bridge-additive-surfaces.test.ts` claiming 30 TPS. Its bounds are in its own header: it reads TEXT and cannot tell a true comment from a stale one that names no number; the arithmetic check needs a rate spelled with `TPS` in the same text, so a bare duration beside a tick constant is invisible to it; it skips a text with more than one duration or a slash list (three today) and one with no tick count to bind to (two today); 44 arithmetic pairs are checked and 5 skipped on the shipped tree.

## 2026-09-06 — A hidden unit was painted as a flat opaque slab on the wall, in a colour that was nobody's (found by the standing loop playing the boot map, FIXED and gated v0.3.216)

**Symptom.** The first thing a player saw on `aoe2-prototype` at tick 0 was a solid, unlit, bright blue humanoid pasted on the Town Centre's wall. It is the x-ray cue for a unit standing behind a building (§14.5), working badly in two separate ways at once. A unit behind a house read as a unit standing ON the roof, because a flat featureless shape in a world where every other box carries three differently lit faces reads as a decal stuck to the surface rather than as something behind it. And an enemy unit rendered vivid MAGENTA, which is no player's colour in this game or in AoE2.

**Attribution.** Found by the standing loop, playing the boot map. The owner reported none of it. (An earlier draft of this entry was headed as owner-reported and that was wrong — the same mistake the "Conquest Victory" entry made earlier the same day.)

**Why no existing test could see it.** `getOccludedUnitStates()` confirmed both figures were the intended cue and that every rule around it — the mid-body anchor, the occluder-region filter, the depth key, fog gating — was doing exactly what it was written to do. The machinery was correct. What was wrong was the lane it drew on and the colours it drew in, and nothing asserted either against anything a player would call legible.

**Root cause of the colours, which is the instructive half.** They had been chosen by MAXIMISING RGB DISTANCE FROM THE BUILDING PALETTE. That is a reasonable-sounding objective and it is the wrong one: maximising distance from the walls also maximises distance from the player colours, and the player colour is the entire message the cue carries. The magenta scored 137 from its nearest building material — better than anything else considered, and the previous coral's 76 was what it replaced — and it still failed at the only job the cue has, because a red player's unit behind a house read as a pink figure. In a game where colour means whose a thing is, a cue that names the wrong player is worse than no cue.

**Root cause of the read, and it is arithmetic rather than taste.** The cue was drawn on the `ui` lane: unlit and fully opaque. Nothing of the building came through it and nothing of the world's light landed on it, so head, torso and arms merged into one blob and the whole shape sat in front of the wall rather than behind it.

**Everything else here was measured, not argued.** Routing the colours through the existing `ownerTint` helper was tried and REJECTED, because that helper rotates hue while preserving luminance — right for a material shade, wrong for a saturated cue. The yellow slot lands within 70 of `roofTile` and the grey slot within 33 of `stoneDark`, which is invisible on a stone wall. The shipped colours are AoE2's own eight player hues, each within 10 degrees of its canonical angle, lightened until they clear every building material at a floor of 90; two slots cannot clear it and the gate names them rather than hiding them, because this world's stone and plaster ARE greys and a sunlit roof IS orange, and AoE2 has both collisions too.

**The cue is drawn TWICE, and the reason is a measured pixel.** The translucent lane's opacity is fixed at 0.5 and is shared with fog ghosts, so coverage is the only dial this cue owns. At one pass a pixel is half cue and half whatever is behind it, and where the background is the tint's complement the two cancel — this game pairs exactly that, a blue player against orange roofs. Measured on the showcase fixture at one pass, the friendly silhouette's HEAD over a sunlit roof rendered (171,126,135), a grey-mauve, while the same silhouette's TORSO over the dark chimney stayed blue: one shape carrying two colours, neither reliably the player's. Two coincident passes leave 75% of the tint on the pixel and put that same head at (131,141,195), which reads blue, while 25% of the roof's own light and shade still comes through — plenty to say "behind".

**Moving to the translucent lane costs no instance batch.** The `memory` lane already exists for fog ghosts, which is exactly what an occluded unit is: something you cannot presently see. The snapshot declares nine batches and both the pinned ceiling and a browser spec forbid a tenth.

**How this class is checked from now on.** `tests/rendering/occlusionSilhouetteReadsAsBehind.test.ts` (`npm test`, so `npm run verify`), holding two RELATIONSHIPS rather than any hex or lane name. (1) Coverage: the lane is translucent and the film's effective coverage is strictly between 0.5 and 1 — above 0.5 so the cue's colour beats its background, below 1 so the building shows through — both bounds read out of the snapshot the engine actually consumes (the batch's material opacity and how many coincident passes it holds), never from a constant the test also imports. (2) Identity: each player slot's cue carries that slot's AoE2 hue, and the composite of cue over background still reads as the cue rather than as the background. `aoeVoxelOcclusionSilhouettes.test.ts` and `aoeVoxelAdapter.occlusion.test.ts` were extended alongside.

**Bounds, named.** This is a SNAPSHOT gate: it proves what the renderer emits for one villager behind one Town Centre and says nothing about how the frame looks. The pixels were proved once, by capture — `scripts/captureMapScreenshot.mjs` at 800x600 and 1280x800, at zoom 0.7 / 1.2 (default) / 2.4, on `aoe2-prototype` at ticks 1 and 3000 and on `occlusion-showcase-fixture`, each diffed against the same framing at `4d398996` at 0.02%–0.49% of frame, confined to the silhouettes — and a later change that keeps both relationships and still looks wrong will pass. The single background the gate composites against is ONE MEASURED PIXEL, the sunlit Town Centre roof at (251,96,15), chosen because the worst case for a translucent cue is its complement; it is not the whole palette and not every lighting angle.

**Open follow-up.** Six of the eight player slots are proved by ARITHMETIC ONLY. A skirmish opponent is in fog at boot and no capture reaches one, so only slots 1 and 2 have ever been photographed; the other six rest on the contrast floor and the hue-angle check. Reaching them needs a fixture that seats a visible unit of each slot behind a building, or a match played far enough to scout one.

## 2026-09-06 — The setup screen clipped its own heading and its Start button off any window shorter than its panel, and nothing scrolled (found by the standing loop, FIXED and gated v0.3.215)

**Symptom.** A bare visit — a URL with no query parameters at all — lands on the match setup screen (§4.6), and that screen is the ONLY route from a bare URL into a match. Below roughly 600 CSS pixels of viewport height the panel was taller than the window and neither end of it could be reached: at 1280x400 the "Skirmish" heading sat at y = -70 and the Start button ran 71px past the fold, where a real click times out. So on a 1366x768 laptop with browser chrome, or in any window snapped to half a screen, the game could not be started at all — not degraded, not awkward, unreachable.

**Attribution.** Found by the standing loop, playing the game. The owner reported none of it.

**Why nothing scrolled, which is three separate reasons and not one.** `body { overflow: hidden }` is there for the world canvas. The overlay is `position: fixed`, so it is outside the document scroll and the body rule is not even the binding constraint — the document could not have scrolled to the panel with that rule deleted. And the overlay had no scroll container of its own. Any one of the three would have been enough.

**Why it survived twenty versions.** `800x600` is the browser suite's own default viewport, and the old panel was 599px tall — it fitted that viewport by ONE pixel. Every spec that has ever visited the setup screen ran at the single height where this defect is invisible.

**Root cause, and the reason the obvious fix would not have worked.** The overlay was `display: flex; align-items: center`. A flex item centred on its cross axis and TALLER than its line overflows both ends of that line, while scrollable overflow only ever extends past the END edge — the start edge is unreachable at every scroll offset. So adding `overflow-y: auto` alone would have made Start reachable and left the heading permanently off the top, which is the fix that looks right and ships half the defect. Automatic margins behave differently on purpose: they absorb free space before alignment runs and resolve to zero when there is none, so a panel centred by `margin: auto` is centred while it fits and top-aligned — fully scrollable, both ends reachable — the moment it does not. Both halves were proved separately before either was written.

**Fix.** `src/styles.css`: the overlay becomes its own scroll container (`overflow-y: auto`, `overscroll-behavior: contain`, `align-items: flex-start`) and the panel is centred by `margin: auto` instead. `body { overflow: hidden }` is untouched. The panel's width moves from `min(420px, 92vw)` to `min(420px, 100%)` against a 16px side gutter on the overlay, because `92vw` measured against the whole viewport rather than the overlay's content box and overflowed it sideways below about 400px wide.

**A first attempt regressed the one viewport that mattered, and the pixel diff is what caught it.** That attempt added 24px of vertical padding to the overlay for breathing room. Vertical padding is dead space at exactly the height that decides this: it pushed 800x600 from "fits" to "scrolls", and the before/after diff came back at **21.62% of the frame on a viewport the change was never meant to touch**. The shipped version has a side gutter only — when the panel fits, the free space `margin: auto` splits IS the breathing room — and diffs at **0.00%** there.

**How this class is checked from now on.** `tests/browser/setup-screen-fits-any-viewport.spec.ts` (`npm run test:browser`, so `npm run verify`). Four viewports — 1280x400, 1024x520, 800x600 and 1280x900 — against the heading plus all TEN `[data-setup]` controls (nine selects and Start, counted in `src/app/bootstrap/setupScreen.ts`; the spec's own header says eleven, which is wrong by one; its `SETUP_CONTROLS` array holds the correct ten, so the test is right and only its description of itself is wrong), in four steps per control: the heading is fully visible AT REST before anything scrolls; every control can be brought fully inside the viewport by WHEELING, with the helper failing the moment a wheel moves nothing, which is exactly the wall; the topmost element at each control's own centre point IS that control, so a box that is on screen behind an invisible overlay goes red; and Start is pressed with the real mouse at those coordinates and the match it configures actually boots.

**The gate deliberately never uses Playwright's `locator.click()`.** That call runs `scrollIntoViewIfNeeded` first, which reaches an element the page itself cannot scroll to — measured on the broken tree at 1280x500, where it found and pressed the button. A spec written the ordinary way would have been green over this defect, which is the same shape as the command-deck overflow of 2026-09-02.

**Bound of that gate, named.** Four heights and one width family; Chromium only, headless, default page zoom and default font size. NOT covered: text zoom and browser minimum-font-size settings, keyboard-only reach (Tab order is not asserted), touch scrolling, non-Chromium engines, and every screen other than setup. A future row added to the panel makes it taller and the gate still passes, because the gate asserts reachability rather than a height budget — which is the intended contract, but it means "the panel fits" is never what a green run here means.

## 2026-09-06 — The browser suite's first run off its home machine failed two specs, and both were measuring the host instead of the game (found by the remote gate, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** CI run **34041883943**, the first execution of the new `browser` job and the first run of this suite on any machine but the one developer Windows box, failed **2 of 197** on `ubuntu-latest`. One entry, not two, because they are one finding: the first run of a suite off the machine that had always run it.

**The first: an assertion about how fast the host is.** `tests/browser/play-opening.spec.ts` read the tick, waited for the HUD clock to leave `00:00`, and read the tick again. The clock had ALREADY left `00:00` by the time that wait started, so it resolved on its first poll — 14 ms — and waited for nothing. What was left was an assertion that at least one tick fits in the 25 ms between two round trips. On `ubuntu-latest` (two cores, software rendering) exactly zero ticks fitted, and it read 20 → 20. It now polls for the world to actually advance past the tick it read, with a 30-second budget and a message naming what it was waiting for; nothing else moves the tick, so a world that stops advancing exhausts the poll and the step goes red.

**Its falsifiability proof is the part to keep.** The first attempt to prove the new form red PASSED when it should have failed. The mutation froze the world at tick 11 rather than at zero, so the poll succeeded for the wrong reason — it was racing the very thing it was trying to disprove. A mutation that races what it is trying to disprove proves nothing, and a green result from one is indistinguishable from a green result from a working gate.

**The second: an assertion with a third of a screen pixel of room.** `tests/browser/game-selection-click.spec.ts` searches `tile-selection-cycle-fixture` for a point where a militia, a house and a sheep all answer one click, then cycles the selection there. Of the **625** world offsets its old scan probed, exactly **four** produced the required ordering, in a ribbon one sample wide — and raster order reaches a region at its TIP, so the point it took had the least room of any point in it. Measured margin: **0.30 screen pixels**, at an edge that moves with the idle animation, because the presented hit polygons are evaluated at the renderer's animation clock. A host that reaches a different tick before the scan freezes the world reads the bodies at a different phase, and the click lands outside.

**Root cause of the second, and it is the FIXTURE.** Co-located bodies are drawn at their cell plus one of four slots, `UNIT_CELL_SLOT_OFFSETS[entityId % 4]`, and a sheep carries a unit transform too, so it takes a slot as well. The slots are half a cell apart while the militia's body is 0.25 cells across and the sheep's 0.231, so two different slots leave a **0.019-cell** gap: the two bodies are either exactly coincident or they never touch, and these two never touched. Ids are handed out one per spawn in list order, so the two share a slot exactly when their positions in the list differ by a multiple of four. The fix is a spawn REORDER that puts the militia first and the sheep fifth, so they coincide as the fixture always intended.

**And the scan now measures what it found.** It aims at the middle of the longest run up the militia's body that cycles, rather than at the first offset raster order happens to reach, then grows an eight-point compass around that middle until one direction stops cycling, and fails BY NAME on the fixture if the margin drops below two screen pixels. Margin went **0.30 px to 6.00 px** at default zoom, held across five animation phases. No existing assertion was changed or weakened; one was added.

**Two things the investigation established that outlive this fix.** (1) **Fixture spawns and live play use DIFFERENT lattices** — half-cell slots for fixture spawns, quarter-cell through the occupancy allocator in play — so a fixture pile is spread twice as far as a real one and its bodies never touch. That is the real root and it needs its own task; the reorder above works around it for one fixture. (2) **There is NO player-facing defect here.** Three units genuinely on one cell were ordered correctly in the running game, are separately clickable, and repeated clicks cycle whichever bodies cover the point — which is this spec's own contract and more than the real game does.

**How this class is checked from now on.** Both specs are their own gates, and both now fail for a reason the reader can act on: the first cannot pass on a dead simulation and cannot pass on a fast host by accident, because it polls for the world to advance rather than betting on a wall-clock window; the second measures the fixture's own margin and names the fixture in its failure message, so the next time the two bodies drift apart it says so instead of failing on a click. Bound: the margin is measured at default zoom in iso pixels before the camera zoom (≥ 1), so the real on-screen margin is at least the number asserted; it is held at five animation phases, not continuously.

**Open risk, recorded because the slower runner may hit it.** Two specs in `tests/browser/game-combat-and-meta-meta.spec.ts` went red purely from machine load on 2026-09-05 and passed when re-run alone. The heavier of them drives **2,000 single-tick steps** inside one `page.evaluate` against the suite's 30-second per-test budget, and the file takes **41.7 s** run on its own. A slower CI runner may trip them. If it does, that is this risk and not these changes — and the answer is not a raised timeout.

## 2026-09-06 — A simulation step that refused to run said nothing, and two sessions then reasoned from a false model of the engine (found by the standing loop, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** `window.__AOE2_TEST__.advanceTicks(3000, 100)` against a game paused from the REAL Esc menu returns after zero ticks, throws nothing, and leaves `matchState.outcome === 'running'` and `engineHalted === null` — the two fields a caller would consult, both saying nothing is wrong. Confirmed live at v0.3.213.

**Investigation.** `bridge.step()` had FOUR paths that decline to advance the world and returned the same nothing for all of them: a manual pause (`setPaused(true)`, which is what the Esc menu applies), `haltState.halted`, a match that already has an outcome, and the replay bridge, which never advances from a frame by design because `ReplayController` owns playback. `advanceTicks` lifts only the pause IT applied (`pausedThroughTestApi`), so a pause set anywhere else survives every step in its loop.

**The expensive part was not the missing ticks.** A play session, reading that silence, published a hazard about the test seam in two forms: that `advanceTicks(n)` throws the engine's re-entrancy `EngineError`, and that it "silently under-advances when the simulation is not paused". Both were repeated in worker assignments as fact. A later session measured them at `d65f84aa` and found BOTH wrong — running and unpaused, 3,000 requested gave 3,000 advanced with no throw; paused through the test API, the same; and `advanceTicks` is a plain loop called from outside any tick, so it cannot reach the engine's re-entrancy guard at all. So two sessions spent effort reasoning from a false model of the engine, and the cause was not a wrong measurement: it was that a function which declined to do its job said nothing about declining, leaving a tick delta of zero as the only evidence and every explanation for it equally available.

**And a caller was already paying for it.** `src/game/playtest/llmRunner.ts` treats `advanced <= 0` as `stopReason = 'engineHalt'` under a comment that asserts the inference is safe — "Reached only when the match is still 'running' ... so no progress here IS a genuine halt". A world paused by anything other than the test API is still `running` and still advances nothing, so that branch reports a PAUSE as an engine crash. It is latent only because the LLM host happens to unpause around each call; nothing in the runner knows that, and nothing would tell it if that stopped being true.

**Root cause.** A function with four distinct refusal conditions and no return value. Every caller had to infer refusal from an unchanged tick counter, and no caller could tell the four apart.

**Fix.** `step()` returns `{ ticks, refusedBecause }` (`src/game/simulation/bridge/stepReport.ts`, typed in `simulationBridgeTypes.ts`), where `refusedBecause` is `'paused' | 'halted' | 'match-over' | 'replay'` or `null`. `ticks` is reported separately from the refusal because zero ticks is legitimate for a `deltaMs` shorter than one tick, so a short step is not a fault. A step that ran some ticks and then hit a failing tick reports BOTH halves. The refusal values are shared FROZEN module constants — one object each for the life of the process, no `Error`, no stack capture, no string building — because a halted, paused or finished match refuses on every frame for the rest of the session, sixty times a second, and that steady state must allocate nothing. `browserTestApi.advanceTicks` now returns the tally (`stepsRequested`, `stepsRun`, `ticksAdvanced`, `refusedBecause`) summed from the steps' own reports rather than read off a tick counter, and warns once per reason for callers that ignore the value.

**Two designs were rejected, and why is the useful part.** THROWING was rejected because the playtest runner polls a finishing match and a resolved match is a normal end there, not an exception. Fixing only the test seam — having `advanceTicks` compare tick counters and report the shortfall — was rejected because it leaves the production silence exactly as it was, and the production silence is the defect: the same `step()` is called by the frame loop and by `runPlaytest`, and it is those callers, not the test harness, that had no way to ask what happened.

**How this class is checked from now on.** `tests/app/stepRefusalIsReported.test.ts` (`npm test`, so `npm run verify`), 13 cases: all four refusal reasons on the REAL objects (`paused`, `halted` and `match-over` on a real `createSimulationBridge`, `replay` on a real `makeReplayBridge`), asserted to be four DISTINCT values — a signal that cannot tell them apart is the same defect in a new costume — plus a control in the other direction (a running unpaused bridge reports no refusal, so the gate cannot pass by refusing everything) and the seam end to end through the real `installBrowserTestApi`. Proven red at `bcdc88c3` with the source reverted: 12 of 13 failed. The 13th passes on both trees ON PURPOSE — it is the backwards-compatibility control for widening `advanceTicks`'s return value — and carries none of the gate's coverage.

**Bounds, named.** Seed `aoe2-prototype`, tick horizons in the tens, and match resolution FORCED with `gameLength` rather than played out, so it proves nothing about how a real match ends. It never runs the live frame loop, which calls `step()` every animation frame and correctly DISCARDS the report; nothing here measures that loop's cost or checks that discarding stays correct. The console warning's dedupe is asserted over two calls, not over a long run.

**Open follow-ups.** (1) `llmRunner.ts` should READ `refusedBecause` instead of inferring a halt from `advanced <= 0`; the signal now exists and the runner still guesses. (2) `src/app/bootstrap/browserTestApi.ts` is now at EXACTLY 500 lines, its cap in `tests/architecture/fileSizeBudget.test.ts`, so the next line added there goes red and the answer is a split by role rather than a trim.

## 2026-09-03 — A decided match never ends, because the AI can only aim at a Town Center (PARTLY FIXED: 1 of 4 seeds resolved then; 3 of 4 after the 2026-09-04 garrison entry above, OPEN)

**Symptom.** The standing acceptance-test failure "matches do not resolve": at 60,000 ticks — 100 minutes of game time — every seed is still running. Measured on four seeds after the wood fix, all four still ran to the horizon.

**Investigation.** `gold-rush` gave the clean case. From tick 48,000 to 60,000, sampled every 4,000 and byte-identical at every sample, owner 2 held:

    pop 0/5   villagers 0   army 0   buildings: blacksmith x1, house x1

Zero units. No Town Center, so it can never produce a unit again. It is functionally dead and cannot recover. Meanwhile owner 1 stood at 130 population with an army of 90, building farms.

**Root cause.** Two rules that are each correct and together deadlock.

`conquestOutcomeSystem` ends a match only when a player has NO UNITS **AND NO BUILDINGS** — which is right; a player with a building standing has not been conquered.

`pickAttackTarget` chooses the AI's march target from `townCentersByOwner`, a map keyed on owners that still HAVE a Town Center. Raze it and that owner drops out of the target list entirely: `targetOwner` becomes null, the attack phase's push branch has nothing to march on, and the surviving buildings are attacked only if they happen to fall inside somebody's vision. The AI is structurally unable to finish what it started.

So the winner cannot satisfy the condition the loser is being kept alive by.

**Fix, and the first version of it was WRONG in a way worth recording.** The obvious shape — give `pickAttackTarget` a fallback map and let it return the surviving building in `targetOwner` / `targetTownCenterId` / `targetTownCenterPosition` — passed its own unit gate, resolved `gold-rush`, and REGRESSED THE AGE-UP: `aiPlayer.test.ts` :: "ages up through the ages within a generous tick budget" went red, the AI reaching Feudal at 2,061 and never Castle within 8,000 ticks. Cause: `targetTownCenterPosition` is not private to the attack phase. `aiFerryPhase` reads it too, and when it is set with the target not land-reachable it stands down the ordinary march AND discretionary building to fund a Transport Ship — so filling that field for a Town-Center-less enemy stalled the economy. Isolated by disabling only the fallback and re-running: 24 of 24 passed.

The shipped fix is therefore SURGICAL. `pickAttackTarget` is byte-identical to what it was. A separate `lastResortTargetId` / `lastResortTargetPosition` pair is computed alongside it and read by the attack phase and nothing else, in its final push branch, only when no enemy Town Center is left. Deterministic — owners and entity ids both sorted, so a replay picks the same building. `gold-rush` still resolves at exactly tick 41,967, and the age-up test is green.

**The lesson, which is about the FIELD and not the feature.** A context field named for one thing (`targetTownCenterPosition`) had a second reader with different semantics, and reusing it to mean "the place to march" was correct for the phase being edited and wrong for the phase that was not. The cost of finding that was one full gate run; the cost of not finding it would have been a shipped economy regression that the resolution measurement would have applauded.

**Effect, measured on four seeds at 60,000 ticks, before and after.** `gold-rush` goes from running-at-60,000 to **victory at tick 41,967**. The other three still run, and the reasons are DIFFERENT defects rather than this one failing:

- `arena` and `coastal`: owner 2 still HAS a Town Center, so the fallback never engages. Owner 1 has an army of 105 and does not finish it — on arena that base sits inside the script's own stone wall ring, which points at attack EXECUTION (pathing to or breaching a walled base) rather than at targeting.
- `fortress`: neither side has an army to attack with (2 and 0 in the Imperial age). That is the villager-target-versus-population-cap chain in the entry above, not this one.

**How it is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/aiAttacksAnyEnemy.test.ts` :: `an enemy with no Town Center is still a target` — four cases: the fallback fires when the enemy has lost its Town Center; a Town Center still outranks a much closer bare building; the two-argument form is unchanged; and an ALLY without a Town Center is never targeted. **Bound of that gate, named.** It is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE. It does not prove the army reaches the building, kills it, or that the match ends — arena and coastal are live proof that a correct target is not sufficient. The end-to-end claim rests on the `gold-rush` seed alone, at one horizon.

**What this predicts.** The remaining two causes are both "the army exists and does not close": walls it will not breach, and an army too small to matter. Both are about what the military DOES rather than what it is pointed at, and neither is touched here.

**What it actually was, found 2026-09-04.** The prediction above was half right and half wrong, and the wrong half is the useful one. **And the bound named above is exactly where it hid.** This gate is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE, and it says so. The defect was one line earlier, in a preference that never got as far as choosing.

t=16504-16506  pos=49,21  next=50,21  proceed

   ... unchanged through t=16539

A two-cell limit cycle. Destination selection runs from the carrier's current position with no memory, so two adjacent cells can disagree about where it should be going, and the carrier chases the disagreement.

## 2026-08-30 — Gather over-subscription counts EVERY owner's villagers (LATENT, not fixed, OPEN)

**What it is.** `villagerEconomySystem` builds `gatherTargetCounts` by walking `activeWorld.query('gatherer')` with no owner filter, so every gatherer in the match counts toward every resource's occupancy. That map is what `MAX_GATHERERS_PER_RESOURCE` (2) is checked against, and its stated purpose is to stop ONE player piling villagers on one node. Counting the opponent's villagers is wrong for that purpose: an enemy working a neutral resource can make it read over-subscribed for you, and your villagers fan out away from a resource that has room.

**Why it is not fixed.** Measured before touching it, which is the point of this entry: across `aoe2-prototype`, `default-seed` and `corpus-seed-b`, sampling every 100 ticks for 12,000 ticks each, **not one tick had a resource targeted by two different owners** — 0 of 360 samples, max owners on any single resource 1. Every current map keeps the players' resource rings apart, so the defect cannot fire. Fixing it means threading a per-owner count through five call sites in two files for no measurable behaviour change today.

**The trigger to watch for.** It becomes live the moment two players' gatherers can reach the same node: a map with a contested middle (gold or stone between bases), overlapping resource rings on a small map, or allied players sharing a base. Any of those should come with the fix — key the counts by owner — and this entry is the note that the machinery is already wrong when they arrive.

**What this predicts.** A shared counter with no owner dimension is a latent cross-player coupling wherever it appears. `gatherTargetCounts` is one; the same shape is worth looking for in any per-tick map keyed only by entity id.

## 2026-08-30 — The behind-building silhouette reads as a unit standing ON the building (OPEN)

**Symptom.** A unit hidden behind a building is cued by a flat-coloured mirror of its body drawn over the building. It is fully opaque, and at a Town Center that reads as a villager standing on the roof rather than as an x-ray of one behind the wall — noticed hands-on on 2026-08-29 ("three of them across the TC's upper tier read as villagers standing on the roof") and confirmed in the `occlusion-showcase-fixture` capture, where the blue figure sits on the roof with nothing to say it is a cue.

**Investigation.** The obvious fix — draw the mirror on a transparent material so the building reads through it — was implemented, measured, and REVERTED. It failed on three independent axes, each measured rather than argued:

1. *It is close to a no-op under the art style the game actually ships.* The default is `moebius`, whose tone-band quantiser snaps **24.1% of the cue's pixels byte-identical to the fully opaque render**; mean change against opaque is 46.6 there against 125.6 under `painted`. One opacity value cannot serve a banding and a non-banding pipeline.
2. *It introduces self-blending seams.* The mirror copies a unit's own overlapping parts, and a translucent instanced part that overlaps itself blends twice — solved per-pixel as 452 px at the declared 0.55, 68 at 0.7975 (1−0.45²) and 6 at 0.9089 (1−0.45³). About 14% of the body draws at the wrong opacity, as streaks down the arm and torso. The engine has the cure and does not export it (see `docs/engine-feedback/current.md`).
3. *It degrades the half of the cue that matters most.* The enemy colour is a coral in the same hue family as every roof in the palette, so blending erases it: mean RGB distance from the background over a bright roof fell from 204.2 opaque to **82.0**, while the friendly blue — orange's complement — held at 196.9. The cue exists to answer "are those mine?", and it was the *not mine* answer that went.

Raising opacity to 0.78 fixed the measured colour bar but none of the three causes.

**Root cause of the DEFECT (still open).** Uniform alpha is the wrong technique for this cue in this renderer. What would work is a treatment that does not depend on blending: a contour, a hatch, or a colour chosen for contrast against the building palette rather than for ownership alone. The enemy coral in particular should be re-picked against the roof palette, which is a change that needs no engine feature and no transparency.

**How it is checked from now on.** It is not, and that is the honest state — no gate covers "does this cue read as behind rather than on top", because it is a judgement about a rendered image. What the attempt DID leave behind is the measurement method: capture background / opaque / candidate under both art styles, solve the per-pixel alpha, and measure mean RGB distance from the local background for BOTH ownership colours over both a bright and a dark building. Any future attempt that does not do that is guessing, and the first attempt's 0.55 was a guess that looked right in one capture of one art style with one owner.

**What this predicts.** Every other overlay that might reach for transparency has the same two traps waiting — the moebius quantiser and self-blending — and every two-colour ownership cue should be checked in BOTH colours against the palette it will sit on, not just the one the showcase fixture happens to spawn.
