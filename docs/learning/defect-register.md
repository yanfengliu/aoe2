# Defect register

The standing list of what the gates could not see. One entry per defect that reached a build: the symptom as it presented, what the investigation found, the root cause, and the check that covers the defect's whole CLASS from now on.

Unlike a lesson, an entry stays after it becomes a gate. The register is not a to-do list — it is the record of where defects came from, which is the best available guide to where the next one is.

**What lives here, and what rolled over.** This file holds the 12 most recent CLOSED entries plus every OPEN one, whatever its age, newest first. Everything older and closed is in [`defect-register-past.md`](defect-register-past.md), moved verbatim. An OPEN entry never rolls over — it leaves this file by being closed, not by ageing, and a heading that says the defect is still live is not a closure however it spells it: "(LATENT, not fixed)", "(PARTLY FIXED: ...)" and "(... still unfixed)" all keep the entry here, and each of those three had been archived once before the gate learned to refuse them. The rollover is done by the task whose new entry pushes this file past 12 closed entries, in that task's own commit, the way the devlog archives its detailed file; never by a later sweep. `tests/architecture/defectRegisterRollover.test.ts` holds all of that. An "entry above" or "entry below" written before the split may name an entry that now lives in the archive, and a rollover can strand a pointer in either direction; the archive's header lists the references that cross the split.

## 2026-10-14 — The frame rate fell through a match, from 59.7 fps at the opening to about 21 at 19:30, the same over the base and an empty corner, because every frame rebuilt and re-sent everything the player knew of (coordinator's play session; PARTLY FIXED by work 130 M1, the still world kept between ticks; the shadow lane and the units still grow with the match, OPEN)

**Symptom.** Found by the coordinator's real-input play session on v0.3.257 (the lobby's defaults, an RTX 4090 through ANGLE D3D11 at 1600x900, dev build): 59.7 fps at 00:15 of game time, 48 at 02:50, 39 in a fight, about 21 at 19:30 with typical frames of 50 ms, over the player's base and over an empty corner at (55,3) alike. The simulation held 15 ticks a second. The page had loaded a save and opened a replay, so match state and page age were not separated.

**Investigation.** Work 130 separated them (docs/debugging/2026-10-15-frame-rate-through-a-match.md). A fresh page that loads a save of a late match is slow at once (31-34 fps against 57 for the opening in the same page); a page kept 20.6 minutes at Normal speed and three replay round trips leave the frame cost and every leak count flat. The runtime's own counters showed one new snapshot revision per drawn frame and matrix writes per frame equal to every instance on screen: 1,513 at the opening, about 3,000 at tick 11,700 of an AI-played match, at about 4.8 µs each. A CPU profile of a late frame put 51% of the main thread in building the snapshot and 19% in the runtime ingesting and rewriting it; the simulation took 3.4%. Separately, the HUD made 30 DOM mutations a frame with the game paused, so each frame paid a style recalculation and a layout.

**Root cause.** `AoeVoxelPresentationCoordinator.syncFromBridge` presents whenever the interpolation alpha moved, which is every frame of a running match, and each present ran every entity's recipe and cast shadow, then sorted and packed every part into batches that all took the snapshot's new revision, so the runtime re-validated, re-paged and rewrote the whole known world on each frame. What the player has seen and built grows through a match (trees kept as fog memory, buildings, villagers, a wider line of sight), and none of it depended on the camera.

**Fixed (work 130 M1).** A still entity keeps its parts while its projected view is equal field by field (`aoeVoxelEntityPrep.ts`), the ground's detail is kept while the visible ground is unchanged (`aoeVoxelTerrainFrame.ts`), and the opaque surfaces come in terrain, still and moving lanes that keep their revision while unchanged (`aoeVoxelLaneBatches.ts`), so the runtime keeps them. The HUD writes a value only when it differs (`ui/hud/hudDomWrites.ts`). Measured interleaved against the base build (4 rounds, 26-32 of 32 CPUs busy with other work): the opening frame's callbacks 14.6 to 7.2 ms, a tick-11,700 frame's 27.3 to 12.5 ms (32.6 to 58.7 fps); matrix writes per frame 1,513 to 876 at the opening and about 3,010 to 2,070 late.

**Recovery (work 130 B, 2026-10-17).** Public runtime deltas omit unchanged batches and patch stable instance slots. The shadow and memory lanes still preserve their one-batch blending order, but no longer need full buffer rewrites merely because a unit moves. A guard sends a full batch if its exact translucent bounds change, keeping Three's sorting equivalent. The actual presenter tests reproduce that bounds defect and then pass. The bounded saved late match falls from 2,018 to 302 matrix writes per frame; eight final B screenshot pairs have zero changed pixels. GPU late play holds approximately 60 fps at 1600×900 on the measured host.

**Open.** The stronger software late/opening frame-cost target remains unresolved. The old cost800 Normal windows overlapped a load toast; the later settled compositor counterfactual used Slow. Those are distinct historical observations, not a valid replacement comparison. Work 133 fixes speed, settling, complete input bindings and actual delivered runtime-frame/tick accounting before scoring one opaque ground-order candidate. No weaker counter threshold closes the original whole-frame report.

**Checked from now on.** presentationFrameWork.test.ts, aoeVoxelLaneEquivalence.test.ts and aoeVoxelStillCache.test.ts hold M1 preparation and instance equivalence. aoeVoxelSnapshotDelta.test.ts and AoeVoxelWorldRendererDelta.test.ts drive actual reducer/runtime/presenter updates, translucent bounds and rollback, including the exact CI pin. The real browser presentation-keeps-still-world.spec.ts now requires matrix writes below 25% of live instances; restoring the full-snapshot route fails at 57.898%. hud-frame-dom-writes.spec.ts holds a paused frame to no DOM writes and no layout. Per-test bounds and mutation evidence remain in docs/learning/gate-proofs.md and work 130's handoff. None of these count gates measures settled software wall-frame rate.

## 2026-10-14 — Hunters stood idle beside the boar they had killed, and foragers whose berries ran out walked 10 to 20 cells to fish distant ponds with the idle count at 0 (found in the coordinator's play session of 2026-10-14, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** Found by the coordinator's play session of 2026-10-14 on v0.3.257, played with the real mouse and keyboard (work 106's card queue), not reported by the owner. Five villagers with Loom killed the boar at about tick 2,995. Four went idle at once, the fifth attacked the second boar and then went idle too, and the carcass kept its 340 food until the player ordered them again. At about 16:30 the berries ran out, and seven food villagers walked 10 to 20 cells to fish ponds at (12,19), (21,24) and (11,30). The idle-villager count stayed 0, so nothing told the player, and food income collapsed. Work 131 reproduced both through the player's right-click before any fix. On `boar-hunt-fixture` all six hunters were idle the tick after the kill, and the carcass still held 340 food 300 ticks later. On `aoe2-prototype` with the AI's attacks off, three foragers ordered onto the berries walked to the shore fish at (12,19) and (25,11) when the last bush went at tick 15,182, with the idle count at 0. A headless Playwright run of the v0.3.257 build, with a real drag, double-click and right-click, showed the same: idle bell 6 beside a 340-food carcass, and three foragers walking to (11,19), (9,20) and (14,17) with the idle bell at 0.

**Investigation.** Two separate paths. A right-click on a live animal is an attack order, and an attack order clears the villager's gather order; when the animal died, the attack step cleared the command and nothing gave the villager the carcass. A human player's gather order set `hasExplicitGatherOrder`, and that flag made the economy system keep the villager working exactly as it keeps the computer's. Whenever the villager stood idle, after every delivery and not only when its node ran out, `assignIdleGatherer` gave it the best node of its stockpile kind by the walk home: inside the home range, then anywhere on the map, then any other kind, in the order wood, food, gold, stone. Shore fish are food, so the foragers fished. Definitive Edition does neither (spec §6.4, "Carrying on when a resource runs out", names the patch notes and forum reports). Its villager carries on to more of the same kind close by and otherwise stands idle, and its hunters gather what they killed and never take on a live boar by themselves.

**Root cause.** The game had one re-tasking policy, the computer's, and applied it to the human player's villagers through the explicit-order flag. So a player's order meant "keep this villager busy with this stockpile kind" rather than "work this node". Nothing turned a finished hunt into a gather.

**Fix (vNEXT).** `gatherCarryOn.ts`: at the swing that empties a node, every gatherer of it carries on to a node of the same type within 5 cells, keeping what it holds; that covers one working it, one walking to it and one carrying a load home from it. The node must be reachable, clear of enemy defences and not another player's animal. With none, the order ends: the load goes home and the villager stands idle. Between trips the human player's villager goes back to its own node, and its unreachable, crowded and given-up recoveries stay inside the same rule. A villager whose attacked animal dies gathers its carcass, with the gather order a right-click gives; the computer's hunter carries no standing order afterwards, so the computer re-tasks it as before. A rally point on a resource gathers that node. The computer's villagers follow the same unit rule, and the computer still re-tasks any villager it leaves idle.

**Checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/gatherCarryOn.test.ts`, 9 cases through the player's right-click. For a berry bush, a tree, gold, stone, a sheep and a shore fish: the villager goes back to its node after its first delivery, on to the same kind 3.6 cells away with its part load, then stands idle with the idle count at 1. It never takes the same kind 5.4 cells away beside the Town Center, nor another kind in reach (a sheep and a shore fish beside the berries). An expired Farm leaves its farmer idle. The radius is held from both sides: a bush exactly 5 cells away is close by, one 5.4 away is not. The computer keeps its own villager working as each node runs out. `tests/simulation/huntCarryOn.test.ts`, 3 cases: every hunter gathers the boar it killed and food reaches the stockpile; once the carcass is eaten, the hunters never attack the live boar beside it and stand idle; and they carry on to another carcass close by. Each mutation below was restored after its run, and each turned the named cases red: no gather of the kill (3), the stockpile kind in place of the type (3), no radius (6), a radius of 4 (the radius case) and of 6 (the radius case and the sheep case), the node forgotten at delivery (7), a part load walked home first (6), the old re-tasking for the player's villagers (8), a Farm that hops to another (1), a hunter that takes the live boar (1), and a computer that never re-tasks (1). A rally point that takes only the kind again turns the rally case of `createSimulationBridge.darkAge.test.ts` red. Three gates were revised under the rule, each with its own red. `villagerGatherSpread.test.ts`: villagers piled onto one tree all work it and carry on along the woodline, every one filling a load; a constructed jam turns it red. `gateBuiltInMatch.test.ts`: the sealed-off gatherer ends its order, and the player sends it again once the gate stands; without the gate's cache bump it is red. `aiEconomyKeepsMoving.test.ts`: it reads owner 2's banked total, because owner 2's Feudal age-up now lands inside the window and spends the stockpile (banked 1,650 against 1,645 on the base); a frozen economy banks 0 and turns it red. ## 2026-09-03 — A decided match never ends, because the AI can only aim at a Town Center (PARTLY FIXED: 1 of 4 seeds resolved then; 3 of 4 after the 2026-09-04 garrison entry above, OPEN)

**Symptom.** The standing acceptance-test failure "matches do not resolve": at 60,000 ticks — 100 minutes of game time — every seed is still running. Measured on four seeds after the wood fix, all four still ran to the horizon.

**Investigation.** `gold-rush` gave the clean case. From tick 48,000 to 60,000, sampled every 4,000 and byte-identical at every sample, owner 2 held:

    pop 0/5   villagers 0   army 0   buildings: blacksmith x1, house x1

Zero units. No Town Center, so it can never produce a unit again. It is functionally dead and cannot recover. Meanwhile owner 1 stood at 130 population with an army of 90, building farms.

**Root cause.** Two rules that are each correct and together deadlock.

`conquestOutcomeSystem` ends a match only when a player has NO UNITS **AND NO BUILDINGS** — which is right; a player with a building standing has not been conquered.

`pickAttackTarget` chooses the AI's march target from `townCentersByOwner`, a map keyed on owners that still HAVE a Town Center. Raze it and that owner drops out of the target list entirely: `targetOwner` becomes null, the attack phase's push branch has nothing to march on, and the surviving buildings are attacked only if they happen to fall inside somebody's vision. The AI is structurally unable to finish what it started.

So the winner cannot satisfy the condition the loser is being kept alive by.

**Fix, and the first version of it was WRONG in a way worth recording.** The obvious shape — give `pickAttackTarget` a fallback map and let it return the surviving building in `targetOwner` / `targetTownCenterId` / `targetTownCenterPosition` — passed its own unit gate, resolved `gold-rush`, and REGRESSED THE AGE-UP: `aiPlayer.test.ts` :: "ages up through the ages within a generous tick budget" went red, the AI reaching Feudal at 2,061 and never Castle within 8,000 ticks. Cause: `targetTownCenterPosition` is not private to the attack phase. `aiFerryPhase` reads it too, and when it is set with the target not land-reachable it stands down the ordinary march AND discretionary building to fund a Transport Ship — so filling that field for a Town-Center-less enemy stalled the economy. Isolated by disabling only the fallback and re-running: 24 of 24 passed.

The shipped fix is therefore SURGICAL. `pickAttackTarget` is byte-identical to what it was. A separate `lastResortTargetId` / `lastResortTargetPosition` pair is computed alongside it and read by the attack phase and nothing else, in its final push branch, only when no enemy Town Center is left. Deterministic — owners and entity ids both sorted, so a replay picks the same building. `gold-rush` still resolves at exactly tick 41,967, and the age-up test is green.

**The lesson, which is about the FIELD and not the feature.** A context field named for one thing (`targetTownCenterPosition`) had a second reader with different semantics, and reusing it to mean "the place to march" was correct for the phase being edited and wrong for the phase that was not. The cost of finding that was one full gate run; the cost of not finding it would have been a shipped economy regression that the resolution measurement would have applauded.

**Effect, measured on four seeds at 60,000 ticks, before and after.** `gold-rush` goes from running-at-60,000 to **victory at tick 41,967**. The other three still run, and the reasons are DIFFERENT defects rather than this one failing:

- `arena` and `coastal`: owner 2 still HAS a Town Center, so the fallback never engages. Owner 1 has an army of 105 and does not finish it — on arena that base sits inside the script's own stone wall ring, which points at attack EXECUTION (pathing to or breaching a walled base) rather than at targeting.
- `fortress`: neither side has an army to attack with (2 and 0 in the Imperial age). That is the villager-target-versus-population-cap chain in the entry above, not this one.

**How it is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/aiAttacksAnyEnemy.test.ts` :: `an enemy with no Town Center is still a target` — four cases: the fallback fires when the enemy has lost its Town Center; a Town Center still outranks a much closer bare building; the two-argument form is unchanged; and an ALLY without a Town Center is never targeted. Red-checked by reintroducing the defect (iterating an empty list instead of the fallback map): the first case fails with `expected null to be 2`, and all four pass again on revert.

**Bound of that gate, named.** It is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE. It does not prove the army reaches the building, kills it, or that the match ends — arena and coastal are live proof that a correct target is not sufficient. The end-to-end claim rests on the `gold-rush` seed alone, at one horizon.

**What this predicts.** The remaining two causes are both "the army exists and does not close": walls it will not breach, and an army too small to matter. Both are about what the military DOES rather than what it is pointed at, and neither is touched here.

**What it actually was, found 2026-09-04.** The prediction above was half right and half wrong, and the wrong half is the useful one. It reads `arena` and `coastal` as "the army exists and does not close" — walls, or too few units. The army was not closing because it was never ORDERED to: the attack phase's first preference is the target enemy's villager, and a GARRISONED villager is alive with no position, so the order silently no-opped and the branch `continue`d before the fallbacks this entry added. Those fallbacks were correct and were never reached. See the 2026-09-04 entry above; with that fixed, three of four seeds resolve.

**And the bound named above is exactly where it hid.** This gate is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE, and it says so. The defect was one line earlier, in a preference that never got as far as choosing.

## 2026-08-30 — Gather over-subscription counts EVERY owner's villagers (LATENT, not fixed, OPEN)

**What it is.** `villagerEconomySystem` builds `gatherTargetCounts` by walking `activeWorld.query('gatherer')` with no owner filter, so every gatherer in the match counts toward every resource's occupancy. That map is what `MAX_GATHERERS_PER_RESOURCE` (2) is checked against, and its stated purpose is to stop ONE player piling villagers on one node. Counting the opponent's villagers is wrong for that purpose: an enemy working a neutral resource can make it read over-subscribed for you, and your villagers fan out away from a resource that has room.

**Why it is not fixed.** Measured before touching it, which is the point of this entry: across `aoe2-prototype`, `default-seed` and `corpus-seed-b`, sampling every 100 ticks for 12,000 ticks each, **not one tick had a resource targeted by two different owners** — 0 of 360 samples, max owners on any single resource 1. Every current map keeps the players' resource rings apart, so the defect cannot fire. Fixing it means threading a per-owner count through five call sites in two files for no measurable behaviour change today.

**The trigger to watch for.** It becomes live the moment two players' gatherers can reach the same node: a map with a contested middle (gold or stone between bases), overlapping resource rings on a small map, or allied players sharing a base. Any of those should come with the fix — key the counts by owner — and this entry is the note that the machinery is already wrong when they arrive.

**What this predicts.** A shared counter with no owner dimension is a latent cross-player coupling wherever it appears. `gatherTargetCounts` is one; the same shape is worth looking for in any per-tick map keyed only by entity id.

## 2026-08-30 — The behind-building silhouette reads as a unit standing ON the building (OPEN)

**Symptom.** A unit hidden behind a building is cued by a flat-coloured mirror of its body drawn over the building. It is fully opaque, and at a Town Center that reads as a villager standing on the roof rather than as an x-ray of one behind the wall — noticed hands-on on 2026-08-29 ("three of them across the TC's upper tier read as villagers standing on the roof") and confirmed in the `occlusion-showcase-fixture` capture, where the blue figure sits on the roof with nothing to say it is a cue.

**Investigation.** The obvious fix — draw the mirror on a transparent material so the building reads through it — was implemented, measured, and REVERTED. It failed on three independent axes, each measured rather than argued:

1. *It is close to a no-op under the art style the game actually ships.* The default is `moebius`, whose tone-band quantiser snaps **24.1% of the cue's pixels byte-identical to the fully opaque render**; mean change against opaque is 46.6 there against 125.6 under `painted`. One opacity value cannot serve a banding and a non-banding pipeline.
2. *It introduces self-blending seams.* The mirror copies a unit's own overlapping parts, and a translucent instanced part that overlaps itself blends twice — solved per-pixel as 452 px at the declared 0.55, 68 at 0.7975 (1−0.45²) and 6 at 0.9089 (1−0.45³). About 14% of the body draws at the wrong opacity, as streaks down the arm and torso. The engine has the cure and does not export it (see `docs/engine-feedback/current.md`).
3. *It degrades the half of the cue that matters most.* The enemy colour is a coral in the same hue family as every roof in the palette, so blending erases it: mean RGB distance from the background over a bright roof fell from 204.2 opaque to **82.0**, while the friendly blue — orange's complement — held at 196.9. The cue exists to answer "are those mine?", and it was the *not mine* answer that went.

Raising opacity to 0.78 fixed the measured colour bar but none of the three causes.

**Root cause of the DEFECT (still open).** Uniform alpha is the wrong technique for this cue in this renderer. What would work is a treatment that does not depend on blending: a contour, a hatch, or a colour chosen for contrast against the building palette rather than for ownership alone. The enemy coral in particular should be re-picked against the roof palette, which is a change that needs no engine feature and no transparency.

**How it is checked from now on.** It is not, and that is the honest state — no gate covers "does this cue read as behind rather than on top", because it is a judgement about a rendered image. What the attempt DID leave behind is the measurement method: capture background / opaque / candidate under both art styles, solve the per-pixel alpha, and measure mean RGB distance from the local background for BOTH ownership colours over both a bright and a dark building. Any future attempt that does not do that is guessing, and the first attempt's 0.55 was a guess that looked right in one capture of one art style with one owner.

**What this predicts.** Every other overlay that might reach for transparency has the same two traps waiting — the moebius quantiser and self-blending — and every two-colour ownership cue should be checked in BOTH colours against the palette it will sit on, not just the one the showcase fixture happens to spawn.
