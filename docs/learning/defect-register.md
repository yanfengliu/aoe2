# Defect register

The standing list of what the gates could not see. One entry per defect that reached a build: the symptom as it presented, what the investigation found, the root cause, and the check that covers the defect's whole CLASS from now on.

Unlike a lesson, an entry stays after it becomes a gate. The register is not a to-do list — it is the record of where defects came from, which is the best available guide to where the next one is.

**What lives here, and what rolled over.** This file holds the 12 most recent CLOSED entries plus every OPEN one, whatever its age, newest first. Everything older and closed is in [`defect-register-past.md`](defect-register-past.md), moved verbatim. An OPEN entry never rolls over — it leaves this file by being closed, not by ageing, and a heading that says the defect is still live is not a closure however it spells it: "(LATENT, not fixed)", "(PARTLY FIXED: ...)" and "(... still unfixed)" all keep the entry here, and each of those three had been archived once before the gate learned to refuse them. The rollover is done by the task whose new entry pushes this file past 12 closed entries, in that task's own commit, the way the devlog archives its detailed file; never by a later sweep. `tests/architecture/defectRegisterRollover.test.ts` holds all of that. An "entry above" or "entry below" written before the split may name an entry that now lives in the archive, and a rollover can strand a pointer in either direction; the archive's header lists the references that cross the split.

## 2026-10-12 — Sheep boxed in on the way to a far target ran one failing path search for every cell of the map, every tick (agent-observed by work 123, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** Agent-observed, not reported by a player. `tests/browser/de-ground.spec.ts:139` ran 84-90 s of its 90 s limit on every CI run of 2026-10-11 (median 83.8 s, 89.8 s at worst), 72 s on this machine's GPU, and timed out work 116's local verify under load. The spec selects every human unit on the boot map, the four sheep included, orders them to (36, 14) and calls `advanceTicks(300)`; its sibling that skips the walk takes 7.7 s. Replayed in Node (`createSimulationBridge('aoe2-prototype')`, the same order, 300 `bridge.step(100)`), the 300 ticks took 61.6 s: 16 ms at the median, 300-600 ms a tick from tick 91 to 175 and from 292 on, 864 ms at worst. A player who sends boxed-in sheep across the map meets the same stall, since a tick has 100 ms.

**Investigation.** A CPU profile of that run put 94% of it under `herdableMovementSystem` → `findMovementPlan` → `findMovementPathToCandidates` → civ-engine's `findGridPath`, most of it reading cell passability. The herd system replans every ordered sheep every tick (it moves one on every sixth), and its candidates are `getNearestMoveCandidates(target)`: every cell of the map, nearest the target first. The planner ran one A* per passable candidate until one reached. Counted with a temporary counter: 4 searches a tick while the sheep walk, then 1,756-2,731 a tick, nearly all failing, once two sheep are boxed in beside their Town Centre. Only bounds, terrain, buildings and resources close a cell to an animal (`blocksWholeCell`), so what boxes them in is the building and the other sheep; walking units only crowd a cell. Each failed search explores the pocket the sheep stands in, and the loop repeats it for each of about 1,400 open candidate cells until it reaches the pocket's own.

**Root cause.** "First candidate a path reaches" was computed by trying candidates one at a time, and callers pass candidate lists the size of the map. A start that reaches few candidates pays one full failed search per candidate, every time it plans.

**Fixed (vNEXT).** After the first failed search the planner floods the start's 4-connected passable region once and skips every candidate outside it. civ-engine's grid A* here steps orthogonally and refuses a blocked start, so it can never reach a goal outside that region; every candidate inside it still gets its own search, under the same 10,000-iteration cap as before (it can bind only on a map past about 8,000 cells, the boundary `builderReachability.ts` records). So the candidate chosen and its path are unchanged. A candidate the flood cannot index (off the grid, or not a whole cell) still goes to `findGridPath`, which judges it as before. In the castle self-play match (45,000 ticks) the flood ran 338 times for 105 ms in all and spared 4,480 failed searches.

**Checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/boxedInMoveSearchCount.test.ts` counts `findGridPath` calls, not time: the boot-map walk with the AI off, 110 ticks, at most one failed search per ordered unit (8) and at most 24 searches a tick, and at least ten ticks with a failed search made by the herd system (read off the call stack), so the fixture still boxes a sheep in. With the old planner restored it fails at tick 91: `failed path searches at tick 91: expected 1776 to be less than or equal to 8`. `tests/simulation/movementPlanCandidateReach.test.ts` holds the answers equal: on 600 seeded random grids the planner returns what the one-search-per-candidate planner (copied into the test) returns, and from a boxed-in cell on a 60x36 map it reads passability fewer than 3 map areas' worth of times, where the old planner read 68,484. Two mutants, a flood missing one direction and a flood that reaches nothing, fail the comparison. `npm run ai:tick-ab -- walk <main> <fix>` (a scenario added for this, in 25-tick chunks) stepped the walk in both trees with identical state at every 25-tick chunk and equal final save digests, at 225.7 against 16.0 CPU ms a tick, and `castle` stepped 45,000 ticks of AI self-play with identical state at every 1,000-tick chunk and equal final save digests (`afc6a004057439e5`), at a CPU ratio of 1.030 under 11-29 busy foreign cores. Bounds: the gate is one map and one order; other callers of the planner (resource and building approaches, wildlife ranges) get the same skip but are not counted; the equivalence argument rests on civ-engine's `findGridPath` keeping orthogonal steps and refusing a blocked start, which the oracle test would catch only on its own grids.

## 2026-10-11 — Six civilization bonuses did not do what Definitive Edition's do: counted twice, given to the wrong units, given to boar alone, missed by villagers already standing, and a node that never ran out (agent-observed by the spec worker and work 120's implementation worker, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** Agent-observed, not reported by a player. On 2026-10-11 the docs-only spec worker checked spec §11.11 against the code by calling each bonus function, and work 120's worker then reproduced each case in a match against a control civilization (`tests/simulation/civBonusDeParityInMatch.test.ts`, 5 of 5 red before the fix). An Indian Scout's first hit on a House did 4 more damage than a Briton Scout's, where DE gives 2 more. An Aztec Dock trained a Fishing Ship in 340 ticks against a control's 400. Deer gave Mongol hunters no +40% and Goth hunters no +15 carry. An Inca villager standing when the Castle Age landed kept armor 0 after the Scale Mail researched in the Feudal Age, while a villager trained after the advance had 1. The last case was found by work 120's worker while giving the Goths DE's "last longer" rule: a Mayan villager emptied a 20-food berry bush at tick 12,466, against a control's 665.

**Investigation.** Each line was read against DE's civilization help texts in the pinned dataset, SiegeEngineers/aoe2techtree at `3bb43b14` (DE update 185872); the SHA-256 of the copy read matched the pins in `tests/content/fixtures/deProjectileStats.json`.
- **Indians.** "Scout Cavalry-line and Camel Units +2 attack vs. buildings" is DE's Hindustani team bonus, and there is no civilization line beside it. v0.3.144 put it in `teamBuildingAttackBonus`, which covers the whole side, owner included, and left the older owner-only copy in `CIV_BONUSES`. `deliverUnitAttackOnBuilding` added both.
- **Aztecs.** "Military Units train +15% faster". The rule was `unit !== 'villager'`, so it also sped the Fishing Ship, Transport Ship, Trade Cog and Trade Cart. It also multiplied the time by 0.85. The dataset holds no effect values, so the rate is a judgement from DE's wording: DE writes Conscription, the Conquerors' x0.75 time, as "work +33% faster", so its "+N% faster" reads as a work rate and +15% as the time divided by 1.15. 0.85 was about +17.6%.
- **Goths.** DE's line is "Hunters carry +15; hunted animals last +20% longer". It has no "Villagers +5 attack vs. wild boar", the line the brief and `civilizations.csv` name, so the missing piece was the +20% longer, not the +5. The carry named boar only.
- **Mongols.** "Hunters work +40% faster" named boar only, and deer have been huntable since v0.3.167.
- **Incas.** "Villagers affected by Infantry Blacksmith upgrades starting in Castle Age" was checked when a technology finished and when a villager was created (`civVillagersTakeInfantryArmor`). The age-up sweep (`applyAgeScaledHpSweep`) re-derived the armor ladders but never this scope. A census of the sweep against the combat-state factory, over every civilization, trainable unit and advance, found this one pair and no other in armor, pierce armor, attack, range and reload.
- **Mayans.** "Resources last +15% longer" capped a swing at the node's amount but removed only that amount divided by 1.15. A node below one swing therefore lost 87% of what was left per swing and reached zero only through floating-point underflow, about 365 swings later. Measured in `queued-orders-fixture`: below one unit at tick 754, empty at 12,466.

**Root cause.** Two shapes. A DE scope was approximated by the nearest predicate at hand (`unit !== 'villager'`, `kind: 'boar'`). And a bonus lives in two places that must agree, with one updated and not the other: the team seam and the civilization table; the factory and the age-up sweep; a swing's yield cap and its removal.

**Fixed (v0.3.248).** The Indian table copy is gone, and the team seam gives the side, owner included, +2. One `isMilitaryUnit` predicate, every unit but the five civilian ones, scopes both the Aztec time and the Inca food. The Aztec time is divided by 1.15. One `HUNTED_KINDS` set (boar, deer) feeds the Mongol rate, the Goth carry and the new Goth duration (x1.2). The sweep gives a standing Inca villager the infantry armor already researched when the clause turns on. Durations come from `civResourceDurationMultiplier`, a table field (Mayans 1.15, Goth hunted animals 1.2): a swing yields at most the node's amount times the bonus, and the swing that takes the last of it empties the node. For an owner with no duration bonus the expression is the old one. The save format is unchanged.

**Checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/civBonusDeParity.test.ts` is a census. For every civilization, trainable unit and age, an owner's total attack bonus against buildings must equal what a Briton ally gets from the team lines, plus DE's owner-only Goth ladder. Every trainable unit's Aztec time and Inca food cost are checked against DE's five civilian units. Every resource kind is classified from DE, and a new kind fails typecheck until classified; the Mongol rate, the Goth carry and duration, and the Mayan duration are checked against that. `tests/simulation/civBonusDeParityInMatch.test.ts` plays each case in a match against a control. `tests/simulation/ageUpSweepMatchesFactory.test.ts` holds the sweep to the factory for every civilization, unit and advance, with nothing and with every hosted technology researched. Mutations, each restored, 15 gate runs, all red: the Indian copy put back (`Indians scout dark-age: expected 4 to be 2`); the Aztec scope (`trade-cart: expected 0.8695652173913044 to be close to 1`, and `expected 348 to be 400` in the match); the 0.85 rate; the Inca food scope; boar-only Mongol rate (`Mongols deer: expected 1 to be 1.4`) and Goth carry; the Goth duration removed (`expected 340 to be close to 408`); the old swing (`expected 3000 to be less than 831.25` and `expected 5396 to be less than 2558.75`); and the sweep's armor step made a no-op (`Incas villager feudal-age -> castle-age: armor: expected 1 to be 4`). Bounds: hit points are outside the sweep census, because the sweep scales stored hit points, flat bonuses included, by the ladder's ratio while the factory adds the flats after. Which units DE's "Foot Archers", "Infantry" and "Camel Units" cover is read from the code, not checked. Monks count as military. Whether DE's Mayan bonus covers farms is not checked; this build excludes them.

## 2026-10-11 — A stone aimed at a deleted House hit the foundation that took its id (found by the independent review of the building-sight change, FIXED and gated v0.3.247)

**Symptom.** A shot in flight named its target and its shooter by bare entity id. Round 2 of the building-sight review (`docs/threads/done/building-vision/2026-09-25/2/REVIEW.md`, "Seen on the way") saw that a shot at a building damages whatever building holds the id when it lands. Reproduced through real commands on `building-shot-landing-fixture` before the fix: owner 1's Mangonel looses a stone at owner 2's House; while it is in the air, owner 2 deletes the House and one of its Villagers puts a new House foundation on the same spot. The foundation took the House's id, and the stone took it from 90 to 15 hit points. Any entity created while a shot is in the air can take its dead target's id. A building that takes a dead building's id takes the shot's building damage wherever it stands; a unit that takes a dead unit's id, or an animal a dead animal's, is hit only if it also stands where the shot lands.

**Investigation.** civ-engine's entity manager (`../civ-engine/src/entity-manager.ts`, read and not modified) raises an id's generation when its entity is destroyed and pushes the id onto a free list that `create()` pops first, so the next entity created anywhere takes the id destroyed last; `projectileTargetIdentity.test.ts` holds that on the game's own world. `resolveOne` in `projectileOps.ts` acted on `shot.targetId` and `shot.attackerId` with no generation in four places: building damage through the `damageBuilding` wiring in `registerAllSystems.ts`, which has no position check; a hit on a unit or an animal; the shooter's exemption from its own blast (`applyUnitBlast` spares `attacker.id`); and the unit an animal the shot hurts turns on (`shooterRef`, which ruled out another player's unit by owner and nothing else).

**Root cause.** `ProjectileState` stored ids without generations, so a shot could not tell its entity from the next one at the same id. The comment on `shooterRef` already named the missing save field.

**Fixed.** A shot stores `attackerGeneration` and `targetGeneration` at launch: `launchProjectile` now takes the world, and a ground target stores `NO_ENTITY_GENERATION` (-1), which matches nothing. When the shot lands, each id counts only while its entity is current. A target that is no longer the entity aimed at takes no building damage, no unit or animal hit and no attack-warning blow, and a stone still blasts where it lands. The blast spares the shooter, and an animal turns on it, only while it is current, whoever owns it by then. `projectilesCodec` carries both fields unchanged. A shot from an older save or recording has neither and resolves by bare id, as before; that bound is in spec §10.4.

**Checked from now on.**
- `tests/simulation/projectileNamedEntities.test.ts`, on a real civ-engine world: each entity a shot names (a building, a unit and an animal as the target; the shooter for its blast and for an animal's turn) is replaced in flight by a new entity that takes its id. A replaced target must take nothing, and a unit that replaced the shooter must be caught by the blast and never turned on. Each case has two controls with nothing replaced, one on fresh ids and one with the shooter, and in the target cases the target too, on recycled ids; a shooter converted in flight is still turned on and still spared its own blast. Red with the target resolved by bare id again (the three target cases), with the blast sparing the shooter's bare id (the blast case), with the shooter told apart by owner again (the replaced and converted animal-turn cases), with an owner check on the blast's exemption (the converted-Mangonel case), and with a stored generation of 0 for every entity (the five recycled-id controls and the generation record).
- `tests/simulation/projectileTargetIdentity.test.ts`: the engine's id reuse, and the House scene through real commands. Red with the bare-id target: "90 -> 15 hit points".
- `tests/simulation/projectileGenerationSave.test.ts`: a save made mid-flight writes and loads both generations, the loaded stone lands on its target standing on a recycled id, and it spares a foundation that takes its target's id after the load. A save without the fields loads, its stone lands on its House, the bare-id bound is pinned and the next stone carries both generations. Red with the bare-id target, with a codec that drops the fields on load, and with a stored generation of 0.
- `tests/replay/projectileGenerationReplay.test.ts`: a recording holds both generations, self-checks and replays the foundation unharmed. A recording whose one snapshot lacks them opens and plays the stone to its landing by bare id. Red with the codec that drops the fields (the modern recording) and with the bare-id target (the old recording no longer differs from the live run).

**Bounds.** One fixture for the live scene. A unit or an animal that takes a dead target's id is hit only if it also stands where the shot lands, which no real command arranges inside one flight, so those cases run on a bare civ-engine world through `resolveDueProjectiles`. The attack warning's feed still names a shot's shooter by its id at launch; nothing in the simulation reads it back. A shot from an older save stays bare-id for the rest of its flight. A recording made before v0.3.247 fails `selfCheck` by design wherever a snapshot holds a shot loosed since the snapshot before it: every shot loosed while it replays gets generations that its snapshots lack (spec §10.4).

**The independent review.** Codex (gpt-6-astra, xhigh) and Claude (claude-opus-5-5[1m], max) reviewed commit 2d78480e read-only and found no blocker. Codex found no material finding and two nits; Claude found two should-fixes (no control would fail a shot that stored a wrong generation, and the selfCheck divergence of older recordings went unsaid) and five nits. All nine findings, one of them the owner's own, were taken: [round 0](../work/114_projectile-target-generation/reviews/0_implementation.md). Claude's focused re-review of the repair found no blocker or should-fix and five nits, all taken: [round 1](../work/114_projectile-target-generation/reviews/1_implementation.md).

## 2026-10-11 — A Byzantine gate took the age-scaled hit-point bonus that civilizations.csv excepts (agent-observed by the docs-only spec worker, FIXED and gated v0.3.245)

**Symptom.** On 2026-10-11 the docs-only spec worker, checking the spec's §11.11 list of civilization bonuses against the code, measured a Britons Stone Gate at 2,750 maximum hit points and a Byzantine one at 3,575 in the Castle Age (2,750 × 1.3). `design/stats/civilizations.csv` gives Byzantine buildings "(except gates)" +10/20/30/40% hit points by age. Work 119 reproduced it in all four ages with the new test before the fix: a Palisade Gate laid out at 275, 300, 325 and 350 against the Britons' 250, and a Stone Gate at 3,025 seeded in the Dark Age, 3,575 laid out in the Castle Age and 3,850 in the Imperial. It also showed in the real game: on the dev server with `?seed=gate-fixture&civ=Byzantines`, a mouse click on the gate made the selection panel read Health 3575 / 3575 with the old factor and 2750 / 2750 with the fix, while the Town Center kept its 3120 (2,400 × 1.3).

**Investigation and root cause.** `ageScaledBuildingHpFactor` in `src/game/simulation/ageScaledHp.ts` discarded its building-type argument (`void buildingType`), and its comment said "gates are not a BuildingType here". `stone-gate` and `palisade-gate` joined `BuildingType` in v0.3.31; the ladder landed in v0.3.86 on the older belief. Two paths read the factor, creation (`addBuildingEntity` in `bridge/entityCreateOps.ts`) and the age-up sweep (`applyAgeScaledHpSweep` in `bridge/ageScaledHpSweep.ts`), so both gave gates the ladder. The existing tests sampled the Town Center, House, Castle, Wonder, Outpost and Barracks, and nothing compared a gate across civilizations.

**Fix and check.** The factor returns 1 for `isGateBuilding` types, the one place both paths read. `tests/simulation/byzantineGateHp.test.ts` walks one of every building type, seeded in the Dark Age, through the Feudal, Castle and Imperial advances (the sweep), and has a villager lay out a gate and a House in each age through the real placement commands (creation), against the same buildings under the Britons. `tests/simulation/ageScaledHp.test.ts` adds the pure census over every `BuildingType` and age. Each mutation was restored by hash after its run. The old factor turned 6 tests red, among them "stone-gate in the castle-age: Byzantine 3575, Britons 2750". A carve-out naming the Stone Gate only turned the same 6 red on the Palisade Gate. Breaking the sweep path alone failed only the sweep case ("stone-gate in the feudal-age: Byzantine 3000, Britons 2750"). Breaking creation alone failed the 5 creation cases. Removing the ladder from every building failed 10, the existing ladder tests included.

**Bound: saves.** A gate already standing in a game saved before v0.3.245 keeps its raised hit points until it is destroyed. Loading restores stored building hit points as they are (`hydrateFromSavedGame.ts`): a gate stored at 3,575 loads at 3,575 and stays there (measured with the bridge's own save and load). The sweep now leaves gates alone, and Masonry and Architecture multiply the stored value, so the excess rides along. Matching Definitive Edition, which never has such a gate, would mean recomputing gate hit points on load. That is a change to `hydrateFromSavedGame.ts`, the save path work 114 owns in this batch, so it is left as it is. Leaving it is reversible: a save holds the civilization, age, technologies and building type, so a later load-time correction can still reach these gates.

## 2026-10-11 — A unit inside a building or aboard a transport during its line upgrade came out with its old sight for the rest of the match (found by the independent review of the building-sight change, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** Found by the review of v0.3.240's building sight, as something seen on the way ([round 2](../threads/done/building-vision/2026-09-25/2/REVIEW.md)), not by a player. Reproduced on the real bridge through the player's own controls in `castle-upgrades-fixture` (Saracens): two Archers trained from one Archery Range queue, one sent into the Town Center with a right click, the Crossbowman upgrade researched at the Archery Range, then Ungarrison. The Crossbowman that came out saw 6, an Archer's line of sight, while its twin outside saw 7. The same happened after a save made while it was inside, and with the upgrade researched in a loaded game.

**Investigation.** Garrisoning (`garrisonOps.ts`, the building path and the transport path alike) takes the unit's `visionSource` off it and keeps a copy in `garrisonedUnitVisionSourcesCodec` until the unit comes out. The line-upgrade sweep (`upgradeOwnedUnits` in `technologyUnitSweeps.ts`) visits every owned unit of the old tier. A unit inside keeps its `unit` component, so it was upgraded in type, hit points and attack, but its sight was re-derived only where `getComponent(id, 'visionSource')` found one. To find the class, a census applied every one of the 143 technologies to a world holding, for every unit type but a ship, one twin inside a Castle or aboard a Transport Ship and one outside. Before the fix it found 158 twin pairs apart: every one of the 39 land rows of the 46-row upgrade table, on each host the unit can use, and nothing else. Tracking, which the task card named as a second member, was retired with DE in v0.3.140 and no longer exists. Town Watch, Town Patrol and the Outpost's per-age sight touch buildings only. A Monk's conversion, the one other in-place write to a unit's sight (its owner), cannot reach a unit inside: the Monk drops a task whose target has no position, and an occupied building holds out.

**Root cause.** A unit's sight lives in two places, on the unit while it is in the field and in a side map while it is inside, and the one effect that changes it in place knew only the first.

**Fixed.** `changeUnitSight` in `garrisonOps.ts` changes a unit's sight wherever it is kept: the component on a unit in the field, or the kept copy, replaced through `accessor.mutate` so the save carries it. The upgrade sweep goes through it. The kept copy is updated rather than re-derived when the unit comes out, because units carry sight their type's table does not give: a real map's starting Scout sees 6 against the Scout's 4, and fixtures name their own. Deriving on the way out would have changed the sight of units that were never upgraded. The save format is unchanged. A game saved before the fix keeps the stale copy it recorded, and nothing repairs it.

**Checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/garrisonedSightUpgrades.test.ts` uses the real controls: trained twins, garrison, research, Ungarrison. The twin that was inside must see what its twin outside sees, live, through a save made while it is inside, and with the upgrade researched after a load. `tests/simulation/garrisonedSightCensus.test.ts` applies every row of `RESEARCH_COSTS` through `applyTechnology`, with twins starting at sight 1 so that every upgrade row is a witness, on a Mongol seat with Frank and Korean allies so the sight bonuses an upgrade re-derives are live. It fails by technology and unit. It also holds its account of the class both ways: the technologies that move a unit's sight must be exactly the upgrade rows with a land unit to upgrade, so a new member, or a row that moves nobody, fails by name. ## 2026-09-03 — A decided match never ends, because the AI can only aim at a Town Center (PARTLY FIXED: 1 of 4 seeds resolved then; 3 of 4 after the 2026-09-04 garrison entry above, OPEN)

**Symptom.** The standing acceptance-test failure "matches do not resolve": at 60,000 ticks — 100 minutes of game time — every seed is still running. Measured on four seeds after the wood fix, all four still ran to the horizon.

**Investigation.** `gold-rush` gave the clean case. From tick 48,000 to 60,000, sampled every 4,000 and byte-identical at every sample, owner 2 held:

    pop 0/5   villagers 0   army 0   buildings: blacksmith x1, house x1

Zero units. No Town Center, so it can never produce a unit again. It is functionally dead and cannot recover. Meanwhile owner 1 stood at 130 population with an army of 90, building farms.

**Root cause.** Two rules that are each correct and together deadlock.

`conquestOutcomeSystem` ends a match only when a player has NO UNITS **AND NO BUILDINGS** — which is right; a player with a building standing has not been conquered.

`pickAttackTarget` chooses the AI's march target from `townCentersByOwner`, a map keyed on owners that still HAVE a Town Center. Raze it and that owner drops out of the target list entirely: `targetOwner` becomes null, the attack phase's push branch has nothing to march on, and the surviving buildings are attacked only if they happen to fall inside somebody's vision. The AI is structurally unable to finish what it started.

So the winner cannot satisfy the condition the loser is being kept alive by.

**Fix, and the first version of it was WRONG in a way worth recording.** The obvious shape — give `pickAttackTarget` a fallback map and let it return the surviving building in `targetOwner` / `targetTownCenterId` / `targetTownCenterPosition` — passed its own unit gate, resolved `gold-rush`, and REGRESSED THE AGE-UP: `aiPlayer.test.ts` :: "ages up through the ages within a generous tick budget" went red, the AI reaching Feudal at 2,061 and never Castle within 8,000 ticks. Cause: `targetTownCenterPosition` is not private to the attack phase. `aiFerryPhase` reads it too, and when it is set with the target not land-reachable it stands down the ordinary march AND discretionary building to fund a Transport Ship — so filling that field for a Town-Center-less enemy stalled the economy. Isolated by disabling only the fallback and re-running: 24 of 24 passed.

The shipped fix is therefore SURGICAL. `pickAttackTarget` is byte-identical to what it was. A separate `lastResortTargetId` / `lastResortTargetPosition` pair is computed alongside it and read by the attack phase and nothing else, in its final push branch, only when no enemy Town Center is left. Deterministic — owners and entity ids both sorted, so a replay picks the same building. `gold-rush` still resolves at exactly tick 41,967, and the age-up test is green.

**The lesson, which is about the FIELD and not the feature.** A context field named for one thing (`targetTownCenterPosition`) had a second reader with different semantics, and reusing it to mean "the place to march" was correct for the phase being edited and wrong for the phase that was not. The cost of finding that was one full gate run; the cost of not finding it would have been a shipped economy regression that the resolution measurement would have applauded.

**Effect, measured on four seeds at 60,000 ticks, before and after.** `gold-rush` goes from running-at-60,000 to **victory at tick 41,967**. The other three still run, and the reasons are DIFFERENT defects rather than this one failing:

- `arena` and `coastal`: owner 2 still HAS a Town Center, so the fallback never engages. Owner 1 has an army of 105 and does not finish it — on arena that base sits inside the script's own stone wall ring, which points at attack EXECUTION (pathing to or breaching a walled base) rather than at targeting.
- `fortress`: neither side has an army to attack with (2 and 0 in the Imperial age). That is the villager-target-versus-population-cap chain in the entry above, not this one.

**How it is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/aiAttacksAnyEnemy.test.ts` :: `an enemy with no Town Center is still a target` — four cases: the fallback fires when the enemy has lost its Town Center; a Town Center still outranks a much closer bare building; the two-argument form is unchanged; and an ALLY without a Town Center is never targeted. Red-checked by reintroducing the defect (iterating an empty list instead of the fallback map): the first case fails with `expected null to be 2`, and all four pass again on revert.

**Bound of that gate, named.** It is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE. It does not prove the army reaches the building, kills it, or that the match ends — arena and coastal are live proof that a correct target is not sufficient. The end-to-end claim rests on the `gold-rush` seed alone, at one horizon.

**What this predicts.** The remaining two causes are both "the army exists and does not close": walls it will not breach, and an army too small to matter. Both are about what the military DOES rather than what it is pointed at, and neither is touched here.

**What it actually was, found 2026-09-04.** The prediction above was half right and half wrong, and the wrong half is the useful one. It reads `arena` and `coastal` as "the army exists and does not close" — walls, or too few units. The army was not closing because it was never ORDERED to: the attack phase's first preference is the target enemy's villager, and a GARRISONED villager is alive with no position, so the order silently no-opped and the branch `continue`d before the fallbacks this entry added. Those fallbacks were correct and were never reached. See the 2026-09-04 entry above; with that fixed, three of four seeds resolve.

**And the bound named above is exactly where it hid.** This gate is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE, and it says so. The defect was one line earlier, in a preference that never got as far as choosing.

## 2026-08-30 — Gather over-subscription counts EVERY owner's villagers (LATENT, not fixed, OPEN)

2. *It introduces self-blending seams.* The mirror copies a unit's own overlapping parts, and a translucent instanced part that overlaps itself blends twice — solved per-pixel as 452 px at the declared 0.55, 68 at 0.7975 (1−0.45²) and 6 at 0.9089 (1−0.45³). About 14% of the body draws at the wrong opacity, as streaks down the arm and torso. The engine has the cure and does not export it (see `docs/engine-feedback/current.md`).
3. *It degrades the half of the cue that matters most.* The enemy colour is a coral in the same hue family as every roof in the palette, so blending erases it: mean RGB distance from the background over a bright roof fell from 204.2 opaque to **82.0**, while the friendly blue — orange's complement — held at 196.9. The cue exists to answer "are those mine?", and it was the *not mine* answer that went.

Raising opacity to 0.78 fixed the measured colour bar but none of the three causes.

**Root cause of the DEFECT (still open).** Uniform alpha is the wrong technique for this cue in this renderer. What would work is a treatment that does not depend on blending: a contour, a hatch, or a colour chosen for contrast against the building palette rather than for ownership alone. The enemy coral in particular should be re-picked against the roof palette, which is a change that needs no engine feature and no transparency.

**How it is checked from now on.** It is not, and that is the honest state — no gate covers "does this cue read as behind rather than on top", because it is a judgement about a rendered image. What the attempt DID leave behind is the measurement method: capture background / opaque / candidate under both art styles, solve the per-pixel alpha, and measure mean RGB distance from the local background for BOTH ownership colours over both a bright and a dark building. Any future attempt that does not do that is guessing, and the first attempt's 0.55 was a guess that looked right in one capture of one art style with one owner.

**What this predicts.** Every other overlay that might reach for transparency has the same two traps waiting — the moebius quantiser and self-blending — and every two-colour ownership cue should be checked in BOTH colours against the palette it will sit on, not just the one the showcase fixture happens to spawn.
