# Defect register

The standing list of what the gates could not see. One entry per defect that reached a build: the symptom as it presented, what the investigation found, the root cause, and the check that covers the defect's whole CLASS from now on.

Unlike a lesson, an entry stays after it becomes a gate. The register is not a to-do list — it is the record of where defects came from, which is the best available guide to where the next one is.

**What lives here, and what rolled over.** This file holds the 12 most recent CLOSED entries plus every OPEN one, whatever its age, newest first. Everything older and closed is in [`defect-register-past.md`](defect-register-past.md), moved verbatim. An OPEN entry never rolls over — it leaves this file by being closed, not by ageing, and a heading that says the defect is still live is not a closure however it spells it: "(LATENT, not fixed)", "(PARTLY FIXED: ...)" and "(... still unfixed)" all keep the entry here, and each of those three had been archived once before the gate learned to refuse them. The rollover is done by the task whose new entry pushes this file past 12 closed entries, in that task's own commit, the way the devlog archives its detailed file; never by a later sweep. `tests/architecture/defectRegisterRollover.test.ts` holds all of that. An "entry above" or "entry below" written before the split may name an entry that now lives in the archive, and a rollover can strand a pointer in either direction; the archive's header lists the seven that cross today.

## 2026-09-10 — The villager-defence gate plays one configuration, and the defect is live in another (found while measuring the Imperial surplus trade, OPEN)

**Symptom.** On the coverage lab's configuration — `aoe2-prototype` with `resourcePreset: 'high'`, `difficulty: 'hard'`, `victory: 'conquest-only'` and `disableAiAttacks`, 45,000 ticks — owner 2 loses 34 villagers on `b929d07d`, 21 of them inside owner 1's Castle's reach. The bar in `tests/simulation/villagersAvoidEnemyDefencesSelfPlay.test.ts` is THREE, and that gate is green, because it plays the same seed with the DEFAULT bridge options and nothing else.

**What made it visible.** v0.3.222 grows owner 2's economy — Imperial 11,500 ticks earlier, a villager peak of 44 against 25 — and the same figure goes to 63 of 87. The seat trains right through it, holding 692 to 828 food, and dies anyway.

**Root cause, as far as this lane took it.** The 2026-09-02 entry ("Villagers gather inside an enemy Town Centre's range and die there", FIXED and gated v0.3.191) predicted exactly this in its own words: *"Any economy that grows enough hits this."* The rule it shipped excludes dangerous nodes and falls back through the other resource kinds *before it accepts a dangerous node as the last thing left* — and a richer preset exhausts the safe ground sooner, so the last-thing-left branch is where a big economy ends up. NOT VERIFIED: this lane did not record the dying villagers' TASK, so "gathering the last dangerous node" is the likeliest path and not a measured one. That is the first thing the next session should record, and `scripts/selfplay-audit.mjs` already prints it.

**Why it is not gated here.** Covering the lab's configuration means a SECOND 45,000-tick match in `npm test`, beside a gate that already costs 159-512 s and is one of the two longest in the suite. The bound is written into that gate's own header instead, with both figures, so its green is not read as a statement about the lab.

## 2026-09-10 — The content-coverage gate was green because the AI was broke, and went red when the AI got better (found by the standing loop, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** `tests/simulation/selfPlayContentCoverage.test.ts` went red on a working tree that had just got BETTER at playing the game, naming two technologies: "Technologies on the coverage floor were never exercised: onager-upgrade, heavy-scorpion-upgrade." Nothing about the content had changed. The same gate had been green on `a30af4fa` and had been green for every tree before it.

**Attribution.** Found by the standing loop. Two AI-affecting lanes — AI site placement and farm walkability — turned it red, and a 2x2 run in one process, with no file edited between arms (both switches were seams, not edits), found the mechanism.

**Root cause: a named technology on that floor was a claim about the AI's PURSE, not about the game's content.** `runProductionPhase` walks each producer's option list and buys **the first entry it can pay for**. So a name on the floor asserted that a particular purse existed at a tick that particular producer happened to be idle. The two most expensive entries on any list are therefore bought only when the AI has a surplus it cannot spend on anything else.

On main, owner 2 bought `onager-upgrade` (800 food, 500 wood) and `heavy-scorpion-upgrade` (1,000 food, 1,100 wood) **while holding 7 gold** — upgrades to an Onager and a Heavy Scorpion it could never train, since both units cost gold it did not have. It bought them because they were the first entries its idle wood bank could cover and it had nothing else to spend on. Its wood ramped from 48 to 1,247 starting at the same sample its gold died, and the two purchases are visible as stockpile drops of 500 and 1,100.

With the site-placement lane present, a monk reaches a relic at tick 31,000 (on main no seat ever deposits one at all), relic gold pays a steady 1 per tick for a total of +10,944, the AI can afford gold-priced units again, its wood sawtooths between 2 and 151 instead of banking, and it never reaches 500 idle wood — so it never buys those two upgrades. **Content coverage went UP, not down.**

**The reconstruction is anchored.** The control arm reproduced main exactly — Imperial at ticks 15,500 and 30,250 and an end purse of 1,262 food, 441 stone and 7 gold, four numbers matching the gate header's own recorded main figures verbatim — so the finding is not an artifact of the harness.

**And the trade is two-sided, which is the part a one-way floor could never show.** The shipped tree researches `plate-mail-armor` and `plate-barding`, two of the three names a 2026-09-06 commit had removed from this same floor *as gold-starved*, plus `conscription`. The same starvation that removed those paid for `onager-upgrade` and `heavy-scorpion-upgrade`; fix the starvation and they swap. (`bracer`, the third name removed on 2026-09-06, did not come back.) Measured on the shipped tree, censused to tick 45,000 with both seats Imperial: **15 building types, 17 unit types, 29 technologies**, against the old floor's 15, 17 and 28 names — a net gain of one, made of three gains and two losses. End purses: owner 1 {food 731, wood 10, gold 23, stone 1,093}; owner 2 {food 58, wood 18, gold 4,681, stone 315}.

**The gate's green depended on the AI doing badly.** That pathology is already an open register entry (2026-09-03, "The AI hoards the resource it cannot spend and starves on the one that gates every unit"), and this gate was leaning on it while saying nothing about it. The hoard did not go away when the lanes landed — it moved, from idle wood to idle gold, and it is still that entry's to close.

**How this class is checked from now on.** The named floors are gone, replaced by two things that cannot be satisfied by the AI being broke and one that cannot be satisfied by the AI being lucky.

- **Content reachability is price-free and match-free.** Three new gates drive every row of the game's own tables through the REAL command path with a purse nothing can exhaust: `tests/simulation/contentPipelineResearch.test.ts` buys all 143 `RESEARCH_COSTS` rows through `queue.research` (31,343 ticks, 51 s); `contentPipelineTraining.test.ts` fields all 93 `TRAINING_COSTS` rows through `queue.train` (30 s); `contentPipelineBuildings.test.ts` places and completes all 25 `AUTHORITATIVE_BUILDING_FOOTPRINTS` rows through `building.placeConfirm` (59 s). Each row must be OFFERED by the same menu surface the HUD reads, ACCEPTED by the real validator, CHARGED exactly its declared price out of a real stockpile, and COMPLETED. `onager-upgrade` and `heavy-scorpion-upgrade` live there now, and no match trajectory can move them. Because civilization tech trees are real content, the seats walk `CIVILIZATION_NAMES` until the table is covered rather than naming civilizations — a Britons seat alone reaches 84 of 143.
- **Self-play asserts per PRODUCER, not per NAME.** `selfPlayContentCoverage.test.ts` now requires every building type its expectation records as producing to research at least one technology from ITS OWN hosted list, and to field at least one unit from its own. `capped-ram-upgrade` at the Siege Workshop satisfies that exactly as `onager-upgrade` would.
- **The census is a two-way snapshot.** The exact censused sets live in `tests/simulation/selfPlayContentCensus.expected.json`, and ANY change fails printing both directions, so a gain costs the same attention as a loss and the commit that updates the file has to say which way the AI moved. That is what would have shown this incident as one trade — "gained conscription, plate-mail-armor and plate-barding; lost onager-upgrade and heavy-scorpion-upgrade" — instead of as a bare failure.

**Proved by mutation, each reaching its own mechanism and only that one.** Removing `heavy-scorpion-upgrade` from the Siege Workshop's hosting table reddens only the research pipeline ("queue.research was refused although the building offered it") while self-play stays green — which is the whole point, since self-play never buys it. Charging half its food reddens only the research pipeline ("the stockpile lost {food:500,wood:1100} but the cost table says {food:1000,wood:1100}"). Removing the blacksmith from the AI's research-building list reddens only the per-producer assertion ("owner 1's blacksmith researched nothing it hosts. It hosts 15 technologies and the seat researched none of them") while all three pipelines stay green. Granting Britons one free technology with no economic effect reddens only the snapshot, with the diff readable in both directions: "technologies: gained [sanctity] lost [—]".

**Bounds, named.** The self-play half is still ONE seed, ONE horizon, ONE configuration, and the snapshot is exact only on this tree. It counts units the owner HOLDS at a 250-tick sample, not units it trained, so a scenario-seeded starting unit counts toward its producer; it counts a building type once, so a second Barracks is invisible; and `researchProducers` asks for one technology, not for a good one. Several completed buildings still research nothing at all — the Market, Mill, Lumber Camp, Mining Camp and Monastery for one or both seats — and the expectation RECORDS that silence rather than asserting it away, because it is the 2026-09-03 hoarding defect showing through and not this gate's to fix. The pipeline half drives each row in ONE age at ONE civilization on one or two fixtures, checks no research or build TIME, no unit stats and no placement rules, and prices `spies` only against its floor (its real cost is a query: 200 gold per living enemy villager). Three technology rows — `bloodlines`, `rocketry`, `elite-chu-ko-nu-upgrade` — and eight unit rows are reachable only through a civilization that discounts them, so nothing ever charges them their raw table row.

## 2026-09-10 — The traffic-relief gate rested on a jam the match happened to produce, and the match stopped producing one twice (found at integration, FIXED and gated)

**Symptom.** `tests/simulation/movementTrafficReliefSelfPlay.test.ts` asserts that the movement arbiter's starvation rule relieves only a unit that is actually WAITING, never one that stood still by choice and then asked once. Both halves need a jam to exist. Twice now the jam stopped existing and the gate went quiet rather than red: `aoe2-prototype` stopped starving on 2026-09-02 when DE build times and nearest-first approaches let owner 1 win the self-play match outright, giving 0 admissions across 0 units at 30,000 ticks; and `default-seed`, the seed it moved to, read 0 admissions across 0 units at 30,000 ticks on THIS tree — re-measured with the old file's own body before the rewrite, so the figure is this session's and not a remembered one — because the jams that fed it were units piling up against farm walls, and farms had just become walkable ground.

**Root cause.** The gate's premise was that a self-play match on a named seed would produce a contested passage. That is a claim about the AI's luck, not about the rule under test, and it is invalidated by any change that makes the AI play differently — which is most changes. Worse, a zero is not a failure here: "phantom 0" over a run where the rule never fired is the vacuous zero, and the file's own history shows a 20,000-tick census calling the boot map "preserved field-for-field" when it had simply stopped before the rule engaged. Moving the seed a third time would have been a third bet on luck, and on `black-forest` and `arena` the inherited sweep is a single unit.

**Fix.** The contest is GEOMETRY now. `src/game/simulation/fixtures/trafficContest.ts` puts one owner's 24 woodcutters between a Town Centre and a woodline with a one-cell gap in a forest wall between them, so every trip crosses the gap twice — empty-handed out, carrying back — and the traffic replenishes itself for as long as the wood lasts. What the geometry guarantees is that the jam is head-on and continuous, which is the only thing an election can run on; the seeds guaranteed nothing. Four villagers parked in the far mouth of the gap, spawned last so they hold the highest ids and the lowest-id rule can never admit them, are the phantom half's instrument: a unit that stood still by choice and then asks. Measured at 4,000 ticks: 52 admissions across 3 units (2175, 2176, 2177), first at tick 1,837, phantom 0, with the owner's wood rising 200 to 720 the whole way — so this is starvation and not deadlock. Identical on four runs. The self-play case is DELETED rather than kept as a report: its 226 seconds were half the problem, and a report nobody fails on is a report nobody reads. Every figure it ever measured is kept in the file's header.

**How this class is checked from now on.** Both halves are asserted together, so neither can pass vacuously: the rule must ENGAGE (admissions greater than zero, with the measured 52 quoted in the failure message so a regression reads as a number rather than as a bare zero), and no unit that was not attempting to move may be relieved over units that were. The fixture asserts its own geometry, and wall time fell from 226 s to 6.7 s on a quiet machine.

**Proved by mutation.** Deleting `|| attemptGap > 1` from `trafficProgressClock` — the E1 rule the gate exists for — reddens the phantom half alone: 5 phantoms across 5 units, four of them the parked villagers relieved on their first ask after a 20-tick silence, while admissions (659) and wood (+440) stay above their floors. Widening the fixture's gap from 1 to 4 cells reddens the engagement half alone: 0 admissions across 0 units, phantom 0 still green and wood rising FASTER through the wide gap, which is the proof that a wide gap is not a blocked one. Each mutation reaches one half and only that half.

**BOUNDS — what a green run does not prove.** ONE geometry: a single one-cell gap in a straight wall of thickness 1 on a 60x36 map. A bend, a longer corridor, two gaps, or a gap made of buildings rather than forest terrain are not exercised. ONE unit type (villager) and ONE owner's crowd — nothing about behaviour between owners or for units of different speeds. A fixed 4,000-tick window with the first admission at 1,837; a change that delays the first admission past the window fails the engagement half rather than passing quietly. NOTHING about self-play, about any shipped seed, or about a real match — that is precisely the claim this file used to make and it is exactly what stopped being true, twice. And nothing about the clock's own numbers: the 750-tick threshold and its boundary stay pinned at unit level by `movementTrafficStarvationClock` and `movementTrafficFairness`.

## 2026-09-10 — A farm was a wall, so the most numerous building on an AI's map was also its most numerous barrier (found by the map-sealing investigation, FIXED and gated v0.3.221)

**Symptom.** A land unit ordered across a farm walked around it or, where farms closed the only gap, never arrived at all. A Town Centre whose whole perimeter was farms could not put down what it trained: the spawn fell to null rather than to a cell. And a unit that did stand on a farm cell held no slot on it — measured through the bridge on the showcase fixture, `villager 2190 cell {"x":8,"y":8} slot undefined,undefined overflow true`. In Definitive Edition a farm is ground with crops on it: every land unit of every player walks across it, and only the Mill or Town Centre beside it blocks.

**Attribution.** Found by the map-sealing investigation of 2026-09-06 (the entry below), not reported by the owner. The AI builds up to eight farms per base from the Feudal Age on — `targetFarmCount` is `max(2, min(8, floor(villagers / 3)))` — so farms were the single most numerous wall segment on a map the AI was sealing shut.

**Root cause, and why the obvious fix is the wrong one.** A building's footprint is an ENGINE occupancy claim, and the engine's sub-cell grid refuses a unit slot on any cell another entity occupies (`occupancy-cell-grid.isBlocked`). So a farm that keeps that claim can never be walked on whatever this side's passability predicate says: flipping the predicate alone would have routed units onto farms and then stacked them there as overflow — routed IN and given nowhere to stand. The claim itself had to change.

**Fix.** `src/game/simulation/passableStructures.ts` decides a CLAIM KIND once — `'farm'` for a farm, `'building'` for everything else — and `worldOccupancy.syncBuilding` takes it. A `'farm'` claim is recorded on this side only, where placement still counts it and movement, spawn and wildlife do not. The one place movement and placement diverge is `worldOccupancyCells.blocksWholeCell`, and it is now stated ONCE: spawn passability, wildlife passability, the spiral search and the group allocator had four copies of that predicate between them, and the allocators' duplicate was deleted with the other three. `isPlacementBlocked` still counts every claim, so nothing may be built on a farm. One consequence needed its own line: a relic dropped on a farm cell would claim it as a `'resource'` and wall it again, so `entityDestroyOps` excludes farm cells from relic drop sites by hand.

**The visual half was verified as a visual change.** Before capture, change, after capture, pixel diff, on `farmer-on-farm-showcase-fixture` at FOCUS 8,8 ZOOM 2.0 800x600, with the before arm taken on this tree under mutation (a) rather than on main, because the fixture does not exist on main. 4,550 of 480,000 pixels differ (0.95%), and the delta is in exactly two places: a 75x128 box holding one villager sprite and its shadow, and 21 pixels of the minimap where the same unit's dot sits. Under mutation (a) the villager is drawn off its own cell, standing amid the neighbouring plot's crop rows; shipped, it stands on the farm in front of the Town Centre with a slot of 0.5,0.75 and a clean ground shadow. Nothing else in the frame moved.

**How this class is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/farmsAreWalkable.test.ts` on `farms-are-walkable-fixture`: a forest cross cuts the map into quadrants, one gap closed by three ENEMY farms and another by two houses. A militia crosses the farm gap and must ENTER a farm cell to do it — positive evidence of the crossing, not inference from arrival — while the same order against the house gap must never enter a house cell and must not arrive, which is the control that stops a fix making every building walkable. A Town Centre ringed by twenty farms still spawns the villager it trains. Nothing can be built on a farm. The fixture's own farm and house counts are asserted at boot, so a fixture that failed to seed reads as red rather than as "never entered". `worldOccupancy.test.ts` and `worldOccupancyFastPath.test.ts` cover the claim kind at unit level, and `tests/rendering/aoeVoxelOcclusionSilhouettes.test.ts` covers what a unit on a farm looks like.

**Proved by mutation.** (a) `structureClaimKind` returning `'building'` for farms reddens the crossing and the spawn cases and leaves the house control green. (b) returning `'farm'` for houses reddens the control alone. Each reaches its own mechanism and only that one.

**BOUNDS — what a green run does not prove.** ONE map (`farms-are-walkable-fixture`), 1x1 farms, one ordered land unit type (militia) plus one trained villager, COMPLETE farms only — a foundation takes the same claim kind but is not exercised — no water domain, no wildlife, no save/load round trip, a 3,000-tick window per move order and a 600-tick window for the trained villager. Ships, deer, foundations, 3x3 farms and the farmer's own stance are outside it. The Fish Trap is the water analogue of the same question and is deliberately NOT answered: `tests/simulation/fishTrapBlocks.test.ts` pins a trap as a wall to ships, and until a line takes that on it stays a `'building'` claim.

## 2026-09-06 — A camera test that was really a frame-rate test, and a duration budget that its own measurement refused (found by the standing loop, FIXED; the proposed gate is REFUTED)

**Symptom.** `tests/browser/game-hud-and-camera-camera.spec.ts:5` failed on CI and passed on re-run with no code change. It holds the D key for 250 ms and required `scrollX` to grow by more than 40. CI read −333.33 → −298.33, exactly 35.

**Root cause is arithmetic, not luck.** Keyboard pan is `deltaMs / 1000 × 420 / zoom`, and `AoeVoxelGameView.frame` clamps `cameraDeltaMs` to `MAX_CAMERA_FRAME_DELTA_MS` = 100. One rendered frame therefore contributes at most `100/1000 × 420 / 1.2` = **35.00 px** at boot zoom, and the CI number is that figure to the last digit — one clamped frame. Requiring more than 40 required TWO frames inside 250 ms. **It was an eight-frames-per-second requirement written as a camera assertion.** The fix holds the key WHILE the poll runs, releasing it in a `finally`, so the spec measures the camera and not the host.

**The rejected instrument is the more useful half.** The first control tried was CPU throttling at 20x. It would have "proved the defect absent": throttling also delays CDP input handling, so the keyup lands late, the key stays down far longer than the spec intends, and the OLD form covered 175 px and PASSED. The control that actually reproduced CI was frame starvation — replacing `requestAnimationFrame` with a 300 ms `setTimeout` shim, which starves rendering without touching input timing.

**Five more specs had the same defect** and were fixed the same way, plus three that could not tell "held still" from "never rendered" and now wait on `waitForRenderedFrames`. The worst was not the camera: a scroll helper in `playOpening.ts` covered 0.0 px on a starved host and then picked its direction from what amounted to a coin toss.

**The queued lesson asked for a per-spec duration budget. It was built, wired in, proven to fire — and the measurement refused it.** Two full-suite runs, same machine, same bundle, nothing else changed. The five specs closest to their timeouts, as a percentage of budget:

| run | percentages |
|---|---|
| 1 | 95 / 59 / 46 / 40 / 39 |
| 2 | 48 / 47 / 44 / 41 / 38 |

Same tests. One went 28.4 s → 14.3 s, a factor of two; another moved the OTHER way. It is not one load factor scaling everything — it is per-test and it points in both directions. A threshold above the worst observation lands past 100%, which is the timeout that already exists; anything below it goes red because somebody was compiling. **So the budget ships as a REPORT, not a gate**: four lines at the end of every run naming the five specs closest to their timeouts, with `BROWSER_DURATION_BUDGET=0.8` to opt into failing. `tests/browser/helpers/durationBudgetReporter.ts`.

**Bounds.** Two runs on ONE machine; the CI runner's distribution is not measured and may be tighter or looser. The report names the top five only. `patrol-interaction.spec.ts:49` cannot be fixed inside the 30 s timeout without raising it and was deliberately left. `game-combat-and-meta-meta.spec.ts:5` sits at 95% of its budget and was deliberately NOT chunked, because a coarser break check would change what it asserts.

## 2026-09-06 — A building's health bar read FULL while it was still a foundation, so a finished building and a stalled one looked identical (found by the standing loop, FIXED and gated v0.3.220)

**Symptom.** A Mill reading `currentHp: 1000, maxHp: 1000` — a full bar, and 1000 is exactly the Mill's `hit_points` in `design/stats/structures.csv` — while it was still drawn as a foundation, still refused to work, and still counted 0 toward the Feudal advance. The reported observation is that it sat that way for more than 11,600 ticks with nothing on screen saying why.

**Attribution.** Found by the standing loop, playing the game. The owner reported none of it.

**Root cause: health and progress were two INDEPENDENT accumulators moving at different speeds.** `builderWorkStep` credited progress on AoE2's crew curve and health on a flat per-builder rate, in the same block, from the same tick:

- Progress: `builderProgressShare(alreadyCredited)` is `1` for the first builder credited on a site that tick and `1/3` for each of the rest, which telescopes to exactly `(n + 2) / 3` for a crew of n — the spec §7/§16 rule that a crew of n builds in `3 × base_time / (n + 2)`.
- Health: `hpPerTick = (maxHp − startHp) / totalBuildTicks`, added once **per builder**, so a crew of n added `n` of them per tick.

The ratio is `n ÷ ((n + 2) / 3)` = **`3n / (n + 2)`** — 1.8x at three builders, 2.7x at eighteen. Health therefore reached `maxHp` when the site was only `(n + 2) / 3n` built: **56% at three builders, 37% at eighteen**, and 100% only for the single builder that never showed the defect. From that moment `Math.min(maxHp, …)` clamped it and the bar said nothing at all for the rest of the build.

**What that did and did not cause.** It did not stall anything: completion is decided by progress alone (`playerCommandsSystem.ts`, `if (advanced.buildProgressTicks >= advanced.totalBuildTicks)`), so a site with a crew on it still finished on the curve. What it destroyed was the player's only reading. A full bar meant nothing, so a site being built, a site nobody was working any more, and a site that was finished were three states with one appearance — while `countCompletedAgePrerequisites` (`researchAvailability.ts`) counts only `isComplete` buildings and told the player they owned none of the buildings they were looking at. The reported match is the middle state: a foundation everyone had walked away from, indistinguishable from a finished Mill.

**Fix.** `src/game/simulation/bridge/constructionHealth.ts` (63 LOC) makes health a READING of progress rather than a second accumulator. `constructionEarnedHp` is the 10% foundation floor rising linearly to exactly `maxHp` at `progressTicks === totalBuildTicks` — the tick the caller finalizes on — so while a site is unfinished its health is strictly below full and a full bar cannot mean anything but a finished building. `constructionHealthAfterWork` composes two rules: that earned value is the CEILING, so health can never run ahead of the build, and `progressDelta × (maxHp − startHp) / totalBuildTicks` is the STEP, so a foundation an enemy chipped mends at the build rate instead of snapping back onto the curve the moment a villager touches it. In the ordinary case the two coincide exactly, which is why the step rule costs nothing: the ceiling is what applies. `entityCreateOps` now reads the same floor out of `constructionStartHp` instead of repeating `Math.max(1, Math.floor(fullHp * 0.1))`, so the module is the single place that knows it.

**How this class is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/constructionHealthMeansComplete.test.ts`, 10 cases on `multi-villager-construction-fixture`, every one driven through the player's own commands (select, place, order, walk away) and reading the numbers the player reads — `getRenderState()`'s `currentHp`/`maxHp`, which is what the renderer and the selection panel draw from, and the Town Centre's own age-up refusal text. Four building types across two footprints at crew sizes 1 and 5, where **crew 1 is the control**: health and progress advanced in lockstep for a single builder, so a crew-1 arm that also went red would have meant the sweep was measuring the fixture and not the crew. Three more cases cover the bar tracking real progress within 2%, an abandoned foundation still reading as unfinished, and the Feudal requirement counting every building whose bar reads full. The test recomputes the 10% floor from its own literal rather than importing `constructionStartHp`, so it is not the code agreeing with itself.

**Bounds, named.** One map (the flat all-grass fixture), owner 1, AI disabled, Dark Age, Britons, no build-rate technology and no Incas/Spanish/Treadmill multiplier — a civilization or technology multiplier changes the progress rate and is not exercised. Eighteen builders, the crew size the reported match used, is not reachable on this fixture, so the ratio is measured at its SMALLEST rather than its worst. Health is read from `getRenderState()`, which proves no pixel was drawn. The invariant is sampled once per tick, so a state existing only between two systems inside one tick is invisible to it. Nothing covers a foundation taking DAMAGE while being built, a save/load round trip, or an age or technology that raises `maxHp` mid-construction. And it says nothing about WHY a crew stops working a site — only that while a site is unfinished, its health bar says so.

## 2026-09-06 — Select the whole army, attack-move across the map, and nothing happens at all (found by the standing loop, FIXED and gated v0.3.219)

**Symptom.** In a 93-minute Black Forest match at Castle Age, 58 units all reporting idle: `issueAttackMoveCommand` returns `true`, and over 9,200 ticks — about fifteen game minutes — the army's centre moves from (14.0, 10.0) to (15.0, 9.7) and then never changes again. No movement, no message, no console error. A plain move to the same cell does the same; so does a move to an open cell eleven tiles away. Short orders work: +4/+3 moved the centre half a tile, an attack-move of −5/+5 moved it about two tiles. This is why no battle had ever happened in this game.

**Attribution.** Found by the standing loop, playing the game. The owner reported none of it.

**Does the path exist? No — and that was established, not assumed.** An independent 4-connected flood over terrain plus building and resource footprints (never over the pathfinder under test), run on a live bridge the way `scripts/aiArmyBlock.mjs` drives one: on `black-forest` with the human idle and only owner 2's AI building, the open ground falls from 370 cells at tick 0 to 315 by tick 20,000, the human's own component from 368 cells to 205, and **enemy units reachable from it from 4/4 to 0/26**. With both sides AI the human's units split into 50-, 43- and 2-cell pockets by tick 15,000. The map seals itself with the buildings a normal match puts up. The control that proves the search itself was sound: a 38-tile order inside the component is carried out and arrives.

**But a real search bug was hiding behind it.** The fallback for "the ordered cell cannot be reached" scanned `getNearestMoveCandidates(target)` — every cell within `max(mapWidth, mapHeight)` = 60 MANHATTAN cells of the target — and ran a full A* per candidate until one succeeded. The greatest distance between two cells on a 60x36 map is 94. A unit sealed in a pocket more than 60 cells from the ordered cell therefore had **no candidate it could reach at all**: `resolveMovePlanFromCache` returned null and `playerCommandsSystem`'s `if (!movePlan) clearUnitCommand(id)` dropped the order in silence. The unit did not walk one step toward it. A gate fixture reproduces exactly that: the sealed unit went from (2,2) to (2,2).

**And the second half: nothing ever told the player.** `clearUnitCommand` on a null plan is the whole of the old behaviour. An order accepted and dropped is the same defect the placement validator and the command-availability work fixed, one layer out — and a player in that state has no way to learn anything is wrong. The same shape had been seen earlier on the boot map: villagers walled in by their owner's own houses went idle and every subsequent gather, build and move order was accepted and dropped.

**Fix.** `src/game/simulation/bridge/moveDestinationSearch.ts` owns where a walk order ends. A flood out of the unit's OWN cell has no radius to run out of, and it picks the reachable cell with the smallest `(manhattan-to-target, y, x)` — deliberately the same key the old ring scan used (rings by distance, row-major within a ring, first reachable wins), so every order the old scan could already carry out ends in exactly the same cell. `describeWalkOrderReach` is the sentence the player now gets, computed where the order is given and put on the rejection queue the HUD already drains into a toast.

**It is also far cheaper, measured with both arms in ONE process against ONE bridge so the tree could not move between them.** Reachable order: 0.89 ms → 0.02 ms per call. Sealed order across a wall: **162.91 ms → 1.09 ms**. Both arms returned the identical destination and the identical path length in every case, which is what makes the equivalence claim a measurement rather than an assertion. The old cost was one unit for one replan; 58 units in that state would have frozen the game.

**How this class is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/walkOrderUnreachable.test.ts` on the new `walled-pocket-fixture`: a long order across a reachable map is carried out, a short order still works (the control against a fix that refuses too much), an order that cannot be satisfied still walks the unit as far toward it as it can, and both a move and an attack-move that cannot be satisfied produce something the player can observe. Proven red on the unfixed tree with the two controls green, then proven again by two TARGETED mutations that bind each half to its own mechanism: silencing only the message reddens exactly the two message cases; restoring only the give-up fallback reddens exactly the movement case, with the same `(2,2) to (2,2)` text.

**Bounds, named.** ONE map size (60x36) and ONE sealed shape — a 6x6 pocket inside a closed ring of trees. A pocket sealed by BUILDINGS is the same predicate (`blocksWholeCell` covers both) but is not exercised. LAND units only: a ship ordered inland finds no water seed and stays silent. Move and attack-move only; patrol, attack-ground, gather, build and garrison walks share the resolver and are not asserted. **A unit standing on a cell that became blocked underneath it still cannot move at all** — the flood returns null, the command is still cleared, and only the toast covers it; that is the spawn-egress class and is untouched here.

## 2026-09-06 — An enemy hit a villager twice in the player's own base and the screen said nothing, and the horn's own rule could never fire for a 4x4 building (found by the standing loop, FIXED and gated v0.3.217)

**Symptom.** At tick 3084 of `aoe2-prototype` an enemy militia walks into the human's base and hits villager #2205 twice, 25 -> 21 -> 17 HP. There was no banner, no toast, no minimap mark, no selection change and no message of any kind. AoE2 tells you when your economy is being killed; this told you nothing.

**Attribution.** Found by the standing loop, playing the game. The owner reported none of it.

**Two defects, and the second had never been hit by anything.**

**Defect one: there was no visual at all.** The horn already fired — counted in the live app by its own oscillators, not inferred from the code — so half the cue existed. A horn is the one cue a muted tab, a headphone-less player or a busy moment takes away, and nothing on screen said an attack was happening or where.

**Defect two: the horn's rule could not match a 4x4 building.** The old rule compared each recorded swing's target cell against every own building's and villager's RENDER ORIGIN, within one cell. A swing does not target the origin — `unitAttackAnimationFeed` records `targetRootX + (footprintWidth - 1) / 2`, the VISUAL CENTRE. For a 4x4 building that is **1.5 cells** away from the origin, so `Math.abs(entity.x - attack.targetX) <= 1` was false for every one of them: **the Town Center, the Market, the Castle and the Wonder were silently dropped by the check written to catch exactly this.** A 3x3 building sat at exactly 1.0 and matched on the boundary; villagers matched only because their footprint is 1x1 and the offset is zero. Nobody had hit it because the case that reproduces it is an attack on a 4x4 building specifically, and the play finding above is a villager.

**Root cause, and it ties both.** The attack feed is an ANIMATION feed. It records that a swing landed at a cell and discards the target id it was handed, because an animation only needs the cell. The one consumer that needs to know WHO was hit therefore had to infer it from coordinates, and it inferred it with the wrong convention. A rule reconstructed from geometry, when the identity was available and thrown away, is the shape of this defect rather than the off-by-1.5 itself.

**Fix.** The feed now carries `participants` — `attackerOwner`, `targetOwner`, and `targetIsEconomy` — read from the components at record time, on the ticks that already recorded a hit. `src/ui/hud/attackWarning.ts` owns the rule, `gameAudioController` becomes the single decision point for all three cues (horn, Space's jump target, minimap mark), and `mountGameAudio` no longer builds a list of own entities to compare against.

**The rule is now exact, and each narrowing was argued rather than assumed.** A warning is raised when a unit owned by ANOTHER PLAYER swings at a villager or a building owned by the human. Wildlife is excluded on both ends: a lured boar biting the villager that shot it happens in the opening of every match, on purpose, under the player's eye, and a horn for it is the storm the rule exists to avoid. Soldiers are excluded as targets: a villager losing health is news by definition, because nobody puts a villager in a fight on purpose, while a soldier taking a hit is the fight you went looking for. And the rule keys on who was HIT, never on who swung or on what happens to be standing nearby, so the Town Centre shooting back raises nothing.

**One tempting narrowing was explicitly REJECTED**: suppressing the warning when the camera is already looking at the spot. That reading is exactly backwards — the reproduced defect happened inside the player's own base with the camera on it, so a "you are already looking" rule would have suppressed the very case this exists for.

**Two placement decisions, both forced by existing rules.** The mark is raised OUTSIDE the mute switch, so a silenced player still sees where the raid is — the horn is the cue a real player most easily loses, which is the whole reason the visual was needed. And it is drawn ON the minimap rather than floating over it, because nothing may cover the minimap (local rules, 2026-09-02) and AoE2's own affordance is the minimap itself flashing.

**How this class is checked from now on.** Three gates. `tests/browser/attack-warning.spec.ts` (`npm run test:browser`) reproduces the raid on the real scenario at the real tick and asserts PIXELS on the minimap canvas — near-white ones, because owner 2's tint is itself a red and a "reddish" threshold would pass on the raider's own marker; the boot frame is asserted to carry zero such pixels, which is what makes the raid frame's count mean anything, and the spec fails with "no villager lost HP" if the scenario ever stops producing the raid, so a run that did not exercise the warning cannot report itself as one that did. `tests/ui/attackWarning.test.ts` holds the rule, the throttle and the mark over a synthetic feed, including the cases that must NOT warn. `tests/ui/gameAudio.test.ts` and `tests/ui/minimap.test.ts` were extended for the single decision point and the mark's paint.

**Bounds, named.** The browser gate is one scenario, one raid, and a tick window of 3050–3600. The feed records a SWING and only a unit can be an attacker in it, so an enemy tower or castle shooting a villager still raises nothing — the same gap the horn already had, and the case that matters (a raid, which is units) is covered. A saved game's restored attack entries carry no `participants`, because `hydrateUnitAttacks` drops them rather than re-validate a feed that is ten ticks deep; a load therefore starts silent until the next live swing. Nothing here checks that the mark is legible against every terrain, only that it is white enough to find.

## 2026-09-06 — The attack-warning throttle is 40 seconds and its own comment says 20, because it computes at a tick rate this build does not use (found at integration, reading the constant, FIXED and gated v0.3.218)

**Symptom.** `ATTACK_WARNING_THROTTLE_TICKS = 400` in `src/ui/hud/attackWarning.ts`, with the comment "AoE2 spaces its 'town under attack' warnings well apart; ~20s at 20 TPS". This build runs at **10 TPS** — `export const TPS = 10` in `src/game/simulation/prototypeScenario.ts`, and the spec's own numbers agree throughout (§9.6's garrison heal is "4 HP per second at 10 ticks per second"; §11's Horse Collar is "200 ticks (20 s)"). So 400 ticks is **40 seconds of game time at speed 1.0**, twice what the comment says it is.

**Attribution.** Found at integration while checking a changelog sentence that quoted the comment's 20 seconds; nobody played it and no gate saw it. It predates v0.3.217: the constant was `HORN_THROTTLE_TICKS = 400` with the same comment before the attack warning moved it, so the horn has been spaced at 40 seconds for as long as it has existed.

**What is established and what is NOT.** Established: the constant, the tick rate, and therefore the 40 seconds. NOT established: what AoE2: Definitive Edition actually does. The comment asserts ~20s, and a remembered game rule is a hypothesis here (local rules, "Behaviour matches the real Age of Empires II") — so this entry deliberately does not say the value is wrong, only that the code's stated intent and the code's behaviour disagree by a factor of two and one of them has to give.

**Why it is worth an entry rather than a comment fix.** Whichever way it resolves, something user-visible changes or a documented intent does: either the throttle halves to 200 ticks and a raided player is warned twice as often, or the intent is restated at 40 seconds and the divergence from the comment's "AoE2's rule" is recorded as deliberate. More importantly the rule that post describes has a **different SHAPE**: it suppresses the alarm only when two attacks fall within ten TILES *and* ten seconds. This throttle is purely temporal, so two raids far apart alarm twice there and once here, and **there is no single DE number to copy**. The value is therefore what the comment always intended — twenty seconds — rather than a measured DE figure, and that is written into the constant's own comment so nobody repeats the search.

**Open follow-up, and it is a feature gap rather than a defect:** the spatial half is unimplemented. DE's reported rule needs BOTH a distance and a time; ours has only the time, so a second raid on the opposite side of the base is silenced for twenty seconds when DE would alarm for it. Nothing here establishes that DE's rule is as that post describes, so implementing it would need the fact established first.

**How this class is checked from now on.** `tests/architecture/tickRateComments.test.ts` (`npm test`, so `npm run verify`) reads every `.ts`/`.tsx` file under `src/` and `tests/` and fails when a stated tick rate, a stated milliseconds-per-tick, or the arithmetic between a stated duration and a stated tick count disagrees with `TPS`. That is how it found a third instance, `bridge-additive-surfaces.test.ts` claiming 30 TPS. Its bounds are in its own header: it reads TEXT and cannot tell a true comment from a stale one that names no number; the arithmetic check needs a rate spelled with `TPS` in the same text, so a bare duration beside a tick constant is invisible to it; it skips a text with more than one duration or a slash list (three today) and one with no tick count to bind to (two today); 44 arithmetic pairs are checked and 5 skipped on the shipped tree.

## 2026-09-03 — A decided match never ends, because the AI can only aim at a Town Center (PARTLY FIXED: 1 of 4 seeds resolved then; 3 of 4 after the 2026-09-04 garrison entry above, OPEN)

**Root cause.** Two rules that are each correct and together deadlock.

`conquestOutcomeSystem` ends a match only when a player has NO UNITS **AND NO BUILDINGS** — which is right; a player with a building standing has not been conquered.

`pickAttackTarget` chooses the AI's march target from `townCentersByOwner`, a map keyed on owners that still HAVE a Town Center. Raze it and that owner drops out of the target list entirely: `targetOwner` becomes null, the attack phase's push branch has nothing to march on, and the surviving buildings are attacked only if they happen to fall inside somebody's vision. The AI is structurally unable to finish what it started.

So the winner cannot satisfy the condition the loser is being kept alive by.

**Fix, and the first version of it was WRONG in a way worth recording.** The obvious shape — give `pickAttackTarget` a fallback map and let it return the surviving building in `targetOwner` / `targetTownCenterId` / `targetTownCenterPosition` — passed its own unit gate, resolved `gold-rush`, and REGRESSED THE AGE-UP: `aiPlayer.test.ts` :: "ages up through the ages within a generous tick budget" went red, the AI reaching Feudal at 2,061 and never Castle within 8,000 ticks. Cause: `targetTownCenterPosition` is not private to the attack phase. `aiFerryPhase` reads it too, and when it is set with the target not land-reachable it stands down the ordinary march AND discretionary building to fund a Transport Ship — so filling that field for a Town-Center-less enemy stalled the economy. Isolated by disabling only the fallback and re-running: 24 of 24 passed.

The shipped fix is therefore SURGICAL. `pickAttackTarget` is byte-identical to what it was. A separate `lastResortTargetId` / `lastResortTargetPosition` pair is computed alongside it and read by the attack phase and nothing else, in its final push branch, only when no enemy Town Center is left. Deterministic — owners and entity ids both sorted, so a replay picks the same building. `gold-rush` still resolves at exactly tick 41,967, and the age-up test is green.

**The lesson, which is about the FIELD and not the feature.** A context field named for one thing (`targetTownCenterPosition`) had a second reader with different semantics, and reusing it to mean "the place to march" was correct for the phase being edited and wrong for the phase that was not. The cost of finding that was one full gate run; the cost of not finding it would have been a shipped economy regression that the resolution measurement would have applauded.

**Effect, measured on four seeds at 60,000 ticks, before and after.** `gold-rush` goes from running-at-60,000 to **victory at tick 41,967**. The other three still run, and the reasons are DIFFERENT defects rather than this one failing:

- `arena` and `coastal`: owner 2 still HAS a Town Center, so the fallback never engages. Owner 1 has an army of 105 and does not finish it — on arena that base sits inside the script's own stone wall ring, which points at attack EXECUTION (pathing to or breaching a walled base) rather than at targeting.
- `fortress`: neither side has an army to attack with (2 and 0 in the Imperial age). That is the villager-target-versus-population-cap chain in the entry above, not this one.

**How it is checked from now on.** `tests/simulation/aiAttacksAnyEnemy.test.ts` :: `an enemy with no Town Center is still a target` — four cases: the fallback fires when the enemy has lost its Town Center; a Town Center still outranks a much closer bare building; the two-argument form is unchanged; and an ALLY without a Town Center is never targeted. Red-checked by reintroducing the defect (iterating an empty list instead of the fallback map): the first case fails with `expected null to be 2`, and all four pass again on revert.

**Bound of that gate, named.** It is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE. It does not prove the army reaches the building, kills it, or that the match ends — arena and coastal are live proof that a correct target is not sufficient. The end-to-end claim rests on the `gold-rush` seed alone, at one horizon.

**What this predicts.** The remaining two causes are both "the army exists and does not close": walls it will not breach, and an army too small to matter. Both are about what the military DOES rather than what it is pointed at, and neither is touched here.

**What it actually was, found 2026-09-04.** The prediction above was half right and half wrong, and the wrong half is the useful one. It reads `arena` and `coastal` as "the army exists and does not close" — walls, or too few units. The army was not closing because it was never ORDERED to: the attack phase's first preference is the target enemy's villager, and a GARRISONED villager is alive with no position, so the order silently no-opped and the branch `continue`d before the fallbacks this entry added. Those fallbacks were correct and were never reached. See the 2026-09-04 entry above; with that fixed, three of four seeds resolve.

**And the bound named above is exactly where it hid.** This gate is a unit test on the TARGET CHOICE, and it says so. The defect was one line earlier, in a preference that never got as far as choosing.

## 2026-08-30 — Gather over-subscription counts EVERY owner's villagers (LATENT, not fixed, OPEN)

**What it is.** `villagerEconomySystem` builds `gatherTargetCounts` by walking `activeWorld.query('gatherer')` with no owner filter, so every gatherer in the match counts toward every resource's occupancy. That map is what `MAX_GATHERERS_PER_RESOURCE` (2) is checked against, and its stated purpose is to stop ONE player piling villagers on one node. Counting the opponent's villagers is wrong for that purpose: an enemy working a neutral resource can make it read over-subscribed for you, and your villagers fan out away from a resource that has room.

**Why it is not fixed.** Measured before touching it, which is the point of this entry: across `aoe2-prototype`, `default-seed` and `corpus-seed-b`, sampling every 100 ticks for 12,000 ticks each, **not one tick had a resource targeted by two different owners** — 0 of 360 samples, max owners on any single resource 1. Every current map keeps the players' resource rings apart, so the defect cannot fire. Fixing it means threading a per-owner count through five call sites in two files for no measurable behaviour change today.

**The trigger to watch for.** It becomes live the moment two players' gatherers can reach the same node: a map with a contested middle (gold or stone between bases), overlapping resource rings on a small map, or allied players sharing a base. Any of those should come with the fix — key the counts by owner — and this entry is the note that the machinery is already wrong when they arrive.

**What this predicts.** A shared counter with no owner dimension is a latent cross-player coupling wherever it appears. `gatherTargetCounts` is one; the same shape is worth looking for in any per-tick map keyed only by entity id.

## 2026-08-30 — The behind-building silhouette reads as a unit standing ON the building (OPEN)

**Symptom.** A unit hidden behind a building is cued by a flat-coloured mirror of its body drawn over the building. It is fully opaque, and at a Town Center that reads as a villager standing on the roof rather than as an x-ray of one behind the wall — noticed hands-on on 2026-08-29 ("three of them across the TC's upper tier read as villagers standing on the roof") and confirmed in the `occlusion-showcase-fixture` capture, where the blue figure sits on the roof with nothing to say it is a cue.

**Investigation.** The obvious fix — draw the mirror on a transparent material so the building reads through it — was implemented, measured, and REVERTED. It failed on three independent axes, each measured rather than argued:

1. *It is close to a no-op under the art style the game actually ships.* The default is `moebius`, whose tone-band quantiser snaps **24.1% of the cue's pixels byte-identical to the fully opaque render**; mean change against opaque is 46.6 there against 125.6 under `painted`. One opacity value cannot serve a banding and a non-banding pipeline.
2. *It introduces self-blending seams.* The mirror copies a unit's own overlapping parts, and a translucent instanced part that overlaps itself blends twice — solved per-pixel as 452 px at the declared 0.55, 68 at 0.7975 (1−0.45²) and 6 at 0.9089 (1−0.45³). About 14% of the body draws at the wrong opacity, as streaks down the arm and torso. The engine has the cure and does not export it (see `docs/engine-feedback/current.md`).
3. *It degrades the half of the cue that matters most.* The enemy colour is a coral in the same hue family as every roof in the palette, so blending erases it: mean RGB distance from the background over a bright roof fell from 204.2 opaque to **82.0**, while the friendly blue — orange's complement — held at 196.9. The cue exists to answer "are those mine?", and it was the *not mine* answer that went.

Raising opacity to 0.78 fixed the measured colour bar but none of the three causes.

**Root cause of the DEFECT (still open).** Uniform alpha is the wrong technique for this cue in this renderer. What would work is a treatment that does not depend on blending: a contour, a hatch, or a colour chosen for contrast against the building palette rather than for ownership alone. The enemy coral in particular should be re-picked against the roof palette, which is a change that needs no engine feature and no transparency.

**How it is checked from now on.** It is not, and that is the honest state — no gate covers "does this cue read as behind rather than on top", because it is a judgement about a rendered image. What the attempt DID leave behind is the measurement method: capture background / opaque / candidate under both art styles, solve the per-pixel alpha, and measure mean RGB distance from the local background for BOTH ownership colours over both a bright and a dark building. Any future attempt that does not do that is guessing, and the first attempt's 0.55 was a guess that looked right in one capture of one art style with one owner.

**What this predicts.** Every other overlay that might reach for transparency has the same two traps waiting — the moebius quantiser and self-blending — and every two-colour ownership cue should be checked in BOTH colours against the palette it will sit on, not just the one the showcase fixture happens to spawn.
