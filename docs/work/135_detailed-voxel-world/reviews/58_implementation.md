# Review 58: implementation

## Target

M22 head/neck artwork against the frozen original: a broad, low dragon head emerging from the ground, four unequal neck sections with three joints, and convincing volume. This is a major-form decision before finer art.

## Reviewers and coverage

Independent native-art reviewer. Inspected the reference and all eleven M22 PNGs individually with `view_image`, `detail: original`, including both sides, overhead, rear, low-up and the seeded exploratory angle. No earlier verdict was read.

## Reports

## Findings and disposition

1. **Major head form passes.** The broad skull, projecting muzzle and separate lower jaw read as a dragon. Overhead and rear views demonstrate real width; the head does not collapse into a side-facing cutout. The ground emergence has no humanoid torso, arms or feet.
2. **Neck structure is sufficient to advance.** Caster-side and caster-close show three larger, unequal lower spans and the short, partly hidden upper transition. Their changes of angle produce the required segmented rise. The joints read less crisply in the matched view, but this is no longer a torso-shaped mass.
3. **Full art quality remains unfinished.** The skull roof is dominated by parallel terraces and the rear projections resemble flat tabs. Against the reference's more varied brow, cheek and jaw planes, this still feels assembled from stacked plates. This is the clearest remaining sculptural weakness; deferred small eyes/teeth and retained islands are not blockers for this stage.

## Verification

Visual evidence only. All twelve image hashes were checked after inspection; the reference matches the assigned digest. No source, runtime, browser or CI checks were needed for this bounded art judgment.

## Round outcome

Advance M22 to finer art; retain it as the major-form baseline, without granting final art acceptance. Next step: sculpt a deliberate brow-to-muzzle break and cheek-to-jaw hinge within this silhouette, replacing the repetitive terracing with a few stronger head planes.
