## 2026-09-03 — Shadows take the shape of their caster (v0.3.194)

**Action.** Owner steering: "the shadow from units, and buildings, and environment objects such as trees and bushes and ores, they all need to look more realistic ... they need to roughly match the shape of the thing that created the shadow instead of always being a rectangular", plus a standing requirement — "whenever the shape of the object changes, the shadow also needs to be re-adjusted in the future". v0.3.193's caster was ONE AABB per entity, so every shadow was the same rectangle-derived hexagon. New `aoeVoxelShadowShape.ts` reads a caster's solid parts as a vertical PROFILE (at most four height bands, merged where consecutive slices share a footprint, reduced to the cap by cheapest adjacent merge) and tiles the union of those bands' swept footprints with triangles; new `aoeVoxelShadowWedge.ts` is the flat triangle geometry the lane draws on; `aoeVoxelCastShadows.ts` keeps only the depth half.

**Why a new geometry.** A silhouette is a polygon and an instanced box can only ever be a parallelogram on the ground — that is *why* every shadow was a rectangle, not an incidental choice. Triangles tile any polygon. The tiling is a strip decomposition in the sun's own frame: in shadow space each band's swept region is exactly `w in [LO_i(u), HI_i(u)]` with both bounds piecewise linear, so the union is the lower envelope of the `LO_i` under the upper envelope of the `HI_i`, sliced at every envelope breakpoint, two triangles per trapezoid, adjacent strips merged while a straight run stays within 0.03 world units of the exact outline.

**What was believed and proved false.**

1. *That the unit tests would catch a geometry error.* The monotone-chain turn test had its sign inverted (`cross * sign > 1e-12` where it must be `< -1e-12`), so `hullChain` returned the upper hull where the lower was asked for and a plain box tiled as two slivers. Every band-level test passed. What caught it was a coverage test that marches the sun ray independently of the tiling — the only assertion in the file that does not share code with the thing it checks.
2. *That the wedge should be a prism like the slab it replaced.* It was, at first. Two neighbouring pieces then stand their 0.036-tall side walls back to back along every SHARED edge, the translucent material blends both, and the tiling draws itself as a mesh of dark seam lines across every shadow in the scene — plainly visible at 2.4 zoom, worse the more pieces a silhouette has. No test saw it; a 12x crop of one tree's shadow did. Flat triangle, no walls, seams gone.
3. *That the overlap test in `resolveShadowLevels` could keep using the instance's own unit cube.* A wedge's enclosing parallelogram is half again the triangle's area, so casters that never share a pixel were reported as overlapping and lifted apart: 0.084 world units of lift on a 6x6 woodline against 0.038 once the test uses the three real corners. Lift is a shadow floating off its caster on screen, so this is a visible cost paid for a phantom.
4. *That "more correct" would look better.* It did not. The correct silhouette made every building look WORSE than the wrong one, and this was the most useful finding of the session — see below.

**The finding: a caster's own base is a receiver, not only a caster.** Almost every building here stands on a plinth wider than its walls, and the sun sits 52 degrees up, so the whole of a building's shadow lands INSIDE its own plinth. v0.3.193's extruded-footprint model escaped the plinth only because it pretended the building was as wide as its plinth all the way to the roof. With a true silhouette, the Town Center reached 0.82 tiles past a 3.44 tile plinth and the rest fell where nothing was drawing it — buildings read as sitting on the map rather than standing on it. A caster whose lowest band is a PAD now draws a second layer: the bands above, projected onto the pad's top plane, clipped to the pad, at the pad's height. The FLATNESS half of the pad test is the load-bearing one and is not a heuristic about size: wide-and-short alone also describes a gold mine's rubble, whose rock keeps going up through the band, so a patch drawn at that height would hang inside the rock. The test is on the widest part contributing to the band — its top must be the pad's top. Measured across nine archetypes: house/town-center/castle/relic pass, gold-mine (widest top 0.531 against a pad top of 0.479), tree, scorpion and sheep fail.

**Measurements.**

- Frame cost, measured rather than estimated: the old build and the new one were served side by side (ports 4173 and 4207) and the boot map's own renderer metrics read off both. Instances 862 -> 1188 (+38%), instance batches 9 -> 9, geometry resources 1 -> 2 (which is what the browser renderer spec's pinned metrics caught), retained typed-array bytes 197,328 -> 214,814 (+8.9%). Per CASTER the mean goes 3.0 -> 15.7 over thirteen archetypes, which overstates the frame impact because the shadow lane is a minority of the scene's instances. Bounded structurally rather than by hope: the envelope's breakpoint count is NOT a function of the band count (four bands can cross 56 ways), so the merge tolerance is doubled until the tiling fits `MAX_SHADOW_STRIPS` = 10, plus `MAX_PAD_STRIPS` = 4 for the pad layer.
- Render-only, and confined to the shadow surface: a sha256 over every unit, resource and building recipe's NON-shadow parts is `e2f40e86a2352cb74c7c071ab2c1e5b4f3f31a5b3d64463b4dd0f316a0471c3a` on BOTH sides, 2326 parts, measured by running the same probe in the pristine checkout and in this worktree. The shadow-only digest moves and the shadow part count goes 438 -> 2304 over those 146 recipe instances. The `memory` characterization digest is the control: memory entities cast nothing and it did not move, while the seven live ones did.
- Before/after at 1280x800, TICKS=1: 3.90% of pixels at the default zoom, 15.08% at 2.4, 15.41% at 2.4 under Painted, 1.40% at 0.7. Each diff confined to ground toward +x,+z of casters and to each building's own plinth rim, plus the translucent HUD bar the world shows through. These were RE-TAKEN: the first set (1.67 / 13.55 / 13.14 / 1.25) was measured before the flat geometry and the pad receiver landed, so it described a build that never shipped — writing it into the docs and then noticing is the reason the numbers here are the second set.

**Known limits, recorded rather than hidden.** The min/max envelope fills a notch: if one caster's bands ever produced a w-DISCONNECTED region at some `u`, the tiling would draw shadow across the gap. For a connected solid resting on the ground that cannot happen, and every recipe here is one, but nothing checks it. Adjacent triangles reach their shared edge through different float32 instance matrices, so a pixel on an internal edge can in principle be covered twice or not at all; the reconstruction error is about 4e-6 world units, under a thousandth of a pixel at the closest zoom, and the same hazard existed between the old three slabs. `evaluateChain` extrapolates outside its domain rather than clamping.

**Process note.** Done in a worktree (`.claude/worktrees/aoe2-07-shadow-shape`, branch `shadow-silhouette`) rather than the shared checkout, under the same-day one-writer-per-checkout rule. A peer session was mid-write in `github/aoe2`: its untracked `src/game/simulation/civProfiles.ts` has a string literal broken across a raw newline, which fails `npm run typecheck` there for anybody. Left untouched, confirmed with the peer that it is live work, and reported to the owner rather than repaired.

## 2026-09-04 — The civilizations panel (v0.3.201)

**Action.** Built §11.14, the compendium the owner asked for and had not got: all thirty civilizations compared side by side, in a match, from a menu button or `F4`. The spec section was written as part of the task — it did not exist.

**Result.** `npm run verify` exit 0. Thirty rows, one screen: army type, unique unit and team bonus each, a row expanding in place for expansion / unique technologies / full bonus list, the player's own civilization marked, and a search that reads every line including the bonuses.

**The design decision worth recording.** The obvious shape is a picker — choose a civilization, read its page. That makes the player hold the other twenty-nine in their head, which is the thing they opened the panel to avoid. The question is a comparison, so the default view is all of them and the detail is the expansion.

**What was believed and proved false, by LOOKING.** Two defects came out of the captures rather than out of a test, and both are the kind a passing spec never sees:

- At 800x600 the responsive rule stacked every cell, so a row was four lines tall and **four civilizations fitted on the screen** — a panel you open to compare thirty, showing four. It now folds to two lines (name+army, unique+team) and shows eight or nine.
- An expanded row's detail had no tint of its own, so it floated between its own row and the next one and which civilization it belonged to was a guess. The open row and its detail now share one raised block.

**A gate that earned its place.** The file-size budget went red at `createApp.ts` 506 lines against the 500 ceiling — twelve lines of wiring did it. Both look-it-up panels (technology tree, compendium) moved into `referencePanels.ts`, which is the split the rule was asking for rather than a workaround: they are one role and share every rule that governs it. `createApp.ts` is 491.

**The instrument had a hole, and it is closed.** `scripts/captureMapScreenshot.mjs` could not open a HUD panel, so every modal in this game had to be photographed by a one-off script — and a one-off has neither the dist-freshness check nor the served-bundle check that are the whole reason a capture can be trusted. It now takes `HOTKEY="F4"`, a comma-separated key sequence pressed before the shot. `HOTKEY="F4,Tab,Tab,Enter"` also proved the keyboard path to a row, and `HOTKEY="F4,v,i,e,t"` the search.

**The join is the fragile part, so it is gated in both directions.** The panel merges two tables that share nothing but a display name: `civProfiles` carries the CSV's design text ("Jaguar Warrior"), the runtime carries `jaguar-warrior`. A formatter change would mark every unique unit "not in the game" and the panel would still render, cheerfully wrong. `civCompendiumModel.test.ts` asserts the mapping both ways — every design-named unit live iff the simulation trains it, AND nothing the simulation trains missing from the design text — and pins that at least one civilization is complete and one is not, so the flag cannot be carrying zero information.

**Numbers.** 18 civilizations complete, 12 partial: the runtime `uniqueUnits` table has no entry at all for the twelve expansion civilizations, and their unique technologies are partly there (Berbers hold Kasbah but not Maghrabi Camels). That is now visible to a player instead of only to `content:validate`.

**Notes.** The panel repaints only its LIST on a keystroke, not the whole panel, or the search box loses focus mid-word. F1 is the technology tree, F2 the debug overlay, F3 the DE pause key, F4 this.
