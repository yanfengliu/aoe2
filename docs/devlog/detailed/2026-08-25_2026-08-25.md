## 2026-08-29 — Two hypotheses about the AI's slow Dark Age, both wrong

**Action.** The pacing retunes left a real game-feel gap: the AI reaches Feudal at tick 10,100 (16m50s) where DE's Standard AI takes about 11-13 minutes. Went looking for the lever. Found two candidates, measured both, and neither survived — which is worth more than the guesses were.

**Hypothesis 1: the Dark-Age villager cap is too low.** `villagerCapForAge` returns 10 for the Dark Age, against AoE2's familiar 22-pop Feudal, and the arithmetic looked decisive — a villager costs 50 food and gathers ~0.33/s, so it repays itself in ~150s against a Dark Age running past 1,000s. Raised the cap to 22 and measured: Feudal moved from tick 10,100 to **13,000**, and the second AI never got there inside 14,000 ticks. The reasoning was wrong in kind, not degree: aging up is a race to a THRESHOLD, not a total-economy optimisation. Every villager is 50 food not banked toward the 500, plus a house, plus another body competing for the same nearby resources. Reverted.

**Hypothesis 2: villagers waste the Dark Age walking.** An earlier probe on the feudal-stone fixture had shown 12 of 23 villagers hauling to drop-off and only 7 gathering, which suggested the AI was hauling everything home instead of planting camps at the resource. Measured properly on `default-seed`, sampling every 25 ticks across 10,000: **gathering 62%, walking out 22%, hauling back 13%, idle 0%**, stable from tick 2,500 onward. And the AI does plant its camps — town-center@48,24 with mining-camp@52,27, lumber-camp@52,25, mill@52,21. The earlier 30% reading was a jammed fixture at the moment of a door clot, not the normal case.

**What the numbers actually say.** The Dark Age is not pathological: nobody idles, the camps go up, and the gather share is reasonable. The Feudal time falls straight out of the two knobs that remain — ten villagers, split food 4 : wood 3 (57% on food) — against honest §6.3 rates. 500 food for the age-up plus ~350 spent growing 3 villagers into 10 is ~850 food at roughly 1.2 food/s of effective food income, which is ~10,600 ticks. The measured 10,100 is that arithmetic, not a defect.

**What that leaves.** Closing the gap to DE means more villagers AND a food-heavier early split TOGETHER — hypothesis 1 failed precisely because it moved one without the other, and the extra bodies inherited the same 57% food weight. That is a multi-variable tuning problem and it deserves a harness that sweeps cap and weights together and reports Feudal tick per combination, rather than another one-off probe. Recorded rather than attempted, because guessing at it twice already cost two runs.

## 2026-08-29 — v0.3.163 and its correction in v0.3.165: the fix that made the bug worse

**The missing entry, written late.** v0.3.163 shipped inside the v0.3.164 commit with no devlog section of its own — a canon miss, recorded here with its correction rather than backdated.

**What v0.3.163 claimed.** The boot-map freeze was root-caused to `GATHER_APPROACH_TIMEOUT_TICKS = 80`, the fan-out timeout for a villager walking to an over-subscribed resource. That constant was written when a unit moved 0.5 tiles/tick, so 80 ticks bought ~40 tiles; the §12.4.2 clock made it 6.4 tiles. Owner 2's food sat a median 7 tiles away, so it abandoned targets just before arriving. The fix made the budget a function of distance.

**What review found, and it is worse than a miss.** The budget was recomputed from the villager's CURRENT position every tick, while `gatherProgressTicks` accumulated from the start. That inverts the rule: the budget shrinks as the villager closes, so it fires at ~56% of ANY walk. Below 11.5 tiles it gives LESS reach than the flat 80 it replaced — 3.92 tiles into a 7-tile walk against the old 6.4. **I halved the reach of the mechanism I said I was lengthening**, and the docstring described a non-shrinking rule that the code did not implement. I verified the arithmetic myself before accepting it.

Three more defects fell out of the same per-tick distance: it used Euclidean `hypot` while movement steps ONE AXIS per tick (travel is MANHATTAN, so the budget was short beyond ~18° off-axis); it collapsed to the 40-tick floor for a villager standing on the destination cell, which happened 744 times in one 24,000-tick run; and past 38 tiles it exceeded the 600-tick unreachable backstop, inverting an ordering that file documents. **And the test certified the false property**: it asserted `budget > distance × 12.5` using the Euclidean model, so it passed while the property did not hold. A test that models the mechanism wrongly is worse than no test — it converts an unknown into a false assurance.

**The correction.** The budget is flat again, but expressed in TILES (32 → 400 ticks) so it still tracks the movement clock — which was the one virtue of the distance version worth keeping. Measured on three seeds, it beats both predecessors: no frozen player anywhere, and `default-seed` owner 1 back to 12,100 from the 15,800 my own fix had caused. The review's flat-400 table reproduced to the tick when I ran it.

**What I should have caught myself.** I flagged this exact risk when commissioning the review — "the budget shrinks as the villager approaches... that would be a new bug shaped like the old one" — and shipped anyway rather than spending ten lines of arithmetic on it. Naming a risk is not testing it.

**Framing corrected too.** The register now says a constant was retuned and re-measured, not that one root cause explains every outcome: disabling the fan-out branch entirely ALSO cures the prototype freeze, and the metric is non-monotone in the budget on `corpus-seed-b`. And one measured claim was simply wrong — `corpus-seed-b` owner 2 reached Feudal at 20,300 under the old constant, not "never", so the tally was one freeze plus one slowdown, not two freezes.
