# 2026-08-17

## Moebius art style, resolved in the engine rather than reimplemented here (v0.3.10)

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-17

**Action:** Migrated AoE2's world canvas to a Moebius art style — ink contours over flat, vibrant colour — by adding a generic `StylizedResolvePass` to the sibling `voxel` package and consuming it here. Painted (the previous look) stays selectable from the in-game menu.

**Prompt:** "Migrate aoe2 to moebius art style", then mid-task "And I don't know if voxel can be used to generalize the art style to be an option for any game made with it in the future", then "Needs brighter more vibrant colors".

### The placement decision

The user's second message was an open question about placement, not a direction, so it was put back to them with the investigation attached: the pass is scene-agnostic (depth, normals, luminance only), `ThreeRenderRuntime` has exactly one `renderer.render(scene, camera)` call at what was `ThreeRenderRuntime.ts:991`, both engines are orthographic (which the depth gradient assumes), and `voxel` has three consumers — `aoe2`, `city`, `3d-maker`. They chose to put it in `voxel`. Recorded in `docs/architecture/decisions.md`.

Two consequences worth keeping in mind. An engine update can now change how this game looks, which is why every tuned constant in `src/rendering/artStyles.ts` carries the frame measurement it was chosen against. And the standing rule that AoE owns every palette, recipe, role, fog cue, and animation meaning is intact: the pass is given no scene knowledge at all.

### What did not come across from the source implementation

`townscaper` bands its sea over a measured `WATER_TONE_RANGE` of 0.55..0.88, reached in the shader by `step(d, 0.9999)` — pixels where nothing wrote depth. That works because *its* water is drawn with `depthWrite` off. AoE's terrain is voxel geometry that writes depth everywhere, so the branch would never fire here, and shipping the constant into a shared library would have published one game's measurement as a property of the technique. It generalizes as a background lane defaulting to the scene lane.

### What was believed and proved false

The first render came out visibly dark and green — mean world-canvas luminance 69.8 against a Painted baseline of 90.1, a 23% loss. The obvious suspect was the linear/sRGB round trip through the offscreen target, since the pass does its own `toDisplay` encode and either a double encode or a missing one would darken the frame.

That was wrong, and one capture settled it. Configuring the pass as a pass-through (`flatten: 0, inkStrength: 0`) reproduced the baseline at **L=90.2 against 90.1** — the colour pipeline is faithful, and the entire problem was tuning. Worth repeating as a method: set a whole-frame effect to identity and compare against the baseline before diagnosing anything, because "the pipeline is broken" and "the numbers are wrong" need completely different work.

The actual cause was `normalEdgeScale: 2.4` inking every cube edge. A normal break is a crease inside one form; voxel geometry has none, because every face junction is exactly 90 degrees and the turn between neighbouring normals is exactly 1.0 everywhere. Any scale at or above 1 inks all of them equally. Recorded as a lesson.

### What the second round of feedback exposed

With contours right, the frame sat at L=86.8 — still *below* the Painted baseline, and the user said it needed brighter, more vibrant colour. This was not reachable by tuning: the pass held hue exactly and moved only luminance, so chroma could not be raised by any setting it had. Flat colour in this idiom is *saturated* colour, and quantising tone does nothing to saturation — the same gap `townscaper` had already recorded as its biggest remaining one.

`saturation` and `brightness` were added to the pass in `voxel`, both gamut-safe and hue-preserving. The saturation boost is pulled back to the largest factor that still fits the gamut rather than letting channels clip one at a time, which is what would otherwise turn a chroma boost into a hue shift.

### Verification

Before, after, and diff artifacts under `docs/devlog/artifacts/2026-04-23-default-map-moebius-{before,after,diff}.png`. The diff reports 69.30% of the frame changed, which is correct for a whole-frame art style — the meaningful confinement check is that the DOM HUD is untouched, and it is: FOOD and AGE panel interiors 6 of ~3,500 pixels at a worst channel-sum delta of 3 (PNG noise, ≤1 per channel), selection panel exactly 0, minimap frame 16 pixels at delta ≤3 from its own live redraw. Gates: aoe2 unit tests, typecheck, lint, build, browser tests, and both audits. `voxel` ran its own full suite plus public-API, consumer-compatibility, three-package, core-only-package, and supply-chain verification before this repo consumed it.

### What the review caught

Escalated to multi-CLI review as a sibling-repo change; synthesis in `docs/threads/current/moebius-art-style/2026-08-17/1/REVIEW.md`. **Codex was out of quota until Aug 19**, so a second Claude instance carried its GPU-lifecycle lens and the change has had no independent second-engine read — retry next session.

Most of the seventeen findings were in `voxel` and are recorded there. What landed in this repo:

- **The ink was being blended in the wrong colour space**, drawing `#2b3a45` at roughly `#0a0e12`. That means this game's look was tuned against an accidentally darker line than the one it asked for. Fixing it in `voxel` moved the shipped frame from L=96.9 to **L=100.8**. Re-inspected at 3x on the Town Center before accepting: contours are still one crisp pixel, so `inkStrength` stays at the preset's 0.85 rather than being raised to chase the old line. The provenance block in `artStyles.ts` records both numbers and why they differ.
- **`setArtStyle` recorded the new id before the swap that can throw.** The voxel side deliberately keeps the old pass drawing when a swap is refused, so assigning first left `artStyleId()` and the menu label naming a style the canvas was not in — and the next cycle would advance from that wrong base and skip a style. The assignment happens after the runtime accepts now.
- **The menu row was entirely untested.** `tests/ui/gameMenuArtStyle.test.ts` now covers the initial label coming from the persisted preference rather than the markup default, click-to-cycle updating both the visible state and the `aria-label`, holding the label when a cycle reports nothing, and the listener coming off on destroy. The initial-label test was confirmed to fail with that render neutralized.
- **Two weak assertions**, both replaced: `gameMenuIcons.test.ts` asserted "every entry but the last is text-free", which silently encoded "debug is the only toggle" and broke the moment a second toggle landed below it — it now keys on a declared `state` per action; and a preference test repeated its predecessor's assertion with a different id while claiming application semantics.

### Notes

- Painted is the *absence* of the pass. `artStyleById('painted').resolve` is `null` and the runtime option is omitted, so the previous look costs exactly what it did before — no second scene render, no fullscreen resolve, no offscreen targets. Pinned by `tests/rendering/AoeVoxelWorldRenderer.test.ts > asks for no pass at all in Painted`.
- Switching style calls `ThreeRenderRuntime.setStylizedResolve`, which rebuilds only the resolve pass. No voxel content is re-uploaded; pinned by asserting `acceptSnapshot` is not called across a switch.
- The style is a display preference in `localStorage`, never in the save format. Storage that throws (private browsing, disabled origin, exhausted quota) must not stop the game booting or refuse an in-session switch; pinned by `tests/rendering/artStyles.test.ts > survives storage that throws on read or write`.

### What the browser gate caught that nothing else did

Adding one row to the game menu broke a test that never mentions art styles. Two distinct causes, both worth recording because neither is visible from the unit tests:

- **A focus-trap test hardcoded the last button.** It tabbed to the end and asserted `menu-debug-cycle` was focused, which encoded "debug is the last row" rather than the rule it meant — that the trap wraps at both ends. Now derived from the action list, so the next row added does not read as a focus regression.
- **A real layout regression.** `.hud-game-menu__panel` had no height ceiling and no overflow, so it simply grew. One extra row was enough that at 390x560 the load form's Cancel button sat below the bottom of the viewport, where Playwright correctly refused to click it — and where a player could neither see nor reach it, leaving Escape as the only exit. The panel is capped at `calc(100vh - 32px)` and scrolls. This was latent before the change and would have bitten the next row whoever added it.

The unit suite passed throughout both. Only the browser gate, driving the real app at a real viewport, saw either.

### Follow-ups

- Tuning was verified at the default camera zoom only. The depth threshold is world-space so it does not drift with zoom, but line *density* relative to content does, and no capture exercised a second zoom or a second seed.
- The pass's banding maths exists twice, hand-transliterated between TypeScript and GLSL. `voxel` pins the uniform *interface* on both sides but no node gate executes the GLSL, so divergence in the function bodies is currently unguarded. Recorded as a follow-up in `voxel`'s devlog.
- Per-style palettes and tapered line weight remain deferred, per the spec bullet in §14.5.
