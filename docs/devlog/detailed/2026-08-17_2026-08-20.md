## The AI was never stuck on gathering — it was stuck in traffic (v0.3.30)

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-20

**Action:** Found and fixed five defects that together had the AI's economy dead from about tick 12000 of every match on the default map: three in movement traffic arbitration, one in spawn placement, one in gather assignment. Added a home range to auto-assignment, a resource-kind fallback for a villager with no workable target, and a stagger on the reachability probe.

**What was believed and proved false.** The previous session left a note in `dropOffStep.ts` saying three attempts there had failed and that the next attempt should start from slot allocation, because four wedged carriers in cell (52,23) were observed sharing one identical fine transform (210, 92) when units are supposed to hold distinct sub-cell slots. That inference is wrong, and the note has been corrected in place. Slot allocation was fine — a later dump showed every stuck villager holding a distinct slot (`[0.25,0]`, `[0.5,0.75]`, `[0.75,0.75]`, …). What made four carriers look identical is simply that none of them had moved, and units that do not move accumulate at whatever transform they were last given. **A "these values are suspiciously equal" observation is not evidence of a shared allocator; it is equally consistent with nothing happening at all.**

**The instrument that settled it.** One run of a per-unit counter around the traffic wrapper — `wait` / `moved` / `moveNull`, plus the attempted leg — answered in 300 ticks what three code-reading attempts had missed: all 18 villagers, `wait` 300/300, `moved` 0. Adding a label per wait rule then named the rule (17 of 18 on `occupiedLane`, 1 on `crossFlow`). Dumping the blocking graph — who is stepping where, and who occupies that cell — showed three independent standoffs and made all three defects readable at once. **Count the decision, not the state it leaves behind.**

**The three traffic defects.** All in `movementTrafficOps.ts`, all only reachable inside a one-cell-wide corridor (`narrowLaneAxis`), which is what the gaps between an AI's own buildings are — an open-field repro never enters these rules at all, and my first synthetic test passed vacuously for exactly that reason.

1. `closeDependencies` returned null when it reached a peer already in `visited`. With several units sharing a cell, every member's walk reaches a peer it has already seen, so no member could close the set and none was admitted — `cyc` was null for all six units in the (51,23)↔(52,23) standoff. Skipping a visited peer instead of aborting lets every member compute the same closed set and agree on the same winner.
2. A unit yielded to any lower-id co-located peer with a different heading, whether or not that peer could move. Villager 2237 stood in (40,23) with an EMPTY cell ahead of it and waited on 2161, which was itself permanently blocked. Yielding now requires the peer to be able to take the turn it is being given.
3. **The one that mattered most, and that the first two fixes exposed rather than caused:** once the closure elected a winner, execution fell through to the co-located rules, which re-judged that winner by a local criterion and vetoed it. `cyc:2256-2206-2277-2278` elects 2206; 2206 is then refused `sameFlow` because 2278 stands further along the shared heading — and 2278 is waiting on the election 2206 just won. Circular refusal, 200 ticks out of 200. The election is the only rule that sees the whole jam and it names exactly one winner, so it now returns `proceed` directly.

**The sealed pocket.** Rendering the base as ASCII (buildings, resources, unit counts per cell) rather than reading placement code: the AI had closed a two-cell pocket at (48,23)–(49,23) between a house at (47,21), the mill at (46,23), the barracks at (49,20), a farm at (50,23) and its own Town Center at (48,24). (48,23) is on the Town Center's spawn perimeter, so every villager trained after that was placed into the pocket — seven of eleven units. Each passed the old egress rule, "at least one cardinal neighbour is passable", because the pocket's other cell IS a passable neighbour. `hasSpawnEgress` now walks the free region and requires 8 cells, stopping there, so the cost is a couple of dozen cells per candidate.

**What was tried and reverted, with numbers.** A traffic starvation guard — let a mover through after 60 consecutive refusals — measured 7 villagers and gold frozen at 470 against a baseline of 18 and 2418, and was reverted. It also pinned a wandering scout, and the bisect for that is worth keeping: storing the wait counter on `unitTransform` was itself the cause, because the arbiter writes that component during arbitration and a component write disturbs the per-tick snapshot it reads. The counter belonged in a closure map beside `attemptedDirections` and `originReservations`. A placement clearance rule — require a walkable margin around every new building — was also reverted: it took the economy gates from one red to two and stopped the AI building farms entirely.

**Verify the instrument.** `bridge.step(n)` takes MILLISECONDS, not ticks. A timeline probe written as `bridge.step(1000)` per window advanced the world by ten ticks a window and reported a healthy economy out to "tick 300" — the run looked fine and meant nothing. Every measurement here is gated on `getDebugSnapshot().tick` reaching a target instead. The baseline itself was also re-measured on clean HEAD with the identical probe before any comparison was drawn, which is what made the reverts unambiguous.

**A performance finding that came free.** The `requireReachable` recovery branch pays bounded pathfinding and was running for every stuck villager on every tick. Staggering it by id (retry every 8 ticks) took 9000 ticks from 91s on clean main to 47s — the simulation is about twice as fast as before this session even though far more units are now actually moving. The spike was invisible while the economy was frozen, because frozen villagers never entered that branch.

**Result:** measured on the same seed at tick 30000 — villagers 20 (frozen, zero gather income; the gold came from market selling) → 21 working; food 139 → 1010; total units 20 → 25; gold 2418 → 2377. Gates: 2430 unit / 2 skipped, typecheck, lint, build.

**Notes / what is still wrong.** Wood income stays near zero and the economy stalls again around tick 15000, so the AI still does not reach Castle Age. That is the next thread, and it is now a narrow one: at tick 20000 the wood villagers hold live targets and full loads, so it is another delivery-side stall rather than an assignment problem. The `to-dropoff` leg still has no give-up path of its own.


## Moebius art style, resolved in the engine rather than reimplemented here (v0.3.10)

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-17

**Action:** Migrated AoE2's world canvas to a Moebius art style — ink contours over flat, vibrant colour — by adding a generic `StylizedResolvePass` to the sibling `voxel` package and consuming it here. Painted (the previous look) stays selectable from the in-game menu.

**Prompt:** "Migrate aoe2 to moebius art style", then mid-task "And I don't know if voxel can be used to generalize the art style to be an option for any game made with it in the future", then "Needs brighter more vibrant colors".

### The placement decision

The user's second message was an open question about placement, not a direction, so it was put back to them with the investigation attached: the pass is scene-agnostic (depth, normals, luminance only), `ThreeRenderRuntime` has exactly one `renderer.render(scene, camera)` call at what was `ThreeRenderRuntime.ts:991`, both engines are orthographic (which the depth gradient assumes), and `voxel` has three consumers — `aoe2`, `city`, `3d-maker`. They chose to put it in `voxel`. Recorded in `docs/architecture/decisions.md`.

Two consequences worth keeping in mind. An engine update can now change how this game looks, which is why every tuned constant in `src/rendering/artStyles.ts` carries the frame measurement it was chosen against. And the standing rule that AoE owns every palette, recipe, role, fog cue, and animation meaning is intact: the pass is given no scene knowledge at all.

### What did not come across from the source implementation

`townscaper` bands its sea over a measured `WATER_TONE_RANGE` of 0.55..0.88, reached in the shader by `step(d, 0.9999)` — pixels where nothing wrote depth. That works because *its* water is drawn with `depthWrite` off. AoE's terrain is voxel geometry that writes depth everywhere, so the branch would never fire here, and shipping the constant into a shared library would have published one game's measurement as a property of the technique. It generalizes as a background lane defaulting to the scene lane.

### What was believed and proved false

The first render came out visibly dark and green — mean world-canvas luminance 69.8 against a Painted baseline of 90.1, a 23% loss. The obvious suspect was the linear/sRGB round trip through the offscreen target, since the pass does its own `toDisplay` encode and either a double encode or a missing one would darken the frame.

That was wrong, and one capture settled it. Configuring the pass as a pass-through (`flatten: 0, inkStrength: 0`) reproduced the baseline at **L=90.2 against 90.1** — the colour pipeline is faithful, and the entire problem was tuning. Worth repeating as a method: set a whole-frame effect to identity and compare against the baseline before diagnosing anything, because "the pipeline is broken" and "the numbers are wrong" need completely different work.

The actual cause was `normalEdgeScale: 2.4` inking every cube edge. A normal break is a crease inside one form; voxel geometry has none, because every face junction is exactly 90 degrees and the turn between neighbouring normals is exactly 1.0 everywhere. Any scale at or above 1 inks all of them equally. Recorded as a lesson.

### What the second round of feedback exposed

With contours right, the frame sat at L=86.8 — still *below* the Painted baseline, and the user said it needed brighter, more vibrant colour. This was not reachable by tuning: the pass held hue exactly and moved only luminance, so chroma could not be raised by any setting it had. Flat colour in this idiom is *saturated* colour, and quantising tone does nothing to saturation — the same gap `townscaper` had already recorded as its biggest remaining one.

`saturation` and `brightness` were added to the pass in `voxel`, both gamut-safe and hue-preserving. The saturation boost is pulled back to the largest factor that still fits the gamut rather than letting channels clip one at a time, which is what would otherwise turn a chroma boost into a hue shift.

Gates: aoe2 unit tests, typecheck, lint, build, browser tests, and both audits. `voxel` ran its own full suite plus public-API, consumer-compatibility, three-package, core-only-package, and supply-chain verification before this repo consumed it.

### What the review caught

Escalated to multi-CLI review as a sibling-repo change; synthesis in `docs/threads/current/moebius-art-style/2026-08-17/1/REVIEW.md`. **Codex was out of quota until Aug 19**, so a second Claude instance carried its GPU-lifecycle lens and the change has had no independent second-engine read — retry next session.

Most of the seventeen findings were in `voxel` and are recorded there. What landed in this repo:

- **The ink was being blended in the wrong colour space**, drawing `#2b3a45` at roughly `#0a0e12`. That means this game's look was tuned against an accidentally darker line than the one it asked for. Fixing it in `voxel` moved the shipped frame from L=96.9 to **L=100.8**. Re-inspected at 3x on the Town Center before accepting: contours are still one crisp pixel, so `inkStrength` stays at the preset's 0.85 rather than being raised to chase the old line. The provenance block in `artStyles.ts` records both numbers and why they differ.
- **`setArtStyle` recorded the new id before the swap that can throw.** The voxel side deliberately keeps the old pass drawing when a swap is refused, so assigning first left `artStyleId()` and the menu label naming a style the canvas was not in — and the next cycle would advance from that wrong base and skip a style. The assignment happens after the runtime accepts now.
- **The menu row was entirely untested.** `tests/ui/gameMenuArtStyle.test.ts` now covers the initial label coming from the persisted preference rather than the markup default, click-to-cycle updating both the visible state and the `aria-label`, holding the label when a cycle reports nothing, and the listener coming off on destroy. The initial-label test was confirmed to fail with that render neutralized.
- **Two weak assertions**, both replaced: `gameMenuIcons.test.ts` asserted "every entry but the last is text-free", which silently encoded "debug is the only toggle" and broke the moment a second toggle landed below it — it now keys on a declared `state` per action; and a preference test repeated its predecessor's assertion with a different id while claiming application semantics.

### Notes

- Painted is the *absence* of the pass. `artStyleById('painted').resolve` is `null` and the runtime option is omitted, so the previous look costs exactly what it did before — no second scene render, no fullscreen resolve, no offscreen targets. Pinned by `tests/rendering/AoeVoxelWorldRenderer.test.ts > asks for no pass at all in Painted`.
- Switching style calls `ThreeRenderRuntime.setStylizedResolve`, which rebuilds only the resolve pass. No voxel content is re-uploaded; pinned by asserting `acceptSnapshot` is not called across a switch.
- The style is a display preference in `localStorage`, never in the save format. Storage that throws (private browsing, disabled origin, exhausted quota) must not stop the game booting or refuse an in-session switch; pinned by `tests/rendering/artStyles.test.ts > survives storage that throws on read or write`.

### What the browser gate caught that nothing else did

Adding one row to the game menu broke a test that never mentions art styles. Two distinct causes, both worth recording because neither is visible from the unit tests:

- **A focus-trap test hardcoded the last button.** It tabbed to the end and asserted `menu-debug-cycle` was focused, which encoded "debug is the last row" rather than the rule it meant — that the trap wraps at both ends. Now derived from the action list, so the next row added does not read as a focus regression.
- **A real layout regression.** `.hud-game-menu__panel` had no height ceiling and no overflow, so it simply grew. One extra row was enough that at 390x560 the load form's Cancel button sat below the bottom of the viewport, where Playwright correctly refused to click it — and where a player could neither see nor reach it, leaving Escape as the only exit. The panel is capped at `calc(100vh - 32px)` and scrolls. This was latent before the change and would have bitten the next row whoever added it.

The unit suite passed throughout both. Only the browser gate, driving the real app at a real viewport, saw either.

### Follow-ups

- Tuning was verified at the default camera zoom only. The depth threshold is world-space so it does not drift with zoom, but line *density* relative to content does, and no capture exercised a second zoom or a second seed.
- The pass's banding maths exists twice, hand-transliterated between TypeScript and GLSL. `voxel` pins the uniform *interface* on both sides but no node gate executes the GLSL, so divergence in the function bodies is currently unguarded. Recorded as a follow-up in `voxel`'s devlog.
- Per-style palettes and tapered line weight remain deferred, per the spec bullet in §14.5.


## Dependency audit gate back to green

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-17

**Action:** `npm audit fix` on five high-severity advisories that predated the art-style change. `package-lock.json` is the only file touched.

All five were dev-only transitives — `--omit=dev` already reported zero, so the shipped bundle was never exposed. Each reported `fixAvailable: true` as a plain boolean rather than an `isSemVerMajor` object, none was a direct dependency, and none listed `effects`, so every one was a leaf that could be bumped in place. A plain `npm audit fix` took all of them at patch or minor: brace-expansion 5.0.6→5.0.9 and 1.1.13→1.1.18, js-yaml 4.3.0→4.3.1, nanoid 3.3.12→3.3.18, postcss 8.5.15→8.5.26, undici 7.28.0→7.29.0. No `--force` was needed and nothing required a major bump, so there is nothing to report as unresolvable.

**Two lockfile entries moved that `audit fix` did not cause**, and a later session could easily read them as unrelated drift: this package went 0.3.9→0.3.10 and the linked `voxel` went 0.1.4→1.2.0. Both are the lockfile catching up with the previous commit, which bumped `package.json` and the sibling package without re-running an install.

### Why the full gate set, and what it showed

postcss and nanoid sit in the Vite build path; js-yaml and brace-expansion sit under eslint. A toolchain bump there can move lint results or build output without any source change, which is why the whole set ran rather than the audit alone: 2190 unit tests, typecheck, lint, build, 111 browser tests, content validation — all green, both audit forms now zero.

**The built JS hash is byte-identical across the bump** (`index-INsJFyIR.js` before and after), which is the strongest single piece of evidence that the bump is inert.

### A false alarm worth recording

The rendered frame was re-captured because postcss processes the CSS pipeline. It came back with 610 of 480,000 pixels differing from the committed baseline, worst channel-sum delta 386 — which looks alarming until it is controlled for.

Two captures of the *same* build differ by 84–522 pixels. Across the bump the figure is 610–796. Same order, overlapping ranges, and the differing pixels sit almost entirely in the world canvas, which is animated (water crests, ambient unit motion, a smoke plume). Combined with the byte-identical JS bundle — and the fact that the world canvas is WebGL drawn entirely by that JS, which CSS cannot reach — the differences are animation phase, not the dependency bump.

The lesson for the next person to re-capture this scene: **it is not deterministic frame to frame**, so a pixel diff against a stored baseline needs a same-build control run before any number from it means anything. The HUD-interior check in the art-style entry above is the stable comparison; the world canvas is not.

# 2026-08-17 — animated shoreline surf and the ground-decoration variant library (v0.3.11)

**Request (user, verbatim complaints):** the water/land boundary "straight light blue lines do not look natural… if it is supposed to be the wave then it should be moving and fading, also it should not be so straight"; and grass/small-stone decorations "need to be more diverse and randomized… generate a library of these things and pull from them, or have a procedural generation algo."

**Action:** replaced the per-edge static foam bar in `createTerrainDetailParts` with up to three animated surf segments per visible land-touching water edge, and rebuilt land decoration on small procedural variant libraries. Split `aoeVoxelTerrain.ts` (329 LOC) into orchestration (179) + `aoeVoxelTerrainWaterDetail.ts` (183) + `aoeVoxelTerrainDecor.ts` (286), all under the 500 cap.

**Surf mechanics:** each segment hashes its along-edge slot (0.25/0.5/0.75 ± 0.08), width (0.14–0.26), inshore distance (0.13–0.20), yaw (±0.09), and a 22% skip roll — so the line is broken and never straight. Animation: shared 2,800 ms period, translation 0.05 perpendicular toward the land, scale amplitude 0.85 on the inshore axis and 0.8 on height (trough collapses the segment to a sliver ≈ the lap dissolving), and phase = along-coast world coordinate × 1.15 + jitter, so the lap sweeps down the coast instead of blinking in unison. A per-segment period would drift the sweep apart, which is why the period is shared — the phase carries the spatial variation.

**Two constraint systems shaped the amplitudes.** (1) The existing containment test samples 65 poses per animated part and requires every corner inside the source cell: inshore centre ≥ 0.13 is the budget for half-depth×1.85 + 0.05 travel + yaw reach ≈ 0.127. (2) The browser water spec pins `animatedBatches: 1` — surf initially shipped as animated `matte`, which silently opens a second animated batch lane; it now rides the `water` surface so crests and surf share the one animated lane (and foam gets the wet low-roughness sheen). The water-band y-check (0.005 < y < 0.12) then applies: surf spans 0.0098–0.042 across all phases.

**Decor libraries** (`aoeVoxelTerrainDecor.ts`): 5 tuft cluster shapes (pair/fan/ring/tall-sentinel/low-patch) × per-blade height-lean-colour jitter from a 5-colour palette; flecks with scattered placement, 0.16–0.34 width, light/dark tint split; pebble scatters (1–4 squashed stones, 3 greys × shade jitter) at 10% on grass, 12% on hills; 4 hill stone formations (boulder+shard/twins/slabs/cairn — the cairn stacks its second stone on the first's top) with hashed anchor, formation yaw, and scale. All streams are `hash01(x, z, salt)` with disjoint salts — same cell, same decoration, forever; the RED suite asserts variant counts, scatter, and per-tile part bounds (≤ 12).

**TDD:** 8 RED assertions (segment irregularity, perpendicular travel per edge orientation, ≥ 0.7 fade amplitudes, phase spread, variant/scatter/tint diversity for tufts–flecks–pebbles–rocks, determinism + per-tile budget) → green without weakening any existing contract. Two density guards were deliberately rescaled with comments: the adversarial checkerboard part bound 100 → 165 (isolated water cells now carry up to 3 segments × 4 edges) and the showcase instance bound 2,000 → 6,000 (measured 3,676; draw calls ≤ 20 and triangles < 100k stay the real cost guards and passed untouched).

**Evidence:** maintained pair on the REAL default view (`aoe2-prototype`, fog on): `2026-04-23-default-map-shore-decor-{before,after,diff}.png` — 19,017/480,000 px (3.96%), confined to the map band (HUD/minimap/panels zero). Motion looked at directly: three live frames 700 ms apart (quarter surf period) show fat foam blobs → slivers → re-swelling, desynchronised along the coast; ~1,530/56,000 px move per step inside a 400×140 water crop. Browser water spec re-proves frozen-time byte-identical frames and advancing-time change with surf present.

**Gates:** 2,198 unit / 2 skips (292 files), typecheck, zero-warning lint, build, full browser suite (see summary line for count), no dependency change (audit gate not re-triggered).

**Watch out:** `diffMapScreenshots.mjs` takes LABEL as a STEM env var and ignores CLI args — invoking it with three path arguments silently diffs the bare tracked `before/after` baselines and reports a nonsense percentage (12.32% here) while writing to the tracked bare `diff.png`. The capture/diff LABEL conventions differ (full-suffix vs stem); re-read the script header before invoking.

**Review:** no CLI reviewer fan-out this session (session policy: no subagents/workflows without user request; change is not in a multi-cli-review high-risk class). # 2026-08-17 — connected surf curve, terrain colour fields, berry-bush forms (v0.3.12)

**Request (user, live iteration on v0.3.11):** "The shoreline still looks discontinued. Why can't it have curves and be connected? Is this a limitation of the voxel engine?" — then, mid-task: grass patches "should have slightly different colors", "Same with water cells", "Where the colors meet should not be awkward or clearcut", and "Berry bush also needs to look diverse."

**Answer to the engine question, recorded:** foam connectedness was aoe2's own design debt (independent per-edge hashes + skip rolls), not an engine limit — fixed render-side. The genuinely engine-level part is the cell-resolution colour seam (per-cell voxel columns; no sub-cell tint or shore meshing), now an explicit low-priority ask in `docs/engine-feedback/current.md`; the same audit moved two long-resolved June items to `past.md`.

**Surf curve (`aoeVoxelTerrainWaterDetail.ts` rewrite):** per shoreline edge, one six-box chain sampled from a quadratic through a hashed mid offset, boxes overlapping (length = chord × 1.3) and yawed to the local tangent. Cross-tile continuity comes from hashing terminal offsets by the shared CORNER's integer coordinates — both tiles compute the same anchor. Corner classification per chain end: side-cell land → concave (endpoint pulled in along-edge too, so the two perpendicular chains meet at the shared diagonal point); diagonal land → straight run (shared corner hash); both water → convex headland (offset pinches to 0.04). A minimum bend (|om − avg| ≥ 0.035) keeps hash-degenerate edges from flattening; terminal flattening (window 0.45) makes adjoining chains agree within a box thickness. Animation: shared 2,800 ms period, phase from the along-coast world coordinate, translation amplitude tapered by sin^1.6(u) so chain ends are pinned (adjacent chains animate on different phase axes — untapered they would tear at joints), and the fade collapses thickness (0.62) and height (0.55) while length breathes only 0.1 — the line thins to a hairline but cannot break.

**Three first-cut defects, all caught by contracts:** (1) wander could collapse to 0.017 on hash-degenerate edges → minimum-bend rule; (2) convex terminals stopped 0.15 from the corner → thickness taper toward chain ends (0.55 + 0.45·sin) + wider flatten + 0.04 pinch offset; (3) the 65-pose containment sweep caught terminal box TIPS overhanging the cell along-edge (overlap factor + breathing) and an unbudgeted travel term — fixed by an along-edge centre clamp and adding travel to the outward-lift reach. The debugging step that unlocked (3): dumping the actual escaping part keys showed `-0` and `-5` (terminal) boxes on the along axis, not the inshore axis the first fix assumed.

**Colour fields (`aoeVoxelTerrainColor.ts`, new):** two-octave bilinear value noise (scales 7.3/3.1) drives per-kind patch fields; grass slides between cool 0x486e3c and warm 0x7d9a4b poles, water between 0x2b5a6d and 0x4f97a8. **The overlays suite caught a real fog bug in the first cut:** absolute accent mixing brightened near-black fog-hidden cells (the near-black palette-entry assertion failed). Fix: accents are luma-MATCHED to the projected base tint — patch fields shift hue only, a separate shade term carries brightness — so fog darkness survives the pipeline by construction. Shallow water: ring-1 land (diagonals included, so the band wraps corners) lightens toward turquoise at full strength, ring-2 at half. Seams: land cells pull 16–24% toward differing land neighbours' colours; water-adjacent land warms toward wet sand 0x9b8a5e. Per-cell grain reduced to ±1.6% so patches read as patches (the old ±5.4% bucket noise was per-cell confetti that drowned the field at adjacent range — measured adjacent/far brightness-delta ratio 7.07/8.4 before, passing < 0.75 after). Palette carries full-precision entries (quantisation capped uniques at 36 < the 40 contract; no engine palette-entry limit exists — verified in voxel core contracts).

**Berry bushes (`aoeVoxelResourceRecipes.ts`):** three forms (twin-mound ≈ the old silhouette, low sprawl, upright dome) × three foliage palettes × 3–5 berry clusters with hashed ring placement, size, and a ripeness mix (entity accent tint vs deep berry red), on the existing `vary()`/`treeVariant()` position-seeded machinery. Spec §14.5's "part KEY SET is unchanged" clause was qualified: berry forms differ in part set BY DESIGN, but each instance's set is a pure position function, so hit/selection/fog-memory stay synchronized.

**Guards rescaled with reasons:** checkerboard part bound 165 → 220 (two water columns, every cell two 6-box chains; ~3 parts/cell), showcase animated-instance bound stays 700 (measured ~380), total instances bound 6,000 unchanged (draws ≤ 20 and triangles < 100k passed untouched as the true cost guards).

**Evidence:** `2026-04-23-default-map-terrain-color-surf-{before,after,diff}.png` — 188,807/480,000 px (39.33%), and the diff image shows the delta is EXACTLY the ground plane: buildings, units, HUD, minimap grey. Motion strip (three live frames 700 ms apart): one continuous curving line per coast, closed rounded loop around the pond, swelling fat → hairline → re-swelling with corner joints pinned. Berry before/after crop on the real default view shows the low-sprawl form replacing the fixed stamp. **Full-frame sweep (new canon rule):** nothing else moved — amber mounds initially mistaken for berry bushes turned out to be a different resource (berry tint is 0x7a4c8e purple per `prototypeEconomyRules.ts`); locating the real bushes via `STARTING_BERRIES` offsets settled it.

**Design decisions.**
- *Stateless rolls.* The repo had NO runtime RNG. Caught by `siegeWorkshop.test.ts` failing with "expected 45 to be 40" — investigated by instrumenting `resolveOne` (proving it ran exactly once) rather than guessing at double-application. 2. **Blast tests were measuring the brawl.** With ~12 ticks of flight, the fixture's units fight and move, so absolute-HP assertions no longer isolated the blast. Rewritten to snapshot positions/HP on the tick the shot leaves the air and assert the delta by distance from the impact point.

## 2026-08-19 - Patrol (v0.3.24)

**Action.** Added patrol, closing all of M6's unit control except formations.

**The design decision the whole slice turns on.** A patrol is NOT a `UnitCommand`. Auto-aggression takes a unit off its walk to fight whatever it meets, and that clears the command — which is why an ATTACK-MOVE is finished the first time it works, a limitation that shipped with it in v0.3.19. A patrol issued the same way would inherit that. Modelling the route as its own per-unit state and re-issuing the leg whenever the unit falls idle makes the behaviour right for free: `patrolSystem` runs after `prototypePlayerCommands`, so an arrival and a finished fight look identical from there. The route survives its own legs because the system re-issues through `resumePatrolLeg`, which passes `keepPatrol` to the shared walk helper; every other path clears it, so any PLAYER order ends the patrol without anything having to enumerate the orders that should.

**Turnaround is conditional, not automatic.** The unit reverses only when it has actually reached the end it was walking toward. A unit that stopped anywhere else - a fight, a blocked step - resumes toward the SAME end. Reversing on every idle tick would make a unit interrupted mid-leg ping-pong around the interruption point.

**Verified through the real browser interaction.** Attack-move is the cautionary case: four green simulation tests while every real click threw `Grid coordinates must be integers`. So patrol has a spec that presses the actual `p`, clicks the actual canvas, captures `pageerror`, and asserts the unit LEAVES and then COMES BACK - leaving alone would also be true of an attack-move, so the return is the part that proves it is a patrol. Clicking at 35%/38% rather than the 60%/55% that lands on the selection panel. Mutation-tested by deleting the P hotkey registration: fails with "the click produced no movement at all".

**Arming is one field.** `ArmedGroundOrder` holds `'attack-move' | 'patrol' | null`. With a boolean per order type, pressing A and then P leaves both set and the click has to arbitrate between them.

**Four splits for the budget**, each a real concept: `patrolCommandOps` (the one order family that keeps state after its walk), `gatherCommandOps` (the one that writes `GathererComponent` rather than a command), `armedGroundOrder`, and `voxelGameViewHelpers` (pure functions that were already free-standing at the bottom of the view).

## 2026-08-20 - The AI's economy was dead (v0.3.29)

**Action.** Set out to give the AI siege production and its civilization's unique unit — it had been building a Siege Workshop and a Castle for many versions and training nothing from either. The change itself was small. Verifying that it would ever FIRE in a real match turned into the actual work.

**The measurement that reframed the task.** A throwaway probe ran the default map for 9000 ticks and printed the AI's units, buildings, and stockpile. Then 26000 ticks. The two were IDENTICAL: food 518, wood 34, gold 513, ten villagers, seven buildings, no change across seventeen thousand ticks. The AI reaches Feudal Age and then stops existing as an opponent.

**Root cause, found by tracing one villager rather than by reading the assignment code.** Villager 2255 sat at (52,23) with `task: 'to-resource'`, `targetResourceId` pointing at a FISH at (56,16), and `gatherProgressTicks: 4800`. A fish is FOOD — the kind filter in `assignNearestResource` matched it exactly as it would a berry bush — and a villager is a LAND unit that can never enter a water cell. It walked to the shoreline and stood there for the rest of the match. Every food villager did the same thing.

**Two more from the same trace.** The AI's build list has no FARM in it at all, so once the finite berries and sheep near its base are eaten its food income is permanently zero and the 800 food for Castle Age is unreachable. And the give-up path for an unreachable target is gated on `overSubscribed` — the comment above it says "A long walk to an UNcontended resource is NOT abandoned", which is exactly right for a distant tree and exactly wrong for one the villager can never reach.

**Before and after, same seed, same map.** Villagers 10 falling to 4 -> 18. Gold frozen at 513 -> climbing past 2400. Farms 0 -> 3. Buildings 7 -> 10.

**A fix I made, measured, and reverted.** Marking a villager stuck when its step does not LAND (rather than only when no plan can be found) reads as obviously correct: a plan existing is not the same as the step succeeding. It made the AI strictly WORSE — it fell from Feudal back to Dark Age. Transient blocking is constant in a crowd, and one refused step latches the 30-tick `GATHER_DROPOFF_RETRY_INTERVAL` skip, so villagers idled a third of their lives. A real no-progress detector needs a CONSECUTIVE-miss count, which is per-unit state that loop does not have. The finding is left as a comment at the site so the next attempt does not start from zero.

**What is still broken, narrowed to one leg before I stopped.** Food and wood remain stalled and the AI does not reach Castle Age. Two further probes cut the search down usefully:

1. It is NOT resource exhaustion. At tick 20000 there are eight trees within ten cells of the AI's town centre, four of them UNTOUCHED at amount 100, three cells from its lumber camp.
2. It is NOT the candidate ordering. Instrumenting the comparator directly shows the top five wood candidates for an AI villager are exactly those near trees — tier 1, unsaturated, drop-off distance 5.

What it IS: the `to-dropoff` leg. Traced gatherers show `to-resource` villagers accumulating `gatherProgressTicks` (198-426) so the new 600-tick give-up will re-target them, while CARRIERS sit at `to-dropoff` with `prog=0` indefinitely, holding ten resources one or two cells from their own camp. That branch calls `clearStuck` whenever a movement PLAN exists, so a step that is refused every tick never registers, and unlike `to-resource` it has no give-up at all. Gold flows only because those particular villagers happen to have a clear lane.

The naive fix is the one I already tried and reverted. What it needs is a consecutive-miss count, or the `movementTrafficOps` seam (`trafficIntentKey` and `trafficAttemptTick` are already on the transform) to unwedge crowded carriers. Written into the roadmap in that shape so the next pass starts from the diagnosis rather than from the symptom.

**Result.** 2418 unit tests / 2 skipped, 113 browser / 2 skipped, typecheck, lint, build. Budget splits: `aiResourceReserve` out of `ai.ts`, `dropOffStep` out of `villagerEconomySystem`.


## 2026-08-20 - Three failed fixes, and what they ruled out (v0.3.29 follow-up)

**Action.** Kept pushing on the wedged-carrier bug after the economy fixes landed, then stopped and wrote down what the failures prove.

**Three attempts, all measured against the same baseline** (default map, same seed: 18 villagers, gold climbing past 2400):
1. `setStuck` on a single non-moving step -> 10 villagers, Dark Age. One refused step latches the 30-tick retry skip and transient crowding is constant.
2. A 150-tick CONSECUTIVE-miss count in `gatherProgressTicks` -> 9 villagers, gold frozen at 490. That field is also the gather timer, and a villager can re-enter `gathering` from the carry leg without depositing, so the counter leaks.
3. Wiring `resolveArrivalRedirect` (spec §12.7 lazy redirect) into the drop-off path, which had only ever been called from `playerCommandsSystem` -> completely INERT. Identical numbers to the baseline.

**What the third one ruled out is the most useful result.** `moveUnitOneSubgridStep` does not refuse on occupancy — it steps the fine transform toward the target transform unconditionally. So a unit that does not move is one whose TARGET transform already equals its CURRENT one. Tracing four wedged carriers in cell (52,23) shows all four sharing one identical fine transform, (210, 92), when units are supposed to hold distinct sub-cell slots.

That places the bug in SLOT ALLOCATION — `getUnitTargetTransformForPosition` and `worldOccupancy.getUnitSlotOffset` — and not in the gather loop at all, which is exactly why three fixes inside that loop all missed. Every attempt, its number, and that conclusion is written at the site in `dropOffStep.ts` and in the roadmap, so the next pass starts from a located bug rather than from the symptom.

**Not escalated to a multi-agent search** because this session is explicitly running without subagents; the handover is the written diagnosis instead.

## 2026-08-23 - The Fish Trap, and three villager-only tests (v0.3.40)

**Action.** Shipped the last building in `structures.csv` the game did not have, closing the roster.

**The mechanic was almost free; the builder was the work.** A Fish Trap is the Farm's seam on the other domain — a completed building that gains a `resource` component, here of kind `fish` holding 715 food — and a Fishing Ship is already a gatherer that fishes. What was NOT free is that a Fishing Ship has to BUILD it, and "only villagers build" turned out to be written in three separate places: `buildingPlaceConfirmValidator` (`not_a_villager`), `placementOps` (three checks across preview, begin, and confirm), and `startConstructionWithBuildersDirect` (twice — once for the primary builder and once per helper). All of them now ask `getBuildOptions(owner, unitType)`, which is the same answer the HUD shows the player, so the menu and the refusal cannot disagree. The "this unit builds nothing at all" case keeps a message of its own, because telling someone who selected a militia that it "cannot construct that building here" would be a lie about which part is wrong.

**A rule that only became visible once there were two builder types.** A mixed selection used to enlist every selected villager as a helper. With two kinds of builder that is wrong in a new way — a villager marched at a trap out at sea can never arrive — so helpers are now filtered by whether they can build the thing being built. The same filter picks the PRIMARY builder, so beginning a Fish Trap with a villager and a ship selected uses the ship.

**Water placement is the Dock's rule inverted, and needed one specific bypass.** The occupancy grid holds a static, non-entity-keyed terrain blocker on every water cell (the same one the felled-tree work met earlier today), so `isPlacementBlocked` would refuse a trap on water for the terrain alone. The trap's branch checks that every footprint cell IS water, then refuses on any claim that is not the terrain one — a ship, another trap, the shore. A finished trap then blocks ships like any building, which is what AoE2 does.

**What the trace found that the test then documented rather than fixed.** The ship built the trap and went idle with food still at zero. That is correct: a human's units idle until tasked, exactly like a villager standing beside a berry bush, and the auto-gather gate says so. So the test right-clicks the trap — and the fixture deliberately has NO other food anywhere, so a rising food count cannot come from anything else.

**Result.** Gates: 2486 unit/2 skips, typecheck, lint, build. The AI does not build Fish Traps yet — its build target list is land-only — which is the obvious next step for the naval economy.

**Postscript — the bug this change introduced, and why it cost three full-suite runs.** Deciding builder eligibility from the build menu is the right rule, but the code asked the menu AFTER `addBuildingEntity`. `buildOptionsFor` offers `wonder` only while the owner does not already have one, so the instant the Wonder's entity existed the menu stopped containing it and every builder — including the villager who ordered it — was rejected for the building they had just started. It stood at zero progress forever.

What made it expensive is that the test HUNG instead of failing: `aiPlayer.test.ts` steps the bridge in a synchronous `for` loop, and a Vitest per-test timeout cannot interrupt synchronous code. The full suite stalled with no failure message, which is indistinguishable from this machine's known worker-RPC flake (`docs/debugging/2026-05-04-vitest-worker-ontaskupdate.md`) — so three full-suite runs went into the wrong hypothesis, and two more were killed by my own `taskkill` cleanup while they were still healthy. `git stash` settled it in one run: without the pending changes the file passed in 25.7s; with them, nothing after 240s. Bisecting by file pointed at `trainingMarketOps.ts`, and `-t "Wonder"` named the test.

Eligibility is now computed once, before the site exists. Lesson recorded: a menu derived from world state answers differently after you mutate that state.
