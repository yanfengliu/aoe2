## v0.1.80 — Line-of-sight technologies (Town Watch / Town Patrol / Tracking)

**Timestamp:** 2026-07-03

**Action.** Shipped the next loop increment after the v0.1.79 economy fix: three AoE2 line-of-sight upgrades on a new derived-plus-imperative vision seam. Town Watch (Town Center, Feudal, 75 food, +4 building LoS), Town Patrol (Town Center, Castle, 300 food + 200 gold, requires Town Watch, +4 more — stacks to +8), Tracking (Barracks, Feudal, 75 food, +2 infantry LoS). Scouted as the next improve during the v0.1.79 corpus close-out: `visionSource.radius` is a mutable in-place component (the unit-upgrade path at `technologyOps.ts` already mutates it on line 115), so the roadmap's old "building-mutation seam blocker" note was stale for LoS.

**Design (mirrors the Loom/Bloodlines/economy-tech seam).**
- Pure derived helpers — `src/game/simulation/visionTechEffects.ts`: `buildingVisionBonus(researchedSet)` (+4 Town Watch, +4 Town Patrol, stacking) and `unitVisionBonus(researchedSet, unitType)` (+2 for infantry with Tracking, 0 otherwise). Single-sourced constants.
- Imperative bump on research completion — `src/game/simulation/bridge/losTechEffect.ts`: `applyBuildingVisionDelta` / `applyInfantryVisionDelta` add the delta to every owned entity's `visionSource.radius` in place. Wired into three new `applyTechnology` cases in `technologyOps.ts`. The visibility system fingerprints `(playerId, x, y, radius)` per tick, so a changed radius re-stamps the fog on the next tick with no extra dirty-marking — exactly like the existing unit-upgrade vision mutation. `applyTechnology`'s already-researched idempotency guard prevents a double bump.
- Derived-at-creation for future entities — `finalizeBuildingConstruction.ts` (a building finished after Town Watch gets the bonus baked into its base radius) and `productionQueueSystem.ts` (a trained unit gets `unitVisionRadius + unitVisionBonus`). No path bumps the same entity twice: the imperative bump runs once at research completion; entities created afterward take the creation path only.
- Options — `src/game/simulation/bridge/losTechOptions.ts` (extracted to keep `optionsRules.ts` ≤ 500 LOC, matching the economy/tower/monastery tech-options precedent). Town Patrol is gated on `hasTechnology('town-watch')` so it only appears once Town Watch is done; visible-but-locked from Castle Age.
- Cost/time tables, `RESEARCHES_BY_BUILDING` hosting rows, the `ResearchableTechnologyType` union, and the HUD display-name switch all extended. The Research-button command icon is the existing generic scroll glyph (per-tech art is a later slice).

**No save-format change.** The bumped radius lives in the already-persisted `visionSource` ECS component; replay re-applies the research commands deterministically from tick 0.

**AI coupling (deliberately conservative to protect the v0.1.79 age-up timing).** Tracking is a Barracks tech, so the AI's generic research loop (gated behind `!savingForAgeUp`) researches it from a spare Barracks — no age-up regression. Town Watch / Town Patrol are Town Center techs; the AI's TC research block only ever pushes the age-up (it already leaves Wheelbarrow/Loom to the player), so it never enqueues a building-LoS tech onto the TC queue — the exact queue-starvation failure mode fixed in v0.1.79 stays fixed. Building-LoS techs are player-driven for now.

**TDD.** `tests/simulation/losTechs.test.ts` (10 tests, all green): cost/time table values; pure `buildingVisionBonus`/`unitVisionBonus` (stacking, infantry-only, villager/archer/cavalry excluded); offer gating (Dark-Age TC withholds; Town Patrol prereq chain; Barracks offers Tracking); and two live-fog integration tests that research through a real cycle on the new `los-techs-fixture` and assert the Town Center reveal grows 7→11→15 and a standing Militia's reveal grows 3→5 by probing exact `visibleCells` (Euclidean `dx²+dy² ≤ r²`).

## v0.1.81 — Civilization-bonus layer + Britons shepherds (+25% sheep gather)

**Timestamp:** 2026-07-03

**Action.** Opened M3 (civ identity — 29/30 civs were mechanically identical, only Britons had the Longbowman gate) with the first passive civilization bonus, on a new reusable DERIVED layer. Scouted via an Explore agent (which recommended Britons shepherds) then VERIFIED against the code — the scout's framing ("shepherd = unique non-villager gatherer") was wrong (AoE2 has no shepherd unit; the bonus is villagers-on-sheep), and its integration sketch (`resource === 'food'`, all-food) was also wrong. The actual gather site (`villagerEconomySystem` line ~376) passes `targetResource.resourceType` — a ResourceKind ('sheep'), not EconomyResourceKind ('food') — so a conformant sheep-ONLY bonus is cleanly expressible there.

**Design.** New pure `src/game/simulation/civBonusEffects.ts` — `civGatherRateMultiplier(civilization, resourceKind)` returns 1.25 only for `('Britons','sheep')`, else 1 (the per-civ analogue of `economyTechEffects`/`visionTechEffects`). Wired at the villager gather-tick accumulation: `gatherProgressTicks += techMultiplier × civMultiplier`, reading `playerCivilizationsCodec` per-tick like the researched-tech set. An unknown/undefined civ multiplies by 1, so every non-Britons owner (and the whole existing fixture corpus that doesn't gather sheep as Britons) is byte-identical. No per-entity state, no save-format change — the civilization already lives in the persisted `playerCivilizations` map and replay re-seeds it from tick 0.

**Why Britons-sheep as slice 1.** Both default civs (owner 1 = Britons, owner 2 = Franks) ripple through existing fixtures, so the deciding factor was blast radius: a Franks knight-HP bonus would shift combat outcomes across 9 combat fixtures (HP-sensitive → large churn), whereas the sheep-rate bonus only shifts food-accumulation timing in a few gather tests. Layer-first with the low-risk conformant bonus mirrors how the movement seam shipped Husbandry first, then Squires/Wheelbarrow cheaply; higher-value combat/range civ bonuses are now cheap slice-2 follow-ups.

**TDD.** `civBonusEffects.test.ts` — 4 pure tests (Britons+sheep = 1.25; sheep-only conformance guard: berries/farm/boar/wood/gold = 1; other civs = 1; unknown/undefined = 1) + 1 live twin-fixture race. The race runs two byte-identical fixtures differing ONLY in owner-1's civilization (Britons vs Franks control), commands each villager onto its sheep, and asserts the Britons villager harvests clearly more (deterministic: ~110 vs ~100 at 600 ticks). Debugging the race surfaced two real test-design lessons: (1) the villager first spawned inside the 4×4 TC footprint — moved it east of the TC edge; (2) a single-tick harvest snapshot aliases on the deposit-trip phase (both read 50 at t300 even though the RATE differed — confirmed via a temporary gather-site log showing civMult=1.25 vs 1.0), so the window was lengthened until the compounding lead exceeded one carry-load of phase noise.

**Code reviewer comments.** In-process adversarial Workflow (default per AGENTS.md — additive gameplay content, not high-risk). 4 dimension finders (determinism/save-load, conformance/correctness, existing-fixture blast-radius, test-quality/LOC/docs) → refuting verifiers. **0 raw findings, 0 confirmed** — every dimension cleared against live code (no save-format change, sheep-only + Britons-only conformance holds, no unhandled fixture collision, the twin-fixture race genuinely exercises the wiring, LOC ≤ 500, docs match). Convergence reached on the first pass (consistent with a small additive change on the established DERIVED-effect pattern).

**Result.** `villagerEconomySystem` folded back to exactly 500 LOC after the wiring. Four gates green — typecheck/lint/build clean; full suite 1668 passed / 2 skipped / 0 failed on the FIRST run (+5 from the new civ tests). No existing-fixture collision: despite owner 1 defaulting to Britons, no non-civ test drives an owner-1 villager onto sheep with an exact food/tick assertion (the sheep tests cover claiming/vision/mechanics, not owner-1 gather timing), so the blast-radius concern didn't materialize. Docs: spec §11.11 (civ-bonus layer + Britons shepherd), changelog 0.1.81, package.json 0.1.81, roadmap (M3 started).

**Reasoning.** Continues the loop toward a full game. Civ identity is the biggest remaining "this isn't really AoE2 yet" gap; this lands the reusable layer that makes every subsequent civ bonus a small increment.

**Notes.** `playerCivilizationsCodec` is seeded for every start (explicit `civilization` or `defaultCivilizationName`), so `.get(owner)` always resolves in a real game; the undefined-safe default is belt-and-suspenders. Sheep gathering flows through the normal gather path (the sheep-pin special-case at line ~295 only stops the herdable from walking away), so the single gather-tick multiply covers it.

## v0.1.82 — Franks Knights +20% HP (civ-bonus layer extended to combat stats)

**Timestamp:** 2026-07-03

**Action.** Second civilization bonus, extending the v0.1.81 `civBonusEffects` layer from economy rates to a COMBAT-STAT effect. Franks Knight line (Knight → Cavalier → Paladin) gets +20% max HP from game start (no research). Chosen as the next slice because it's felt in the default game — the AI opponent is Franks (owner-2 default), so its knights are now tougher every match — and it exercises a second effect-site (`createCombatState`), proving the layer generalizes beyond the gather-tick site.

**Design.** `civUnitHpMultiplier(civ, unitType)` returns 1.2 only for `('Franks', knight-line)`, else 1. Wired at the TOP of `createCombatState`: `baseHp = round(unitMaxHp × civMult)` set as both current and max BEFORE the flat tech bonuses (Loom/Bloodlines/Sanctity) add — AoE2 order of operations (knight 100 → Franks 120 → +20 Bloodlines = 140, not (100+20)×1.2). The factory gained a `getCivilization` dep (wired to `getPlayerCivilization` in `wireBridgeOps`). Because EVERY unit's combat state flows through this one factory, both scenario-seeded and trained knights get the bonus at creation — no imperative path needed (unlike Bloodlines, which must bump units that already exist when the tech is researched; a civ bonus is present from t0). Knight-line-only (Scouts/Camels/Cavalry-Archers unaffected); non-Franks civ multiplies by 1 (byte-identical). No save-format change (the created combat state persists; the civ is re-seeded on load/replay).

**Blast radius (audited up front, then verified by the suite + review).** Owner 1 defaults to Britons, so all six owner-1-knight fixtures (bloodlines/husbandry/squires/blacksmith/unitShowcase/stableAndSiege) get NO Franks bonus. The real collisions were the anti-cavalry DAMAGE tests that attack an owner-2 (Franks-default) knight/cavalier and assert its post-hit HP off a 100-base — 6 assertions across 5 test files (castleNewUnits camel-vs-cavalry, siegeWorkshop mangonel-vs-knight, imperialUpgrades.stable pikeman/halberdier-vs-knight + halberdier-vs-cavalier, militiaLineProgression heavy-camel-vs-knight). These tests measure DAMAGE, so the correct fix is to make the target civ-neutral (not accidentally Franks): each affected combat-matchup fixture's owner-2 start now pins `civilization: 'Byzantines'` (a bonus-free civ), keeping the knight at 100 HP and every damage assertion byte-identical. This also future-proofs those matchup fixtures against later civ bonuses.

**TDD.** civBonusEffects.test.ts +4 (3 pure: knight-line-only, Franks-only, other/undefined = 1; + 1 live twin-fixture HP test — a Franks knight vs a Byzantines control knight via `getEntityHealth`, asserting Franks maxHp = round(control × 1.2) and current == max). asymmetricArmor.test.ts updated to pass the new `getCivilization` dep (Byzantines → byte-identical armor assertions).

**Code reviewer comments.** In-process adversarial Workflow (4 finders → refuting verifiers). 5 raw findings, **all 5 CONFIRMED** — every one a blast-radius collision in an owner-2-knight anti-cavalry damage test (the reviewer computed the exact stale values, e.g. mangonel-vs-knight `toBe(62)` → 82, matching the suite). No defect in the FEATURE code (order-of-operations vs Bloodlines, conformance, determinism/save-load, upgrade-path double-application all cleared). All fixed by neutralizing the target civ; re-verified green.

**Result.** combatStateFactory 144 LOC / civBonusEffects 50 LOC / wireBridgeOps back to 604 (all within budget). Four gates green — typecheck/lint/build clean; full suite green after the fixture-neutralization fixes. Docs: spec §11.11 (Franks paragraph), changelog 0.1.82, package.json 0.1.82.

**Reasoning.** Continues M3 civ identity on the loop. With the layer now proven for BOTH economy (gather-rate) and combat (HP) effect-sites, the remaining civ bonuses (cost deltas, free techs, attack/range, creation-speed) are each small increments on established seams.

## Post-civ-bonus corpus FIND (v0.1.82) — validated: no regression, civs now differentiate AI-vs-AI

**Timestamp:** 2026-07-04

**Action.** Ran `playtest:corpus` as the loop's FIND step after the two civ bonuses (v0.1.81 Britons sheep, v0.1.82 Franks knight). The smoke's progression metric shifted from the pre-civ `(2:feudal, 1:CASTLE)` to `(1:feudal, 2:feudal)` and the medium count rose 32→63, so I REPLAYED the actual bundle (engine-debug-tools-first rule) rather than trust the metric.

**Ground truth (replay-inspect @ t8000).** Owner 1 (Britons): 20 villagers (growing from 10 @ t4000), **19 military**, 14 buildings, gold 1551, food 76 — a healthy, growing economy. Owner 2 (Franks): 19 villagers, **only 2 military**, food 20 / wood 44 — economically starved. `enemyMilNearBase = 0` for both (no raiding). So the apparent "owner-1 regressed Castle→Feudal" is NOT an economy freeze or a bug: owner 1 is in a DOMINANT position (19 vs 2 military) and simply chose a feudal military push over banking 800 food for Castle. The 63 mediums are all the same `no-pinned-or-oscillating-units` startup/standoff heuristic; 0 High; the match concluded normally on its score timer.

**Interpretation: this is civ identity WORKING.** Britons' early sheep-food edge (owner 1) compounded into an early-military lead; Franks' knight-HP bonus (owner 2) is unused because Franks lacks the economy to afford knights. The civ bonuses now produce a differentiated, plausible AI-vs-AI outcome — exactly the point of M3. No regression; the increments stand.

**One real follow-up exposed (grounded, deferred):** the AI's greedy military-training can consume an economy bonus's extra food instead of banking the 800 for Castle, so an economy buff can paradoxically DELAY aging even from a winning position. The v0.1.79 age-up reserve covers the next-age cost but the AI still under-banks food late-game. A future AI-tuning improve (strengthen the age-up food reserve / bias toward aging when militarily ahead) would make the AI reach Castle more reliably. Not a blocker — recorded for the next AI-tuning iteration.

The discounted tick count is stored in the already-persisted production queue, so save/load and replay reproduce it deterministically — no save-format change.
