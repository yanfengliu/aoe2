## 2026-09-02 — Villagers stop walking under the enemy Town Centre (v0.3.191)

**Timestamp.** 2026-09-02, two sessions. The first was killed mid-task; the second picked its files up off disk, merged `main` (v0.3.190) onto them, and re-measured everything from scratch — which is what found the residual below.

**Action.** Closed the register's OPEN entry. `src/game/simulation/bridge/enemyDefenceRange.ts` (new) answers "is this cell inside the reach of a COMPLETE building belonging to somebody I am at war with", from the same effective-range function `towerCombatSystem` fires with (moved there, so the keep-out and the arrows cannot disagree), memoised per tick and per owner. `villagerGatherAssignment` filters those nodes out before distance ranks anything and returns an `AssignmentOutcome` (`assigned` / `none` / `dangerous-only`); `idleGatherAssignment` walks every resource kind safe-first and only then revisits the kinds that had nothing but dangerous nodes; `targetFindingOps.findNearestDropOffBuilding` prefers a drop-off outside enemy reach; `aiSystemHuntPhase` takes the nearest boar that stands clear; `enemyDefenceRetarget` (new) moves a committed gatherer off a node a tower has just covered, on the reachability retry cadence. The exception the spec grants — a dangerous node when nothing else of that kind is reachable — survives, after the kind walk. `SimulationBridge` exposes `getRecentUnitDeaths()` (it already existed internally for the render feed) so a villager-death census can be measured at all.

**What was believed and proved false — twice, and both by re-measuring rather than re-reading.** (1) The first session recorded "the six-seed audit shows nothing: BEFORE and AFTER identical to the tick, the baseline economies never reach the enemy", and concluded the fix could only be demonstrated on the reverted v0.3.177 candidate. That was true of the tree it was measured on and false three versions later: v0.3.190's DE build times and nearest-first approaches move the boot map's owner 2 from Castle at 35,250 to 26,750, and a bigger economy is the precondition for the defect. On `main` as it stands, the ORDINARY baseline loses 43 villagers under the enemy Town Centre. The lesson is not "re-measure after a merge" in the abstract — it is that a conclusion of the form "this cannot be shown on the baseline" expires the moment the baseline moves, and nothing marks it stale. (2) That the fix worked. With the rule on, nine villagers still died at ticks 38,793-39,191, every one of them 1-6 cells from owner 1's Town Centre.

**How the residual was found, and what it was.** Not by reading the code — by a per-death trace recording each villager's gather target at the tick it died and asking whether THAT NODE was inside enemy reach. All nine: `desired=gold`, `explicit=false`, `targetDangerous=YES`, targets the gold mines at (7,13), (6,12), (5,12), three cells from owner 1's Town Centre at (8,8). So the rule was not being bypassed by a path, a raid or an explicit order; the exception was firing, over and over. Cause: `villagerEconomySystem` assigns work in TWO places — once at the top of the villager loop, through `assignIdleGatherer` (the safe-first kind walk), and once at the bottom, for the villager that has just delivered a load and is idle again in the same tick. The second call went straight to `assignResource`, so it skipped the kind walk and took the exception the moment safe gold ran out. Every delivery handed the miner the same enemy mine back. `toResourceStep` had two more bare calls of the same shape. The give-up branch now takes the same walk; the over-subscription branch was left alone at first on the reasoning that it can only reach a dangerous node when the kind has no safe node at all — true, and still not good enough, because the spec written in the same commit said "at EVERY automatic assignment site". A critic caught that pair, and that site now refuses a dangerous node outright and leaves the villager on the node it already has, which is the right answer for the one site that must not change a villager's resource KIND. **A rule installed in a shared assignment function is only as good as the enumeration of its callers, and `assignNearestResource` has six.**

**What number moved, and from what.** 45,000 ticks, same tree twice, the only difference an env switch making `collectStaticDefences` return nothing. `aoe2-prototype` owner 2: villagers lost **50 → 7**, of them under enemy defences **43 → 0**. Owner 1: 25 → 27 lost, 0 → 0 under. `seed-2`: byte-identical both ways (34/0 and 10/0) — neither economy there reaches the other's base, which is exactly the seed shape that misled session one. Progression identical on both seeds with and without the rule (boot map owner 2: Feudal 13,750, qualified 22,000, Castle 26,750, 14 building types, 10 unit types, peak army 11). **That is weaker evidence than it looks and the critic was right to say so**: those columns are sampled every 250 ticks and are insensitive at this scale — two of MY OWN builds, both with the rule on, differed by nine villagers and by the whole shape of the endgame ledger, and every progression column was byte-identical. So the honest reading is "the rule saves 43 villagers and nothing the coarse columns can see moves against it", not "the 43 were free".

**The gate, and its red-check.** `tests/simulation/villagersAvoidEnemyDefencesSelfPlay.test.ts` plays the 45,000-tick boot-map match and asserts no owner loses more than three villagers inside an enemy defence's fully-upgraded reach. Green here at 0 and 0 — 159 s alone, 229-512 s under the full suite depending on contention, against a 30-minute timeout. Made to go RED by removing the rule and re-running the same file: 43 against the bar of 3, exit 1. Two smaller layers underneath it (geometry + assignment filter; four bridge fixtures) and two new audit columns (`vill lost`, `under def`) so a future run reports the number instead of needing someone to count.

**Three defects fixed that the measurement did not surface.** (a) Under `requireSafe` + `requireReachable`, safe nodes that exist but cannot be REACHED answered `none`, not `dangerous-only`; `assignIdleGatherer`'s second pass only revisits kinds that said `dangerous-only`, so that kind's reachable-but-dangerous nodes were hidden for good and the villager could be left with no work at all. (b) `isFootprintInsideDefenceReach` scanned every footprint cell against every defence on the drop-off hot path; it is a box-to-box Manhattan gap now, same answer, O(defences). (c) The per-tick memo keyed on the tick alone, so two worlds standing at the same tick would have shared an answer; it keys on the world as well.

**Known gap, recorded rather than fixed.** The rule bounds where a villager is SENT, not the route it walks: a path to a safe node may still cross an enemy defence's reach. None of the 43 measured deaths was of that kind — all were at dangerous nodes — and the boot map's bases are forty cells apart, so it is not visible here. It is in §6.4 and in the register's "what this predicts".

*In code.* (1) HIGH — the anti-truncation assertion the FIRST critic prompted was a tautology: `lastTick >= 40_000 || outcome !== 'running'` is true by construction on a loop that only exits those two ways, so it guarded nothing but a lowered horizon, and three documents had already banked it as a fix. **Notes.** The A/B instrument was a one-line env switch inside `collectStaticDefences`, applied in a throwaway worktree only — it disables the keep-out at every call site while leaving `towerCombatSystem` firing (which reads `effectiveStaticDefenceRange` directly, not the collector), so the arrows still kill.
