# Render realism — one improvement, chosen by audit (2026-09-02)

Owner steering (local-rules.md, 2026-09-02): "The rendering needs to feel realistic" — for the WORLD, within the original voxel art direction, original/procedural art only. Scope here is exactly one improvement, chosen by an audit of the real default view, prototyped, measured and verified. Not a redesign.

## Audit of the real default view

Captured first, before any code was read: `SEED=aoe2-prototype` (fog on, real base) at the default zoom, at 0.7 and at 2.4, with `scripts/captureMapScreenshot.mjs` (`tmp/shots/before*.png`, task-run evidence, gitignored). Then `src/rendering/voxel/` and the sibling `voxel` package's rig were read against what the pixels showed.

Ranked by visible payoff at the default zoom on that view:

1. **No cast shadows.** Every building, tree and unit sits on the grass with no directional shadow. There IS a "contact shadow" (`contactShadow` in `aoeVoxelRecipeTypes.ts`: an unlit 22%-alpha slab under the footprint), but it is invisible on the default view: a building's slab (82% x 78% of the footprint) lies entirely under its plinth (86% x 82%), and a tree's slab lies under its crown from this camera. The result is the classic voxel-toy read: objects float on a flat plane, and nothing ties a tree or the Town Center to the ground. This is also the single most recognisable ground cue of AoE2 HD, whose every sprite carries a long directional shadow.
2. **The daylight rig is backlit.** The sun (`sunOffset {-22, 36, -18}` in `AoeVoxelWorldRenderer.ts`) sits behind every object relative to the fixed isometric camera (which stands at +x,+z): the two wall faces a player can see (+x and +z normals) receive zero directional light and are lit by the hemisphere fill only — measured: the Town Center's left and right plinth faces are the identical pixel (156,144,111) in `before-z24.png`. Only the tops are sunlit, so every box reads as "bright lid, two equal dull sides". Fixing it means moving the sun, which re-shades every face on the map (a whole-frame pixel diff, and every colour-asserting test at risk) and changes where shadows must fall — so it is a separate, later improvement, and the shadow direction below is derived from the rig so the two stay consistent when the sun moves.
3. **No ambient occlusion at creases.** Not addressable from this repo at the default view: the terrain is flat (elevation is always 0 in the projection), buildings are separate instanced cubes, and a screen-space AO pass would live in the sibling `voxel` package.
4. **Water** already has per-cell colour variation, a shallow-water band, animated crests and a shoreline surf curve; **grass** already has patch-scale colour fields, grain and tufts. Neither is the biggest gap.

The rig is not "a single flat directional light": a `HemisphereLight` sky/ground fill plus a `DirectionalLight` sun already exist in `voxel/src/three/daylightRig.ts`. Real shadow maps (`renderer.shadowMap`, `light.castShadow`, chunk `receiveShadow`) are never enabled by that package and there is no seam to enable them from here, so a cast shadow must be geometry this repo emits.

## Chosen: sun-projected cast shadows for buildings, trees/resources and units

Replace the contact slab with the shadow the entity's mass actually casts under the rig's sun, drawn on the existing `shadow` surface (same material, same batch — no new material or batch, so the `materialResources: 7` / `instanceBatches: 9` contracts hold).

- **Projection** comes from the rig: a point at height `h` lands at `(x + h * 22/36, z + h * 18/36)` — away from the sun. The sun offset moves into one shared constant (`aoeVoxelDaylight.ts`) read by both the renderer and the projector, so they cannot drift apart.
- **Caster** per entity is one box: the union footprint of the entity's solid parts whose footprint area is at least a quarter of its largest part's, from the lowest solid part's bottom to the tallest qualifying part's top. This keeps thin accents (flag poles, beams, spear shafts, a tower on a Town Center) from casting a whole-footprint-wide shadow, while a tree's crown, a house's walls and a horse's body all cast.
- **Shape** is exact for that box: the swept footprint is a hexagon, drawn as a partition into three pieces — the footprint rectangle, and two parallelograms swept from the two leading edges. No two pieces of one entity overlap, so an entity never darkens itself twice. Parallelograms need a sheared instance matrix, which the recipe part model gains as an optional `groundAxes` (world-space horizontal edge vectors); everything else keeps rotation + scale.
- **Overlap between entities** must not draw twice at one height. (This design said the failure was double darkness — 22% twice is 39%, banding a forest edge. That was wrong: coincident translucent slabs Z-FIGHT and tear into stripes. See the second outcome section.) The `shadow` material blends, and instances draw in array order with depth writes on, so: shadows are drawn top-down. Each entity's shadow gets one of nine height levels from its anchor cell (`x mod 3`, `z mod 3`), 0.002 world units apart — the depth-buffer floor for a 4000-unit orthographic range — and the shadow lane is ordered by level descending. Two shadows that overlap are then always on different levels (same-level casters are three cells apart, beyond a tree's or unit's reach), and the lower one fails the depth test where the higher one already drew: exactly one layer per pixel. The whole spread is 0.016 units, under a pixel at the default zoom.
- **Memory (fogged) entities** cast nothing, as before. A unit-layer fallback with no solid parts keeps a small footprint slab so the adapter characterisation holds.

## Acceptance criteria (fixed before implementation)

(a) Visible at the default zoom on `aoe2-prototype`: the Town Center and the trees nearest the camera show a dark shape on the grass toward the bottom of the screen (the +x,+z direction, away from the sun), and units show a small one at their feet.
(b) The pixel diff between `before.png` and `after.png` at the default zoom is confined to ground cells adjacent to (toward +x,+z of) buildings, trees, resources and units, plus the ~1% animated noise floor this game's captures carry. No change to sky, HUD, water without a caster nearby, or entity bodies.
(c) Render cost: mean frame time on the default view regresses by at most 10%, measured from the renderer's own `frames` counter (`window.__AOE2_TEST__.getWorldRendererState().metrics.frames` over a fixed wall-clock window, vsync uncapped), with `instances`, `triangles` and `drawCalls` reported alongside. Draw calls must not change (same batch).
(d) All browser tests still pass without loosening any pixel assertion; entity tints and silhouette colours are untouched (the shadow surface is the only thing that changes).

## Non-goals

Moving the sun, real shadow maps, per-part shadows, AO, water or grass changes. Recorded here so the next session can pick candidate 2 with the measurement above already in hand.

## Outcome (measured after implementation, v0.3.193)

(a) Met. `tmp/shots/default-after.png`: the Town Center throws a dark band on the grass along its two camera-facing plinth faces, every tree near the camera has a shadow toward the bottom of the screen, villagers and the scout have a small one at the feet. Under Painted the same shapes read as soft 22% darkening; under Moebius the tone bands quantise them to a full band step (grass 121/150/51 to 65/82/31 at zoom 2.4), which is the style's property.

(b) Met. Default-zoom diff 7.41% of pixels (0.7: 2.72%; 2.4: 14.0%; Painted 8.07%) against the ~1% animated floor; the diff image shows only ground on the +x,+z side of casters plus the usual crest/tuft noise — no sky, HUD, entity body or caster-free water changed.

(c) Not resolvable here. Four interleaved sequential A/B runs on a machine sibling agents held at 73-77% CPU (23-33 node processes) gave median-of-medians ratios 0.74, 1.26, 1.03, 1.00 (Moebius) and 1.20 medians / 0.99 paired (Painted), with per-round swings of 2-5x inside each arm; a concurrent two-page variant never booted its second page. Deterministic cost: instances 786 to 862, triangles 14,112 to 15,936, draw calls 13 to 13, materials and batches unchanged, extra unlit translucent fill 6.4% of the frame. Reported as unmeasured rather than met; re-measure on a quiet machine before building on it.

(d) Met. Unit, architecture and browser suites pass without loosening any pixel assertion; five geometry-containment tests learned to skip the shadow surface (the shadow is meant to leave the footprint), and the unit characterization digests were re-recorded behind a per-unit proof that no non-shadow part changed.

## Second pass, same day: the one-layer guarantee did not hold, and why (v0.3.193)

The work above shipped unreviewed and is corrected here. Two findings, both measured rather than argued.

**The failure mode was misdiagnosed.** Two coincident 22%-alpha slabs do not composite to 39%; the depth test admits both at equal depth and the pair z-fights, so the overlap tears into per-pixel stripes. Proved by control: `SHADOW_LEVEL_STEP` forced to 0, rebuilt, and the boot view re-captured — a boar's shadow came back shredded and a before-to-after tone census showed about ten thousand previously shadowed pixels reverting to lit grass instead of darkening. That control also justifies the level machinery, which nothing had tested end to end: with levels the same frame's shadow tones collapse to one after-tone per before-tone.

**The nine levels did not cover the case that actually occurs.** The level came from the anchor cell, so it separates casters that are not three cells apart — and cannot separate two casters in the SAME cell. Units share cells as ordinary behaviour. Measured on a 12,000-tick AI-vs-AI run of the boot map (`forceAiForOwners: {1,2}`, sampled every 1,500 ticks): up to four villagers on one tile, and up to 13 same-height overlapping slab pairs in a single frame, in 5 of 8 sampled frames. The design's claim — "same-level casters are three cells apart, beyond a tree's or unit's reach" — never considered zero cells apart.

**Fix.** `resolveShadowLevels`, a whole-lane pass in `aoeVoxelCastShadows.ts` that `makePartBatches` runs: group the lane's slabs by owner, and step any caster whose emitted height a caster it overlaps already holds up by whole levels until it is clear. Same measurement after the fix: 0 clashing pairs at every sample. Plain greedy colouring WITHOUT the cell seed was implemented first and is worse — a tree's shadow reaches 2.4 tiles, so a 3x3 block of trees is a nine-clique, and greedy from level 0 exhausted all nine levels on a 6x6 forest and dropped casters back onto a neighbour's. The cell rule is a provably good nine-colouring for the common case and stays as the seed.

**Then an independent critic broke the fix, on a scene I had not tried.** The whole-lane pass capped its search at nine levels and, when all nine were taken, fell back on the caster's EMITTED height — which is taken by construction, since the search only starts when it is. That is bit-for-bit the `STEP=0` control, applied to those casters, and it is reachable in ordinary play: a 6x6 woodline with 12 villagers gathering inside it gives 34 coincident overlapping slab pairs across 7 casters. Every gate and capture I had sat just under the bound (the mixed-scene gate used 6 units, the crowd capture 8; failure starts around 11 mutually overlapping casters). The search is unbounded now, which is the only correct answer — a clique of K mutually overlapping casters needs K distinct heights — so a dense cluster rises above the nine-level band instead of clashing, measured at 0.058 (twenty levels), under four pixels of screen offset at the closest camera zoom. The same review found the sweep was never clamped to the ground plane, so every felled carcass swept its underground mass (a boar's shadow ran a quarter tile too long), and that the default-style pixel diffs cannot distinguish one blend from two at all, because Moebius quantises both into the same tone band.

**A second review pass could not break the unbounded search**, across a 20x20 forest, that forest with 100 villagers in it, 60- and 100-unit army blobs and a 200-population deathball at 0.35-tile packing. What it did find: my spec merge had silently DUPLICATED the art-direction bullet (the older copy still naming the retired contact-shadow material), the stated reason the cell seed works is false for large casters (a Town Center's shadow spans 4.53 tiles, so one at (10,10) and a House at (13,13) share a level and overlap), and the height comparison was exact only because `ground` is hard-coded to 0. All fixed. Three limits are now recorded rather than fixed: the shadow BOBS as a crowd walks (18.9% of caster-frames, up to three pixels — the flicker the rest-pose rule removed from the pose channel lives in the level channel), the lift above the band is bounded only by how tightly the simulation can pack casters, and the 0.002 level step assumes a 24-bit depth buffer that nothing in `rendererParameters` pins. A `gl.getParameter(gl.DEPTH_BITS)` assertion in the browser suite is the cheapest next improvement and the one the review rated highest.

**Still open, and deliberately not taken here.** The daylight rig is still backlit (candidate 2 above, unchanged). Frame cost is still unmeasured at the +/-10% criterion. And the `arena` stone wall (roadmap M7) is unchanged: cast shadows make it read slightly worse, because each segment casts its own hexagon and the gaps between segments are repeated in the shadow.
