## What "done" means for this project (2026-09-03/04, owner, standing)

The game is built **to completion**, matching Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition. Feature-complete means all five of rendering, technology, playability, freedom from bugs, and performance — and it is verified with **AI self-play**, not with a passing suite.

The acceptance test is a sentence, and it is re-checked every time the work looks finished: **"can we play this and feel like we're playing DE?"**

**Why:** the owner has restated this at the head of every standing-loop session, and it kept arriving as prompt text rather than as anything the repo held — which is the one place it cannot survive. It is the tiebreak above every other rule here: the [§1 source-of-truth order](../../design/spec-final.md) says what a rule IS, "Behaviour matches the real Age of Empires II" above says which edition wins, and this says when the work is over.

**How to apply:** a change is not done because its gate is green. It is done when a match has been played with it (see "Every feature is playtested the way a human plays" above) and the whole rendered result has been swept for what is WRONG rather than for what was touched. All visual work is ORIGINAL or procedural art and all audio is procedural or original — no reproduced AoE assets — which is the one place this project deliberately does not match DE, alongside the modern HUD.

## A/B control builds: never `ln -s` node_modules into a worktree (2026-09-05)

A control build of an older commit lives in a `git worktree` under the scratchpad. Its `node_modules` is a junction made by `cmd /c mklink /J <worktree>\node_modules <aoe2>\node_modules`, and that junction is removed with `cmd /c rmdir <worktree>\node_modules` BEFORE `git worktree remove`. Never `ln -s` it — Git Bash copies the directory, and the copy carries the `civ-engine` and `voxel` junctions — and never `rm -rf` or `Remove-Item -Recurse` a tree that still holds a junction.

**Why:** on 2026-09-03 `git worktree remove --force` on exactly such a tree followed the copied `civ-engine` junction and emptied `../civ-engine/.git` down to `refs/` (defect register, 2026-09-05). Git's recursive delete on Windows follows junctions; a 267-character path was all that stopped it.

**How to apply:** the three commands above, in that order, every time; `scripts/controlWorktree.mjs`, once it exists, is the fixed command that does them and nothing else.

## HUD styling: modern, not medieval (2026-08-18, owner)

The interface chrome targets a **modern** look — translucent dark-glass panels, one hairline border, soft two-part elevation, a token palette, generous radii — and explicitly NOT the AoE2-era framed wood-and-stone treatment that shipped in v0.1.38.

**Why:** the owner asked for "a modern looking UI" directly. The earlier direction came from the "perfect AoE2 HD clone" north star, which still holds for the **world** (terrain, units, buildings, effects). Only the HUD surface direction changed.

**How to apply:** every HUD colour comes from the tokens in `styles.css:root` (`--hud-surface*`, `--hud-hairline*`, `--hud-text*`, `--hud-accent`, the per-resource hues, and the radius/elevation scales). Do not reintroduce a raw colour literal into a HUD rule — `tests/ui/hudPalette.test.ts` fails on the retired wood-and-stone literals and holds text contrast against a translucent panel over sunlit grass. `hudChrome.css` owns the chassis, `hudCommandPanel.css` the components inside it, `hudIcons.css` glyph sizing.

Authoritative description: `design/spec-final.md` §14.1 "HUD chrome".

## The HUD holds its shape, and nothing floats over the minimap (2026-09-02, owner)

Entering a mode — placement, a menu, a queue filling up — must not move, shrink, or hide the controls the player was just using (a label may make room for the mode's own pill; a button may not move), and no floating element (tooltip, toast, badge) may cover the minimap.

**Why:** the owner asked for the UI to be "elegant" the day they found, by playing, that clicking a build card collapsed the build palette to a sliver and put its tooltip on the minimap. The bar is a flex row with exactly one member that absorbs width changes, so a status rendered as a sibling took its width from the palette; and a positioner that clamps to the viewport does not clamp to the HUD. Both are the kind of defect a boot screenshot never shows and a player finds in the first minute.

**How to apply:** a mode's announcement goes INSIDE the group it describes (the placement pill lives in the Build heading), never as a new sibling of the bar. Floating elements are positioned against the HUD's surfaces, not only the viewport — `tooltips.ts` floats a command-bar tooltip above the bar and slides it clear of `.hud-panel--map`. A change to the bar is checked at both its regimes (wrapped below 1120px, unwrapped above) and at 1280x720, the laptop width where the palette is narrowest; `tests/browser/placement-mode-hud-layout.spec.ts` is the shape of that check, and it measures VISIBLE width (the rect clipped by the scrolling panel), because a layout box keeps its min-width after the panel has clipped it. Register entry: 2026-09-02.

**Amended 2026-09-02 (v0.3.189, play-test findings F5/F6):** the bar also never HIDES what it holds. It never runs off sideways at any width (below 1120px there is no horizontal scroll, so sideways overflow is unreachable by any means), anything it cannot show is reachable by wheeling the deck, and the common cards fit outright at 800x600 as well as above it. `tests/browser/command-deck-fits.spec.ts` is that check: three viewports x villager (both build pages) / scout / Town Centre for the strong rule, plus five real fixtures — Blacksmith, Town Centre, Market, Castle, Monastery — for the reachability rule. A scroll region is only an answer once it can be reached: the panel is `pointer-events: none`, so before this the bar's own overflow could not be wheeled at all, and Playwright's `locator.click()` scrolls hidden overflow, which is why a whole invisible BUILD group passed every existing test. The bar must also never reach the CENTRE of the canvas: every "go to X" affordance centres the camera there, so a bar that covers it hides what the player just asked to see (a 276px ceiling at 800x600 put the bar's top at y 312 and swallowed a Dock's new Transport Ship at 319; the ceiling is `min(42vh, 252px)` for that reason). Two consequences to check whenever the bar's height changes, both of which bit before a reviewer saw them: any test that picks a world cell by pointer-reachability (`findValidPlacementNearTownCenter`) will choose a different cell, and any test that clicks an entity on the canvas near the bottom of the screen may find it behind the bar.

## Rendering is inspected from several angles and zoom levels (2026-08-23, owner)

Whenever the rendering changes, look at the result from **multiple camera angles and multiple zoom levels** before calling it done — not one framing, and not only the region that was edited.

**Why:** the owner asked for it directly, and this repo has paid for it repeatedly: v0.3.34's black-bordered diorama, v0.3.36's tan hill blotches, and v0.3.33's 620px selection panel were all invisible from the framing the author happened to choose and obvious from another one. The fleet canon states the principle ("sweep several camera angles and zoom levels"); this entry is the repo-specific form with the tools named.

**How to apply:** this renderer's isometric angle is FIXED, so "several angles" here means several framings — `scripts/captureMapScreenshot.mjs` takes `SEED`, `LABEL`, `FOCUS="x,y"`, `ZOOM` (camera clamp 0.7-2.4, opens at 2.0), `SIZE="WxH"`, `SELECT="villager"` (selects the human's first unit of that type, so the command bar is captured in its SELECTED state — where the 2026-09-02 idle-bell overlap lived), `HOTKEY="F4"` (a comma-separated key sequence pressed before the shot, which is the only way a HUD PANEL reaches a capture — `HOTKEY="F4,Tab,Tab,Enter"` also proves the keyboard path into one, and `HOTKEY="F4,v,i,e,t"` drives a search box) and `PREVIEW_PORT` env vars (which `vite preview` to capture from — with several worktrees on one machine, whatever squats on 4173 is a sibling's build; `npm run test:browser` honours the same variable), so every view in a sweep comes from the maintained script rather than a one-off — capture at minimum a default-zoom view of the real default scenario (`aoe2-prototype`, with fog and a real base: a full-vision showcase fixture hides fog, accent and coordinate defects), a zoomed-in view of the thing that changed, and a zoomed-out view of the whole map. Both scripts write under the gitignored `tmp/captures` (override with `OUT_DIR`), because a capture is task-run evidence rather than a repository input. Pair the before/after of the changed region with `scripts/diffMapScreenshots.mjs` — capture twice as `LABEL=<stem>-before` / `LABEL=<stem>-after`, then diff with `LABEL=<stem>` — per the AGENTS.md visual invariant, and read the diff against this game's ~1% animated noise floor. Give both sides of a pair `TICKS=1` (or the same `TICKS`): the init-script pause freezes the sim but not which frame of the minimap's first reveal the canvas shows, and two DIFFERENT builds freeze on different frames — measured 2026-09-02: ≤4/255 per channel across the minimap, 0.66% of a 1280x720 frame on top of a 0.58% change, while same-build pairs were 0.00% and `TICKS=1` pairs exact — so a "confined to the change" claim needs both frames drawn after the pause. The last look is always a whole-frame sweep for what is WRONG, including parts the change never touched.

## Every feature is playtested the way a human plays (2026-08-23, owner)

A feature is not done when its tests pass — it is done when a full match has been played with it. Use the playtest infrastructure the engine and this repo already ship (`npm run playtest:llm`, `playtest:corpus-llm`, `playtest:findings`, `replay:inspect`, `run-oracles`); do not hand-write a synthetic probe where the harness would answer the question.

**Why:** owner directive, and the standing evidence is the whole v0.3.29–v0.3.30 arc — siege production shipped green and could not fire in a real match, and the AI's economy had been frozen since roughly tick 6000 for versions on end with every unit test passing. "Adding to a system does not prove it RUNS" is the standing form of it.

**How to apply:** the agent plays the HUMAN slot (player 1) against the in-game AI, reads the rendered screen, and plays a match long enough to reach the mechanic under test — a Castle-Age technology is not exercised by a 3000-tick run. Orders may ride the JSON command bridge (owner decision, 2026-08-23: it is the reliable surface), but **anything a human is supposed to be able to do must also be reachable with the mouse and keyboard**, and that half is proved by the Playwright browser suite, not assumed. `playtest:findings` turns each run into the objective backlog; the harness only ever observes and reports (§15.7).

## Behaviour matches the real Age of Empires II (2026-08-23, owner)

The game must **function identically to the real Age of Empires II** — same costs, rates, counters, tech-tree gating, controls, and consequences. A divergence is a defect even when nothing in this repo asserts the correct value.

**Why:** owner directive 2026-08-23. It is the standing tiebreak for every "is this good enough?" question: the answer is whatever the real game does.

**How to apply:** resolve rules in the [§1 source-of-truth order](../../design/spec-final.md) — `design/stats/` CSVs first, then the spec, then official references. **Definitive Edition is the authoritative edition** (owner, 2026-08-23: "HD" in the directive is shorthand for the real game, and the CSVs are DE-derived); where an HD-era rule differs from DE, DE wins and the choice is recorded. A remembered game rule is a hypothesis — verify it against an authoritative reference before implementing to match it; so is an LLM conformance probe's rule claim, which has misdiagnosed a correct implementation as a bug. Two deliberate exceptions, both owner-set and both about LOOK rather than behaviour: the HUD chrome is modern (above), and all art is original/procedural rather than reproduced AoE2 assets.

**Restated by the owner 2026-09-02, mid-play-test:** "The rendering needs to feel realistic. The UI needs to be elegant. Game needs to be well optimized and correct. Bug free." This does not change the north star above — realism is for the WORLD within the original voxel art direction, elegance is the HUD's "modern looking UI" — but it fixes the order of proof: a change to either surface is judged by playing it, not only by a passing test, and the play test that found the placement-mode panel collapse is the template.
- **No `git stash` in a worktree while other worktrees are live.** The stash list is shared across every worktree of a repo, so two agents stashing to merge main cross each other's positional `stash@{0}` pops and drops (2026-09-02: one agent's `drop` removed another's "castshadow-wip-2"; the other may have popped the first's enemy-defence files into its own tree). Bring main in by committing a throwaway WIP on the branch, merging, and squashing before the final commit. If a stash is unavoidable, name it (`git stash push -m <name>`) and address it only by name (`git stash pop stash^{/<name>}`), never by position.


## A map-generation change is measured against the maps it cannot reach (2026-09-04)

Any change to map generation is A/B'd on the whole seed set, and the maps the change CANNOT touch are the control: they must come back **byte-identical on every column of both owners**. If they move, the reasoning is wrong or the arms differ by more than the variable, and nothing else in the run can be trusted.

**Why:** it has paid twice in one day. Raising `DROP_OFF_ANCHOR_RADIUS` can only bite where the anchor was null, so `coastal` — nearest tree 5-7 cells — had to be unchanged, and was. Putting a woodline inside a wall can only reach a map that HAS a wall, so `aoe2-prototype`, `arabia`, `gold-rush` and `coastal` had to be unchanged, and were, across three different placement orderings. That last part is what made the three orderings comparable to each other at all. The one time a control did move, the cause was source edits landing between the two arms rather than a real effect.

**How to apply:** run `SEEDS=aoe2-prototype,arabia,arena,fortress,gold-rush,coastal TICKS=60000 SAMPLE=500 npm run ai:army-block` for both arms, back to back, in ONE background job that flips the variable itself and verifies `src` is unchanged at the end — never by editing between two hand-started runs. `diff` the control rows first and stop if they differ. The walled maps are `arena` and `fortress`; every other shipped seed is a control for anything that only reaches a wall.

## Standing-loop entry point (2026-09-02, owner)

The canon rule "a standing loop takes its next task from exercising the artifact the way its user does" resolves here to **playing a real match**: `npm run dev`, scenario `aoe2-prototype` (fog on, a real base), default zoom, the human slot — then sweep the whole frame for what is WRONG before choosing what to work on.

**Why:** the owner asked (2026-09-02) for the standing loop to be stated generally enough for every repo, so the fleet rule leaves the entry point to each one. This repo has a runnable game, so "the way its user does" means playing it rather than reading it. An unsteered loop sources work from code instead, which yields refactors and audits — easy to start, easy to declare done, and blind to everything a player hits first.

**How to apply:** the play itself follows "Every feature is playtested the way a human plays" above (`npm run playtest:llm` for a match long enough to reach the mechanic; the mouse-and-keyboard half proved by the browser suite, never assumed). Views come from `scripts/captureMapScreenshot.mjs` per "Rendering is inspected from several angles and zoom levels" above — never a one-off capture, never a showcase fixture. When the default view shows nothing wrong, play DEEPER rather than switching to code: mid- and late-game states are the under-inspected ones, and a Castle-Age mechanic is not reached by a 3000-tick run.

## Several sessions, one repo: one writer per checkout (2026-09-03, owner)

The owner runs multiple Claude sessions against this repo at once — three peers were live when this was written, two in aoe2 and one in fleet. The rule that makes that safe is not a habit, it is a boundary: **a checkout has exactly ONE writing session at a time.** Everything else in this entry is damage control for when that boundary is crossed.

**Why:** every cross-session loss this repo has paid for came from two sessions sharing one working tree, never from two sessions in separate worktrees. A `git stash` drop that deleted a sibling's named WIP; `git stash push -- <dir>` that reverted five of another session's in-flight files to HEAD (`ae1ec54`); three merges aborted on a shared `dist/` race; `git add -A` that swept `fleet`'s canon sync into a defect-register commit whose message never mentioned it (2026-09-03, twice in one day); and `git reset --soft`, which restores HEAD byte-perfectly while deleting `.git/MERGE_HEAD`, so a merge in progress loses its second parent and the next commit records the content with the branch never marked merged. While this very entry was being written, `scripts/cropZoom.mjs`, `src/game/simulation/civProfiles.ts` and `tests/content/civProfiles.test.ts` appeared untracked in the shared checkout — a peer's unfinished work, one `git add -A` away from being committed by the wrong session under the wrong message.

**How to apply.**

- **Take a lane.** One session owns the shared checkout at `github/aoe2` and does the integrating — merges, version bumps, changelog, gates, pushes. Every other session works in its own `git worktree` on its own branch and hands the branch over; it never commits in the shared checkout. Use available coordination tools to establish the designated integration owner and observed peer status. `git worktree list` shows worktrees, not whether their owners are live; do not infer visibility or ownership from an unavailable tool.
- **In a shared checkout, never use a whole-tree command.** Stage by explicit pathspec (`git commit -m ... -- path/one path/two`). Not `git add -A`, not `git commit -a`. A pathspec is not a scope either: `git stash push -- <dir>` takes everything dirty under that directory, not your change within it.
- **No `git stash` at all while a peer is live.** The stash list is shared across every worktree of the repo, so positional `stash@{0}` pops and drops cross sessions. To bring `main` into a branch, commit a throwaway WIP, merge, and squash before the final commit. If a stash is truly unavoidable, name it (`git stash push -m <name>`) and address it only by name (`git stash pop stash^{/<name>}`).
- **Never run destructive git in a checkout you do not own** — `reset`, `checkout --`, `merge --abort`, `clean`. If you have already disturbed someone's tree, say which invariant you broke rather than that the state is restored.
- **One preview port per session, never the default.** `PREVIEW_PORT` is honoured by `npm run test:browser`, `captureMapScreenshot.mjs` and `diffMapScreenshots.mjs`; whatever squats on 4173 belongs to a sibling. Capturing from a sibling's preview measures THEIR build as yours, which is why the capture script refuses a server whose served asset hashes differ from this checkout's `dist/`. Check first with `netstat -ano | grep LISTENING | grep -oE ':4[0-9]{3}'`.
- **`dist/` is per checkout, not per session.** Two sessions running `npm run build` in one checkout race, and the loser's gate run measures the winner's bundle.
- **Pull before push; never force-push `main`.** Expect non-fast-forward rejections as normal traffic when peers are live.
- **A generated file changing under you is not a conflict to resolve.** `AGENTS.md`'s canon block is written by `npm run sync-canon` from `../fleet/FLEET.md`, and changed three times in one day here. Commit it on its own with a message that says so; never fold it into unrelated work.
- **Untracked files you did not create are someone's work in progress.** Leave them. Do not add them, do not clean them, do not assume they are debris.

## A feature's cost is its own share of tick time, never whole-match ticks per second (2026-09-05)

When a change is measured for cost, the number that counts is the change's OWN share of step time — its timer, or its inclusive share in a CPU profile — and, as the sanity check, ticks per second sampled INTERLEAVED (baseline, candidate, baseline, candidate, same map, same minute, machine confirmed quiet). Whole-match ticks per second over an A/B is not a cost measurement and must not be written up as one.

**Why:** the gather-walk A/B (v0.3.204) read as a 34% slowdown on the boot map from whole-match ticks per second, and the change's own timer put it at 0.2% of step time. The rest was two things ticks per second cannot separate: the candidate's economy built a bigger world (43 to 64 units on Black Forest), and an orphaned `chrome-headless-shell` was burning twelve CPU-minutes per minute, so identical baseline work measured 366 and 268 ticks per second forty minutes apart. Interleaved on a quiet machine the candidate was not slower at equal ticks — and even that sample compares worlds of different size (a critic found arena at 29 units under the candidate against 48 in the baseline at the same tick), so interleaving removes the machine and nothing else. Equal WORK is the premise, and only the feature's own timer has it.

**How to apply:** `npm run ai:gather-walk` prints the walk field's rebuild count and wall time from the debug snapshot and milliseconds per thousand unit-ticks beside ticks per second — read those first. Before any timing sample, list the processes with real CPU time (`tasklist /FO CSV /V` through `node -e`, since the worktree shell guard refuses `powershell`) and kill nothing you do not own; report the load instead. A CPU profile is one command: `node --cpu-prof --cpu-prof-dir=<dir> --import tsx <runner>` on a 30,000-tick run, summed by inclusive share per file pattern. Candidate for the fleet canon; staged here because the measurement was made here.

## A thread is closed by the task that closes it, and every task ends with a look at the open ones (2026-09-05, owner)

`docs/threads/current` holds objectives that are being worked on, and nothing else. The commit that ships or drops an objective moves its thread to `docs/threads/done/` with a closure line under the title (`Closed YYYY-MM-DD: <version or decision>`), fixes every pointer to the old path, and gives each leftover follow-up a home: the lessons queue if it names a gate, the register if it is a defect, or an explicit drop with the reason. At the end of every task, each thread in `current/` that the task advanced gets a dated status line saying what moved and what is still open, and each one whose objective has since shipped or been dropped is moved.

**Why:** the owner asked (2026-09-05) whether the project really had so many open threads. It did not. Six of the eight folders in `current/` were finished work: three shipped on 2026-07-15 and were never moved (`auto-mine-after-camp` was CREATED by a commit titled "close auto-mine"), one had been dismantled by the 2026-08-01 decision that removed the fix arm, one shipped in August and one was scoped to a single improvement that had shipped. Two code comments still pointed at threads moved months earlier: one had survived the 2026-06-09 housekeeping review because that sweep read `src/` only, and the other was written six days AFTER it by the very commit that moved its thread — a move is a rename, and the commit that renames is the one most likely to write the old path. A reader of `current/` would have taken shipped work for open work, and the one thread that WAS open, `lumber-camp-routing`, stopped at round 5 while three versions of follow-on work went into the register and the devlog. Closing was the LAST step of a plan (PLAN step 9, "move thread folder to done") instead of part of the shipping commit, and nothing read `current/` at the end of a task, so a thread not moved in its own commit was never moved. Register entry: 2026-09-05.

**How to apply:** closing is part of shipping, so it happens in the same commit, never as a later sweep. `tests/architecture/threadHygiene.test.ts` is the gate, three checks. (1) Every `docs/threads/(current|done)/...` path referenced from code, tests, scripts, the spec, the architecture docs, the policies and the learning docs must exist on disk with its exact casing, and the scan must find at least three real pointers in each of `src`, `tests`, `design` and `docs/architecture`, so a scan that reads less than it claims goes red. The devlog, the changelog, the debugging logs and the threads themselves are exempt by this rule's own decision, not by an earlier ruling: they narrate moves and pending states as they were ("the folder moves from `current/` to `done/` in this commit"), so a literal scan of them cannot be satisfied without falsifying the narrative. (2) No file under `current/` may carry a `Closed YYYY-MM-DD:` line: the ritual writes that line and moves the folder in one commit, so a closure line still under `current/` is the move forgotten — and without this check the closure line's own date would satisfy (3) forever. (3) Every thread in `current/` must carry a `YYYY-MM-DD` date, in a file or a folder name, within 30 days of the newer of HEAD's commit date and the newest date in `docs/devlog/summary.md`, so a thread a month of commits has not written a dated line into goes red until it gets a status line or a move. Said plainly: (2) catches a thread closed by the ritual at once, (3) catches the rest only after 30 days, and on the morning this was written two of the six (19 and 3 days old) would have passed (3) alone. (3) reads dates as text — a deadline or a version's date counts as maintenance, and a false status line dated today passes. Candidate for the fleet canon: seven repos use the `docs/threads` convention and the constitution does not mention it; staged here because the defect was paid for here.
