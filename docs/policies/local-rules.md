## What "done" means for this project (2026-09-03/04, owner, standing)

The game is built **to completion**, matching Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition. Feature-complete means all five of rendering, technology, playability, freedom from bugs, and performance — and it is verified with **AI self-play**, not with a passing suite.

The acceptance test is a sentence, and it is re-checked every time the work looks finished: **"can we play this and feel like we're playing DE?"**

**Why:** the owner has restated this at the head of every standing-loop session, and it kept arriving as prompt text rather than as anything the repo held — which is the one place it cannot survive. It is the tiebreak above every other rule here: the [§1 source-of-truth order](../../design/spec-final.md) says what a rule IS, "Behaviour matches the real Age of Empires II" above says which edition wins, and this says when the work is over.

**How to apply:** a change is not done because its gate is green. It is done when a match has been played with it (see "Every feature is playtested the way a human plays" above) and the whole rendered result has been swept for what is WRONG rather than for what was touched. All visual work is ORIGINAL or procedural art and all audio is procedural or original — no reproduced AoE assets — which is the one place this project deliberately does not match DE, alongside the modern HUD.

## A/B control builds: never `ln -s` node_modules into a worktree (2026-09-05)

A control build of an older commit lives in a `git worktree` under the scratchpad. Its `node_modules` is a junction made by `cmd /c mklink /J <worktree>\node_modules <aoe2>\node_modules`, and that junction is removed with `cmd /c rmdir <worktree>\node_modules` BEFORE `git worktree remove`. Never `ln -s` it — Git Bash copies the directory, and the copy carries the `civ-engine` and `voxel` junctions — and never `rm -rf` or `Remove-Item -Recurse` a tree that still holds a junction.

**Why:** on 2026-09-03 `git worktree remove --force` on exactly such a tree followed the copied `civ-engine` junction and emptied `../civ-engine/.git` down to `refs/` (defect register, 2026-09-05). Git's recursive delete on Windows follows junctions; a 267-character path was all that stopped it.

**How to apply:** the three commands above, in that order, every time; `scripts/controlWorktree.mjs`, once it exists, is the fixed command that does them and nothing else.

## HUD styling: modern, not medieval (2026-08-18, owner)

The interface chrome targets a **modern** look — translucent dark-glass panels, one hairline border, soft two-part elevation, a token palette, generous radii — and explicitly NOT the AoE2-era framed wood-and-stone treatment that shipped in v0.1.38.

**Why:** the owner asked for "a modern looking UI" directly. The earlier direction came from the "perfect AoE2 HD clone" north star, which still holds for the **world** (terrain, units, buildings, effects). Only the HUD surface direction changed.

**How to apply:** every HUD colour comes from the tokens in `styles.css:root` (`--hud-surface*`, `--hud-hairline*`, `--hud-text*`, `--hud-accent`, the per-resource hues, and the radius/elevation scales). Do not reintroduce a raw colour literal into a HUD rule — `tests/ui/hudPalette.test.ts` fails on the retired wood-and-stone literals and holds text contrast against a translucent panel over sunlit grass. `hudChrome.css` owns the chassis, `hudCommandPanel.css` the components inside it, `hudIcons.css` glyph sizing.

Authoritative description: `design/spec-final.md` §14.1 "HUD chrome".

## The HUD holds its shape, and nothing floats over the minimap (2026-09-02, owner)

Entering a mode — placement, a menu, a queue filling up — must not move, shrink, or hide the controls the player was just using (a label may make room for the mode's own pill; a button may not move), and no floating element (tooltip, toast, badge) may cover the minimap.

**Why:** the owner asked for the UI to be "elegant" the day they found, by playing, that clicking a build card collapsed the build palette to a sliver and put its tooltip on the minimap. The bar is a flex row with exactly one member that absorbs width changes, so a status rendered as a sibling took its width from the palette; and a positioner that clamps to the viewport does not clamp to the HUD. Both are the kind of defect a boot screenshot never shows and a player finds in the first minute.

**How to apply:** a mode's announcement goes INSIDE the group it describes (the placement pill lives in the Build heading), never as a new sibling of the bar. Floating elements are positioned against the HUD's surfaces, not only the viewport — `tooltips.ts` floats a command-bar tooltip above the bar and slides it clear of `.hud-panel--map`. A change to the bar is checked at both its regimes (wrapped below 1120px, unwrapped above) and at 1280x720, the laptop width where the palette is narrowest; `tests/browser/placement-mode-hud-layout.spec.ts` is the shape of that check, and it measures VISIBLE width (the rect clipped by the scrolling panel), because a layout box keeps its min-width after the panel has clipped it. Register entry: 2026-09-02.

**Amended 2026-09-02 (v0.3.189, play-test findings F5/F6):** the bar also never HIDES what it holds. It never runs off sideways at any width (below 1120px there is no horizontal scroll, so sideways overflow is unreachable by any means), anything it cannot show is reachable by wheeling the deck, and the common cards fit outright at 800x600 as well as above it. `tests/browser/command-deck-fits.spec.ts` is that check: three viewports x villager (both build pages) / scout / Town Centre for the strong rule, plus five real fixtures — Blacksmith, Town Centre, Market, Castle, Monastery — for the reachability rule. A scroll region is only an answer once it can be reached: the panel is `pointer-events: none`, so before this the bar's own overflow could not be wheeled at all, and Playwright's `locator.click()` scrolls hidden overflow, which is why a whole invisible BUILD group passed every existing test. The bar must also never reach the CENTRE of the canvas: every "go to X" affordance centres the camera there, so a bar that covers it hides what the player just asked to see (a 276px ceiling at 800x600 put the bar's top at y 312 and swallowed a Dock's new Transport Ship at 319; the ceiling is `min(42vh, 252px)` for that reason). Two consequences to check whenever the bar's height changes, both of which bit before a reviewer saw them: any test that picks a world cell by pointer-reachability (`findValidPlacementNearTownCenter`) will choose a different cell, and any test that clicks an entity on the canvas near the bottom of the screen may find it behind the bar.

## Rendering is inspected from several angles and zoom levels (2026-08-23, owner)

Whenever the rendering changes, look at the result from **multiple camera angles and multiple zoom levels** before calling it done — not one framing, and not only the region that was edited.

**Why:** the owner asked for it directly, and this repo has paid for it repeatedly: v0.3.34's black-bordered diorama, v0.3.36's tan hill blotches, and v0.3.33's 620px selection panel were all invisible from the framing the author happened to choose and obvious from another one. The fleet canon states the principle ("sweep several camera angles and zoom levels"); this entry is the repo-specific form with the tools named.

**How to apply:** this renderer's isometric angle is FIXED, so "several angles" here means several framings — `scripts/captureMapScreenshot.mjs` takes `SEED`, `LABEL`, `FOCUS="x,y"`, `ZOOM` (camera clamp 0.7-2.4, opens at 2.0), `SIZE="WxH"`, `SELECT="<type>"` (selects the human's first entity of that type — a unit type such as `villager` or `scout`, a building type such as `town-center` or `house`, or a resource in the human's base: `sheep`, `tree`, `berry-bush`, `gold-mine`, `stone-mine` — or `SELECT="box"` for every unit the human owns, the mixed selection a marquee over the base makes; so the command bar is captured in its SELECTED state — where the 2026-09-02 idle-bell overlap lived, and where the bar's height depended on WHICH of these was selected until v0.3.207), `HOTKEY="F4"` (a comma-separated key sequence pressed before the shot, which is the only way a HUD PANEL reaches a capture — `HOTKEY="F4,Tab,Tab,Enter"` also proves the keyboard path into one, and `HOTKEY="F4,v,i,e,t"` drives a search box) and `PREVIEW_PORT` env vars (which `vite preview` to capture from — with several worktrees on one machine, whatever squats on 4173 is a sibling's build; `npm run test:browser` honours the same variable), so every view in a sweep comes from the maintained script rather than a one-off — capture at minimum a default-zoom view of the real default scenario (`aoe2-prototype`, with fog and a real base: a full-vision showcase fixture hides fog, accent and coordinate defects), a zoomed-in view of the thing that changed, and a zoomed-out view of the whole map. Both scripts write under the gitignored `tmp/captures` (override with `OUT_DIR`), because a capture is task-run evidence rather than a repository input. Pair the before/after of the changed region with `scripts/diffMapScreenshots.mjs` — capture twice as `LABEL=<stem>-before` / `LABEL=<stem>-after`, then diff with `LABEL=<stem>` — per the AGENTS.md visual invariant, and read the diff against this game's ~1% animated noise floor. Give both sides of a pair `TICKS=1` (or the same `TICKS`): the init-script pause freezes the sim but not which frame of the minimap's first reveal the canvas shows, and two DIFFERENT builds freeze on different frames — measured 2026-09-02: ≤4/255 per channel across the minimap, 0.66% of a 1280x720 frame on top of a 0.58% change, while same-build pairs were 0.00% and `TICKS=1` pairs exact — so a "confined to the change" claim needs both frames drawn after the pause. The last look is always a whole-frame sweep for what is WRONG, including parts the change never touched.

## Every feature is playtested the way a human plays (2026-08-23, owner)

A feature is not done when its tests pass — it is done when a full match has been played with it. Use the playtest infrastructure the engine and this repo already ship (`npm run playtest:llm`, `playtest:corpus-llm`, `playtest:findings`, `replay:inspect`, `run-oracles`); do not hand-write a synthetic probe where the harness would answer the question.

**Why:** owner directive, and the standing evidence is the whole v0.3.29–v0.3.30 arc — siege production shipped green and could not fire in a real match, and the AI's economy had been frozen since roughly tick 6000 for versions on end with every unit test passing. "Adding to a system does not prove it RUNS" is the standing form of it.

**How to apply:** the agent plays the HUMAN slot (player 1) against the in-game AI, reads the rendered screen, and plays a match long enough to reach the mechanic under test — a Castle-Age technology is not exercised by a 3000-tick run. Orders may ride the JSON command bridge (owner decision, 2026-08-23: it is the reliable surface), but **anything a human is supposed to be able to do must also be reachable with the mouse and keyboard**, and that half is proved by the Playwright browser suite, not assumed. `playtest:findings` turns each run into the objective backlog; the harness only ever observes and reports (§15.7).

## Behaviour matches the real Age of Empires II (2026-08-23, owner)

The game must **function identically to the real Age of Empires II** — same costs, rates, counters, tech-tree gating, controls, and consequences. A divergence is a defect even when nothing in this repo asserts the correct value.

**Why:** owner directive 2026-08-23. It is the standing tiebreak for every "is this good enough?" question: the answer is whatever the real game does.

**How to apply:** resolve rules in the [§1 source-of-truth order](../../design/spec-final.md) — `design/stats/` CSVs first, then the spec, then official references. **Definitive Edition is the authoritative edition** (owner, 2026-08-23: "HD" in the directive is shorthand for the real game, and the CSVs are DE-derived); where an HD-era rule differs from DE, DE wins and the choice is recorded. A remembered game rule is a hypothesis — verify it against an authoritative reference before implementing to match it; so is an LLM conformance probe's rule claim, which has misdiagnosed a correct implementation as a bug. Two deliberate exceptions, both owner-set and both about LOOK rather than behaviour: the HUD chrome is modern (above), and all art is original/procedural rather than reproduced AoE2 assets.

**Restated by the owner 2026-09-02, mid-play-test:** "The rendering needs to feel realistic. The UI needs to be elegant. Game needs to be well optimized and correct. Bug free." This does not change the north star above — realism is for the WORLD within the original voxel art direction, elegance is the HUD's "modern looking UI" — but it fixes the order of proof: a change to either surface is judged by playing it, not only by a passing test, and the play test that found the placement-mode panel collapse is the template.
- **No `git stash` in a worktree while other worktrees are live.** The stash list is shared across every worktree of a repo, so two agents stashing to merge main cross each other's positional `stash@{0}` pops and drops (2026-09-02: one agent's `drop` removed another's "castshadow-wip-2"; the other may have popped the first's enemy-defence files into its own tree). Bring main in by committing a throwaway WIP on the branch, merging, and squashing before the final commit. If a stash is unavoidable, name it (`git stash push -m <name>`) and address it only by name (`git stash pop stash^{/<name>}`), never by position.


## A map-generation change is measured against the maps it cannot reach (2026-09-04)

Any change to map generation is A/B'd on the whole seed set, and the maps the change CANNOT touch are the control: they must come back **byte-identical on every column of both owners**. If they move, the reasoning is wrong or the arms differ by more than the variable, and nothing else in the run can be trusted.

**Why:** it has paid twice in one day. Raising `DROP_OFF_ANCHOR_RADIUS` can only bite where the anchor was null, so `coastal` — nearest tree 5-7 cells — had to be unchanged, and was. Putting a woodline inside a wall can only reach a map that HAS a wall, so `aoe2-prototype`, `arabia`, `gold-rush` and `coastal` had to be unchanged, and were, across three different placement orderings. That last part is what made the three orderings comparable to each other at all. The one time a control did move, the cause was source edits landing between the two arms rather than a real effect.

**How to apply:** run `SEEDS=aoe2-prototype,arabia,arena,fortress,gold-rush,coastal TICKS=60000 SAMPLE=500 npm run ai:army-block` for both arms, back to back, in ONE background job that flips the variable itself and verifies `src` is unchanged at the end — never by editing between two hand-started runs. `diff` the control rows first and stop if they differ. The walled maps are `arena` and `fortress`; every other shipped seed is a control for anything that only reaches a wall.

## Several sessions, one repo: one writer per checkout (2026-09-03, owner)

The owner runs multiple Claude sessions against this repo at once — three peers were live when this was written, two in aoe2 and one in fleet. The rule that makes that safe is not a habit, it is a boundary: **a checkout has exactly ONE writing session at a time.** Everything else in this entry is damage control for when that boundary is crossed.

**Why:** every cross-session loss this repo has paid for came from two sessions sharing one working tree, never from two sessions in separate worktrees. A `git stash` drop that deleted a sibling's named WIP; `git stash push -- <dir>` that reverted five of another session's in-flight files to HEAD (`ae1ec54`); three merges aborted on a shared `dist/` race; `git add -A` that swept `fleet`'s canon sync into a defect-register commit whose message never mentioned it (2026-09-03, twice in one day); and `git reset --soft`, which restores HEAD byte-perfectly while deleting `.git/MERGE_HEAD`, so a merge in progress loses its second parent and the next commit records the content with the branch never marked merged. While this very entry was being written, `scripts/cropZoom.mjs`, `src/game/simulation/civProfiles.ts` and `tests/content/civProfiles.test.ts` appeared untracked in the shared checkout — a peer's unfinished work, one `git add -A` away from being committed by the wrong session under the wrong message.

**How to apply.**

- **Take a lane.** One session owns the shared checkout at `github/aoe2` and does the integrating — merges, version bumps, changelog, gates, pushes. Every other session works in its own `git worktree` on its own branch and hands the branch over; it never commits in the shared checkout. Use available coordination tools to establish the designated integration owner and observed peer status. `git worktree list` shows worktrees, not whether their owners are live; do not infer visibility or ownership from an unavailable tool.
- **In a shared checkout, never use a whole-tree command.** Stage by explicit pathspec (`git commit -m ... -- path/one path/two`). Not `git add -A`, not `git commit -a`. A pathspec is not a scope either: `git stash push -- <dir>` takes everything dirty under that directory, not your change within it.
- **No `git stash` at all while a peer is live.** The stash list is shared across every worktree of the repo, so positional `stash@{0}` pops and drops cross sessions. To bring `main` into a branch, commit a throwaway WIP, merge, and squash before the final commit. If a stash is truly unavoidable, name it (`git stash push -m <name>`) and address it only by name (`git stash pop stash^{/<name>}`).
- **Never run destructive git in a checkout you do not own** — `reset`, `checkout --`, `merge --abort`, `clean`. If you have already disturbed someone's tree, say which invariant you broke rather than that the state is restored.
- **One preview port per session, never the default.** `PREVIEW_PORT` is honoured by `npm run test:browser`, `captureMapScreenshot.mjs` and `diffMapScreenshots.mjs`; whatever squats on 4173 belongs to a sibling. Capturing from a sibling's preview measures THEIR build as yours, which is why the capture script refuses a server whose served asset hashes differ from this checkout's `dist/`. Check first with `netstat -ano | grep LISTENING | grep -oE ':4[0-9]{3}'`.
- **`dist/` is per checkout, not per session.** Two sessions running `npm run build` in one checkout race, and the loser's gate run measures the winner's bundle.
- **Pull before push; never force-push `main`.** Expect non-fast-forward rejections as normal traffic when peers are live.
- **A generated file changing under you is not a conflict to resolve.** `AGENTS.md`'s canon block is written by `npm run sync-canon` from `../fleet/FLEET.md`, and changed three times in one day here. Commit it on its own with a message that says so; never fold it into unrelated work.
- **Untracked files you did not create are someone's work in progress.** Leave them. Do not add them, do not clean them, do not assume they are debris.

## A feature's cost is its own share of tick time, never whole-match ticks per second (2026-09-05)

When a change is measured for cost, the number that counts is the change's OWN share of step time — its timer, or its inclusive share in a CPU profile — and, as the sanity check, ticks per second sampled INTERLEAVED (baseline, candidate, baseline, candidate, same map, same minute, machine confirmed quiet). Whole-match ticks per second over an A/B is not a cost measurement and must not be written up as one.

**Why:** the gather-walk A/B (v0.3.204) read as a 34% slowdown on the boot map from whole-match ticks per second, and the change's own timer put it at 0.2% of step time. The rest was two things ticks per second cannot separate: the candidate's economy built a bigger world (43 to 64 units on Black Forest), and an orphaned `chrome-headless-shell` was burning twelve CPU-minutes per minute, so identical baseline work measured 366 and 268 ticks per second forty minutes apart. Interleaved on a quiet machine the candidate was not slower at equal ticks — and even that sample compares worlds of different size (a critic found arena at 29 units under the candidate against 48 in the baseline at the same tick), so interleaving removes the machine and nothing else. Equal WORK is the premise, and only the feature's own timer has it.

**How to apply:** `npm run ai:gather-walk` prints the walk field's rebuild count and wall time from the debug snapshot and milliseconds per thousand unit-ticks beside ticks per second — read those first. Before any timing sample, list the processes with real CPU time (`tasklist /FO CSV /V` through `node -e`, since the worktree shell guard refuses `powershell`) and kill nothing you do not own; report the load instead. A CPU profile is one command: `node --cpu-prof --cpu-prof-dir=<dir> --import tsx <runner>` on a 30,000-tick run, summed by inclusive share per file pattern. Candidate for the fleet canon; staged here because the measurement was made here.

## A thread is closed by the task that closes it, and every task ends with a look at the open ones (2026-09-05, owner)

`docs/threads/current` holds objectives that are being worked on, and nothing else. The commit that ships or drops an objective moves its thread to `docs/threads/done/` with a closure line under the title (`Closed YYYY-MM-DD: <version or decision>`), fixes every pointer to the old path, and gives each leftover follow-up a home: the lessons queue if it names a gate, the register if it is a defect, or an explicit drop with the reason. At the end of every task, each thread in `current/` that the task advanced gets a dated status line saying what moved and what is still open, and each one whose objective has since shipped or been dropped is moved.

**Why:** the owner asked (2026-09-05) whether the project really had so many open threads. It did not. Six of the eight folders in `current/` were finished work: three shipped on 2026-07-15 and were never moved (`auto-mine-after-camp` was CREATED by a commit titled "close auto-mine"), one had been dismantled by the 2026-08-01 decision that removed the fix arm, one shipped in August and one was scoped to a single improvement that had shipped. Two code comments still pointed at threads moved months earlier: one had survived the 2026-06-09 housekeeping review because that sweep read `src/` only, and the other was written six days AFTER it by the very commit that moved its thread — a move is a rename, and the commit that renames is the one most likely to write the old path. A reader of `current/` would have taken shipped work for open work, and the one thread that WAS open, `lumber-camp-routing`, stopped at round 5 while three versions of follow-on work went into the register and the devlog. Closing was the LAST step of a plan (PLAN step 9, "move thread folder to done") instead of part of the shipping commit, and nothing read `current/` at the end of a task, so a thread not moved in its own commit was never moved. Register entry: 2026-09-05.

**How to apply:** closing is part of shipping, so it happens in the same commit, never as a later sweep. `tests/architecture/threadHygiene.test.ts` is the gate, three checks. (1) Every `docs/threads/(current|done)/...` path referenced from code, tests, scripts, the spec, the architecture docs, the policies and the learning docs must exist on disk with its exact casing, and the scan must find at least three real pointers in each of `src`, `tests`, `design` and `docs/architecture`, so a scan that reads less than it claims goes red. The devlog, the changelog, the debugging logs and the threads themselves are exempt by this rule's own decision, not by an earlier ruling: they narrate moves and pending states as they were ("the folder moves from `current/` to `done/` in this commit"), so a literal scan of them cannot be satisfied without falsifying the narrative. (2) No file under `current/` may carry a `Closed YYYY-MM-DD:` line: the ritual writes that line and moves the folder in one commit, so a closure line still under `current/` is the move forgotten — and without this check the closure line's own date would satisfy (3) forever. (3) Every thread in `current/` must carry a `YYYY-MM-DD` date, in a file or a folder name, within 30 days of the newer of HEAD's commit date and the newest date in `docs/devlog/summary.md`, so a thread a month of commits has not written a dated line into goes red until it gets a status line or a move. Said plainly: (2) catches a thread closed by the ritual at once, (3) catches the rest only after 30 days, and on the morning this was written two of the six (19 and 3 days old) would have passed (3) alone. (3) reads dates as text — a deadline or a version's date counts as maintenance, and a false status line dated today passes. Candidate for the fleet canon: seven repos use the `docs/threads` convention and the constitution does not mention it; staged here because the defect was paid for here.

## Several workers in one checkout: disjoint files, and ONE gate run over the combined tree (2026-09-05)

When several workers are given work in this one shared checkout at the same time, each gets a **disjoint set of files**, **no worker runs `npm run verify`**, and a single integrator rebuilds `dist/` and runs the gate ONCE over the combined tree before the batch of commits that ships it. Every commit in that batch says plainly that the gate covered the combined tree rather than that commit alone. This is the concurrent-worker case of "one writer per checkout" above, not an exception to it: there is still exactly one session integrating, and the workers are its hands.

**Why:** three separate costs, all paid on 2026-09-05.

- `npm run verify` takes about **20 minutes**, seventeen of them the browser suite. Four workers each running it is over an hour of serialized wall time to integrate one change, and every one of those runs measures a tree that is about to change anyway.
- **`npm run build` regenerates `dist/` FROM THE SHARED TREE.** A worker that builds bakes a peer's half-finished code into the bundle the browser suite then tests — the suite is served from `dist/`, so it is no longer testing what that worker wrote. This happened here and forced a rebuild before the real gate could be trusted.
- **Concurrent heavy lanes make the gate less trustworthy, not more.** Two browser specs went red purely from machine load that day and passed when re-run alone. A red run that is really a busy machine costs the same investigation as a real defect, and teaches the next reader to discount red.

**How to apply.**

- **Name the peers' owned paths in every assignment**, not just the worker's own. A worker who knows which files are not its own can tell a peer's in-flight work from its own breakage.
- **Tell workers that a peer's in-flight file can make `tsc` red.** The instruction is to check that their own files are absent from the error list and carry on, rather than to fix or revert a file they do not own.
- **One lane owns the browser pane, the preview port and `dist/`.** Everyone else gets a `PREVIEW_PORT` away from 4173 and 4199, and does not build. A worker that needs to see something rendered asks the lane that owns the pane.
- **Verify `git diff --cached --name-only` before every commit in the batch**, against the file set that commit is supposed to carry. Staging is by explicit pathspec, as "Several sessions, one repo" above requires; the check is that the staged set is what you meant, because the tree holds other people's work.

## The defect register rolls over, and a live defect never rolls over (2026-09-05)

`docs/learning/defect-register.md` holds the **12 most recent CLOSED entries plus every OPEN one, whatever its age**, newest first. Everything older and closed is in `docs/learning/defect-register-past.md`, moved verbatim. The rollover is done by the task whose new entry pushes the active file past 12 closed, in that task's own commit — never a later sweep, exactly as the devlog archives its detailed file past 500 lines. An entry leaves the active file by being CLOSED, not by ageing: a heading that says the defect is still live is not a closure however it spells it.

**Why:** every other long-lived document here already rolls over — the devlog archives its detailed file, engine-feedback splits `current`/`past`, threads split `current`/`done` — and the register did not, so it reached 1,437 lines and 58 entries before anything split it, while its whole value is that it stays readable. The first rollover then buried THREE live defects, because "(SUPERSEDED … still unfixed)", "(PARTLY FIXED: …)" and "(LATENT, not fixed)" all read as closures to a classifier looking for the word FIXED; they were moved back with `, OPEN` added inside the trailing group and nothing else changed. The rule met its first real use in the same session that wrote it, and the gate caught the rollover being got wrong three times.

**How to apply:** `tests/architecture/defectRegisterRollover.test.ts` is the gate, in `npm test` and so in `npm run verify`. Write every entry heading flush left, exactly `## `, and put the status inside a trailing parenthesised group — `(OPEN` unclosed, `[OPEN]`, `(OPEN).` and a trailing `— OPEN` are invisible to the marker parser and go red as ambiguous rather than passing as closed, which is the direction that would let a live defect roll over. A rollover MOVES an entry and never copies it, and each half's header must name the other. Two consequences of the gate's own bounds, both stated in its header and in `gate-proofs.md`: it does not require the twelve kept entries to be the twelve NEWEST — keeping any twelve closed passes — so **oldest first** is a convention this rule states rather than something the gate enforces; and the oldest closed entry is not always the one to move, because an entry whose marker leaves the defect live (`(PROVEN, fix measured and NOT shipped)` stands in the active file today) is allowed where the reader can see it and goes red the moment it is buried. Move the oldest closed entry the gate will accept.

**Candidate for the fleet canon**, staged here because the defect was paid for here: eleven repos under `github/` keep a `docs/learning/defect-register.md` and this is the only one with an archive beside it (measured 2026-09-05 — `ls ../*/docs/learning/defect-register*.md`, 11 active files, 1 `-past.md`).

## A batch of commits split from one gated tree: every intermediate state gets the cheap checks (2026-09-06)

When several commits are split out of ONE tree that passed `npm run verify` once, the gate covers the final state and nothing before it — every commit but the last is unproven. So the cheap per-commit checks run against EACH intermediate state as it is staged, and the split itself is verified by proving the reconstructed final tree is byte-identical to the tree the gate passed on.

**Why:** on 2026-09-06 five commits were split out of one gated tree, and the third would have shipped a defect-register entry standing in BOTH the active file and the archive at once — a rollover half-applied, which is exactly what `tests/architecture/defectRegisterRollover.test.ts` exists to catch. It was caught by the worker reading its own staged set, not by any gate, and the worker's own words are the rule: *no gate would have caught it, because the gate ran on the final state only.* By the final state the duplicate was gone, so a green `npm run verify` was a true statement about the only tree nobody would ever check out. This is not a rollover problem, it is the shape of every batch here: `npm run verify` costs 20-25 minutes, most of it the browser suite, so this repo deliberately gates a combined tree ONCE (see "Several workers in one checkout" above) and splits the commits afterwards for a readable history — and the readable history it produces is a sequence of states nothing ever checked. Anyone who bisects, reverts, or checks out an intermediate commit lands on one of them.

**How to apply.**

- **Run the architecture suite against every intermediate state, not only the last.** `npx vitest run tests/architecture --reporter=dot` is 1.4 s of wall time for 34 tests across 8 files (measured 2026-09-06), and it holds precisely the invariants a split breaks: the register rollover, thread hygiene, and the file-size budget. The loop is stage by explicit pathspec, run it, read the exit code directly, commit, repeat. It is cheap enough that there is no argument for skipping it; everything more expensive stays where it is, once, on the combined tree.
- **Prove the split reconstructs the gated tree exactly.** Before the first commit, snapshot the gated file set — copy it to the scratchpad, or record `git hash-object` per path — and after the last commit diff the tree against that snapshot, requiring EMPTY. Compare trees, not diffs: a hunk that lands in the wrong commit of the batch is invisible in every per-commit diff and shows up only here.
- **Say plainly what this does NOT buy.** An intermediate commit still has not run `npm run verify` — not the browser suite, not the build, not the full unit suite — and it cannot without paying the 20-25 minutes. What this buys is the architecture invariants at every step plus an exact final reconstruction, and nothing beyond that. A bisect that lands inside a batch is still landing on a state whose full gate was never run, so its result is a hypothesis about the batch rather than a fact about that commit. Every commit in the batch still says that the gate covered the combined tree rather than that commit alone, per "Several workers in one checkout" above.

## The coordinator's operating mode, and the contract every implementer assignment inherits (2026-09-06, owner)

The owner asked for a named operating mode for this repo's coordinator and its implementer subagents. It has two halves: what the coordinator does and never does, and a standing contract an assignment inherits by writing "the implementer contract applies" and then adding only what is task-specific.

**Why:** about thirty subagents ran here on 2026-09-06, and the same eight or nine requirements were retyped into every assignment. That is how a clause goes missing — it survives only as long as whoever writes the next brief remembers it. Every clause below was paid for, most of them that same day, and each one carries its incident, because a rule with its war story attached survives and a bare imperative does not.

**Half one — what the coordinator does.**

- **It never implements.** Its hands stay on reading enough to write an assignment and on inspecting a handoff to accept or reject it. That is fleet canon already (owner directive, 2026-09-05); it is restated here only as the anchor for the rest.
- **It sources the next task by PLAYING, never by reading code for something to improve.** This is the standing-loop entry point above, seen from the coordinator's chair. On 2026-09-06 four fixed defects were found by playing and the owner reported none of them; the day before, play found that about a third of the painted command bar passed clicks straight through to the map — 1,065 of 3,567 painted points at 1280x800 resolved to the world canvas — hours after v0.3.207 (`bf5ff47f`) had shipped green over that same panel and, by making the bar a fixed painted band, grown the hole. A green gate over a panel is not a report about that panel (register, 2026-09-05).
- **It runs three or four lanes with disjoint file ownership, and serialises the expensive gate to ONE integrator per batch.** The mechanics are "Several workers in one checkout" above: `npm run verify` is 20-25 minutes, so four workers each running it is over an hour of serialized wall time measuring trees that are about to change anyway, and a worker that builds bakes a peer's half-finished code into the bundle the browser suite then tests.
- **It commits as work is accepted, not in a pile at the end.** On 2026-09-06 five commits were split out of one gated tree and the third would have shipped a defect-register entry standing in the active file and the archive at once — caught by a worker reading its own staged set, by no gate, because the gate had run on the final state where the duplicate was already gone. The cheap per-commit loop in the entry above is the repair; committing as each piece is accepted is what keeps the pile small enough for that loop to be worth anything.
- **It verifies a fact before passing it on. A brief is a claim, not a fact.** This one has the most evidence. In one day the coordinator handed on at least five statements that were false and a worker or a later session caught every one: a spec described as "passing for two months on an action no player could perform", which was twelve days and pointed the other way — nothing had been passing wrongly at all, a layout change had invalidated the test's premise and a second defect concealed it; a register entry headed `(owner-reported)` for a match the coordinator's own read-only play session had played; two hazards about `advanceTicks` — that it throws the engine's re-entrancy error, and that it under-advances when the simulation is not paused — written down from a tick delta of zero, repeated in worker assignments as established fact, and refuted by measuring the live dev server (3,000 requested, 3,000 advanced, no throw); and a test count of 197 quoted into a paragraph about the green CI run, which belonged to the red one and was really 199. None of these had a gate that could see them. They sit in prose, they are adjacent and plausible, and they are load-bearing for whoever builds on them.
- **It decides rather than blocks.** Routine ambiguity is resolved with the standing tiebreak — behaviour matches Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition — and a reversible default, not by asking. It stops for destructive acts and for product calls that are genuinely the owner's; how much of a small window the command bar may occupy is one such call and is recorded OPEN as the owner's rather than settled by the coordinator (register, 2026-09-05).

**Half two — the implementer contract.**

- **Reproduce in the real game before writing a fixture.** The two `advanceTicks` hazards above came from reading a tick delta of zero rather than from driving the thing. What actually under-advances silently is a different mechanism entirely — a match paused through the real Esc menu, where `advanceTicks(3000)` delivers 0 ticks and throws nothing — and only driving the real menu found it.
- **Fix the class, not the instance.** One report of a building that ate its wood and never got built became a check over the whole map: ten unreachable cells on the starting map at tick 600, plus the same defect pointing the other way, four of twelve refusals naming ground nobody could reach. One greyed-out age-up button became refusal reasons for research, train, build and market — all four command kinds — computed at the bridge so a tooltip cannot disagree with the validator that would reject the same click.
- **Name the instrument that answers the question before writing a probe.** The hook in `.claude/settings.json` refuses a probe with no `harness:` line, and AGENTS.md's Debugging list is the list to name from. Note that the list was rewritten on 2026-09-06 because all three tools it used to name were wrong in three different ways: `replay:inspect` was presented as an engine capability and is this repo's own script, `createOccupancyDebugProbe` cannot attach here at all, and `createPathQueueDebugProbe` wants stats from a path queue this game does not have. An agent that reaches for the advertised tool, finds it does not fit, and never reaches again is behaving correctly — so if the named instrument does not fit, say so in the handoff and record it, rather than quietly falling back to a scratch probe.
- **Prove the gate red by mutation, and check that the mutation is not racing what it means to disprove.** On 2026-09-06 a falsifiability proof PASSED when it should have failed: the mutation froze the world at tick 11 rather than at zero, so the poll it was meant to break succeeded for the wrong reason. A mutation that races the condition it is trying to disprove proves nothing, and its green is indistinguishable from the green of a working gate. The other shape of the same error, met the same day: a case that stays green with the defect reintroduced because both sides are the same code agreeing with itself. And a third shape, met on 2026-09-11 in the Escape-layer work and worth the line it takes: **a green mutation is a no-op until you have shown it changed behaviour, and a no-op mutation is pixel-identical to a hole in the gate.** The first attempt at reversing the layer order swapped only the two `name:` lines of the layer records — display strings the arbitration never reads — so the spec stayed green, and the obvious reading was that the gate could not see the defect. It could: swapping the two BLOCKS, which is what actually reorders the stack, failed with `expect(locator).toBeHidden() failed / Expected: hidden / Received: visible`. The check that separates the two costs one step — before reporting a gate hole, confirm the mutated build BEHAVES differently (drive it, or assert the mutated value is one the code under test actually reads), because "the gate did not notice" and "there was nothing to notice" produce the same green.
- **State the bound — what a green run does not prove — in the gate's own header and in the handoff.** The placement gate is ONE map at ONE tick, land builders only, footprints of 1x1 and 2x2 of which only the 1x1 sweep can detect anything. The command-availability gate names the six commands that still refuse silently. A bound is not an apology; it is the part of the report that tells the next reader where to look.
- **Measure rather than argue, and verify the instrument before trusting the measurement.** Three self-caught cases on 2026-09-06, each one command from being missed: a visual comparison whose "before" arm answered with a sentence the base build never had, so the pixel diff came back 0.00% and measured nothing; the 197 quoted from the red CI run into a paragraph about the green one, caught by asking the run for its own count instead of reusing the number already on the page; and a three-way configuration comparison that would have been refreshed in one row only, assembling the comparison out of two different trees. The A/B rule above is the same point from the other side: arms that differ by more than the variable under test — including because the tree moved between them — produce a result that reads exactly like a finding.
- **Never weaken an assertion, raise a timeout or move a budget to get green. Report instead.** Two browser specs failed on the slower CI runner on 2026-09-06 and both were fixed by making them measure what they claimed to measure: the clock step now polls for the world to actually advance past the tick it read, and the stack-point scan aims at the middle of the qualifying region and fails by name below two screen pixels, taking its margin from 0.30 px to 6.00 px. No existing assertion was changed or weakened; one was added. The open risk recorded beside them says it plainly — if CI trips the two heavy combat specs, the answer is not a raised timeout. Where something does come off a floor, it is a deferral only if a control proves it: `blast-furnace` stayed on the coverage floor at the same tier and passed, which is what made the four removed names a gold-timing problem rather than a weakened gate.
- **Never end the turn while a gate is running.** `npm run verify` is 20-25 minutes here and the architecture suite is about 1.4 s; either way the run belongs to the worker who started it. A gate that finishes unattended has to have its result recovered from a log by someone who did not run it, and until then nobody can say whether the work is done.
- **Report what you did NOT check.** An area nobody opened is not a clean area, and a handoff that lists only what passed reads as if the rest was looked at. Say which files, viewports, states and paths were never exercised.
- **Correct the coordinator when the brief is wrong.** Every worker that did so on 2026-09-06 was right. A worker that treats the brief as authoritative builds on a false premise and the premise is the expensive part — a brief is the coordinator's current belief, and the worker is usually the first person to hold the actual file.

## A browser spec asserts on frames rendered or on game state, never on ground covered inside a wall-clock window (2026-09-06)

No browser spec may require that a quantity move by some amount within a fixed `waitForTimeout`. Hold the input WHILE polling for the state you actually mean and release it in a `finally`; or wait on `waitForRenderedFrames` when the claim is that something did NOT move, because a sleep containing no frames cannot tell "held still" from "never rendered". A duration threshold is NOT available as a gate here, and this rule replaces the lessons-queue entry that asked for one.

**Why.** `game-hud-and-camera-camera.spec.ts` held D for 250 ms and required `scrollX` to grow by more than 40. CI read exactly 35, and 35 is not luck: keyboard pan is `deltaMs / 1000 × 420 / zoom` (`KEYBOARD_SCROLL_SPEED_PX_PER_SECOND`) and `AoeVoxelGameView.frame` clamps `cameraDeltaMs` to `MAX_CAMERA_FRAME_DELTA_MS` = 100, so one rendered frame contributes at most `100/1000 × 420 / 1.2` = 35.00 px at boot zoom. Requiring more than 40 required TWO frames inside 250 ms — an eight-frames-per-second requirement written as a camera assertion. Five more specs had the same shape, and the worst was a scroll helper in `playOpening.ts` that covered 0.0 px on a starved host and then chose its direction from what amounted to a coin toss.

**Why the threshold was refused, with the numbers.** The per-spec duration budget was built, wired into `playwright.config.ts`, and proven to fire. Then two full-suite runs on the SAME machine and the SAME bundle, nothing else changed, gave 95/59/46/40/39 and 48/47/44/41/38 percent of budget for the five specs closest to their timeouts. One test went 28.4 s → 14.3 s and another moved the OTHER way, so it is not one load factor scaling everything — it is per-test and it points in both directions. A threshold above the worst observation lands past 100%, which is the timeout that already exists; anything below it goes red because somebody was compiling.

**How to apply.** The standing instrument is `tests/browser/helpers/durationBudgetReporter.ts`, a REPORT rather than a gate: four lines at the end of every `npm run test:browser` naming the five specs closest to their own timeouts. Those five names are where to look first when the suite gets slower, and they are the honest substitute for the gate. `BROWSER_DURATION_BUDGET=0.8` opts into failing on it for a deliberate investigation; do not put it in CI on the strength of these two runs, whose bound is ONE machine — the CI runner's distribution is unmeasured. When a spec is genuinely too slow, fix what it does rather than raising its timeout: `patrol-interaction.spec.ts:49` cannot be fixed inside the 30 s timeout without raising it and was deliberately LEFT rather than quietly given more room, and `game-combat-and-meta-meta.spec.ts:5` sits at 95% of budget and was deliberately not chunked, because a coarser break check would change what it asserts. One instrument note worth keeping: CPU throttling is the WRONG control for this class — at 20x it also delays CDP input handling, so the keyup lands late, the key stays down far longer than the spec intends, and the broken form covered 175 px and PASSED. Frame starvation is the right one: replace `requestAnimationFrame` with a 300 ms `setTimeout` shim, which starves rendering without touching input timing.

## A coverage floor names a PRODUCER, and a census is compared both ways (2026-09-10)

No self-play gate may put a specific PURCHASE on a floor — a named technology, unit or building type that a match must reach. A floor over a match names the PRODUCER and asks it to produce anything from its own list. Whether a particular row of the content tables can be bought AT ALL is a separate, price-free gate that drives the real command path (`queue.research`, `queue.train`, `building.placeConfirm`) with a purse nothing can exhaust. And any census a self-play gate keeps is compared for EQUALITY against a checked-in expectation that prints gains and losses in both directions, so a gain costs the same attention as a loss and the commit that re-records the file has to say which way the AI moved.

**Why.** `runProductionPhase` buys the FIRST entry on a producer's option list it can pay for, so a named technology asserts that a particular purse existed at a tick that producer happened to be idle — never that the content is reachable. The two most expensive entries on a list are bought only out of a surplus the AI cannot spend on anything else. On `a30af4fa` owner 2 bought `onager-upgrade` (800 food, 500 wood) and `heavy-scorpion-upgrade` (1,000 food, 1,100 wood) while holding 7 gold, as upgrades to units it could never train; two AI improvements gave it gold to spend, it stopped buying them, and the coverage gate went RED while coverage went UP — 29 technologies against the floor's 28, the same 15 buildings and 17 units. The one-way floor also hid the other half of the same trade: `plate-mail-armor` and `plate-barding`, removed from that floor three days earlier *as gold-starved*, came back in the same change. The 2026-09-10 register entry carries the reconstruction.

**No gate can carry this**, which is why it is here rather than in the lessons queue: nothing mechanical can tell a floor that names a producer from one that names a purchase, and the three pipeline gates plus the two-way snapshot fix this instance without making the class impossible. What a reviewer can check is one question — *could this assertion be satisfied by the subject being BROKE?* — and the answer for a named purchase is always yes.
