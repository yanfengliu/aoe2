# A coverage floor names a PRODUCER, and a census is compared both ways (2026-09-10)

One of [this repo's local rules](../local-rules.md), where "above" and "below" mean earlier and later in its list.

No self-play gate may put a specific PURCHASE on a floor — a named technology, unit or building type that a match must reach. A floor over a match names the PRODUCER and asks it to produce anything from its own list. Whether a particular row of the content tables can be bought AT ALL is a separate, price-free gate that drives the real command path (`queue.research`, `queue.train`, `building.placeConfirm`) with a purse nothing can exhaust. And any census a self-play gate keeps is compared for EQUALITY against a checked-in expectation that prints gains and losses in both directions, so a gain costs the same attention as a loss and the commit that re-records the file has to say which way the AI moved.

**Why.** `runProductionPhase` buys the FIRST entry on a producer's option list it can pay for, so a named technology asserts that a particular purse existed at a tick that producer happened to be idle — never that the content is reachable. The two most expensive entries on a list are bought only out of a surplus the AI cannot spend on anything else. On `a30af4fa` owner 2 bought `onager-upgrade` (800 food, 500 wood — itself a mispricing: `technologies.csv` and DE ask 500 gold, corrected 2026-09-23) and `heavy-scorpion-upgrade` (1,000 food, 1,100 wood) while holding 7 gold, as upgrades to units it could never train; two AI improvements gave it gold to spend, it stopped buying them, and the coverage gate went RED while coverage went UP — 29 technologies against the floor's 28, the same 15 buildings and 17 units. The one-way floor also hid the other half of the same trade: `plate-mail-armor` and `plate-barding`, removed from that floor three days earlier *as gold-starved*, came back in the same change. The 2026-09-10 register entry carries the reconstruction.

**No gate can carry this**, which is why it is here rather than in the lessons queue: nothing mechanical can tell a floor that names a producer from one that names a purchase, and the three pipeline gates plus the two-way snapshot fix this instance without making the class impossible. What a reviewer can check is one question — *could this assertion be satisfied by the subject being BROKE?* — and the answer for a named purchase is always yes.
