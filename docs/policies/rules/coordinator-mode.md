# The coordinator's operating mode, and the contract every implementer assignment inherits (2026-09-06, owner)

One of [this repo's local rules](../local-rules.md), where "above" and "below" mean earlier and later in its list.

The owner asked for a named operating mode for this repo's coordinator and its implementer subagents. It has two halves: what the coordinator does and never does, and a standing contract an assignment inherits by writing "the implementer contract applies" and then adding only what is task-specific.

**Why:** about thirty subagents ran here on 2026-09-06, and the same eight or nine requirements were retyped into every assignment. That is how a clause goes missing — it survives only as long as whoever writes the next brief remembers it. Every clause below was paid for, most of them that same day, and each one carries its incident, because a rule with its war story attached survives and a bare imperative does not.

**Half one — what the coordinator does.**

- **It delegates every change except a trivial one.** Fleet canon [R2] (owner directive, 2026-09-23, superseding the 2026-09-05 "never implements"): the coordinator may make a change itself when delegating would cost more than the change, where trivial means at most about 20 changed lines of docs, config or an integration fix, nothing high-risk, and no product logic. The same gates, review and worktree rules apply. Everything else still goes to a worker, and its own hands otherwise stay on writing assignments and inspecting handoffs.
- **It sources the next task by PLAYING, never by reading code for something to improve.** This is the standing-loop entry point above, seen from the coordinator's chair. On 2026-09-06 four fixed defects were found by playing and the owner reported none of them; the day before, play found that about a third of the painted command bar passed clicks straight through to the map — 1,065 of 3,567 painted points at 1280x800 resolved to the world canvas — hours after v0.3.207 (`bf5ff47f`) had shipped green over that same panel and, by making the bar a fixed painted band, grown the hole. A green gate over a panel is not a report about that panel (register, 2026-09-05).
- **It runs three or four lanes, each in its own worktree made by `scripts/controlWorktree.mjs` (never the harness's `isolation: "worktree"`), with disjoint file ownership, and serialises the expensive gate to ONE integrator per batch.** The mechanics are "Several workers in one checkout" above: `npm run verify` is 20-25 minutes, so four workers each running it is over an hour of serialized wall time measuring trees that are about to change anyway, and a worker that builds bakes a peer's half-finished code into the bundle the browser suite then tests.
- **It commits as work is accepted, not in a pile at the end.** On 2026-09-06 five commits were split out of one gated tree and the third would have shipped a defect-register entry standing in the active file and the archive at once — caught by a worker reading its own staged set, by no gate, because the gate had run on the final state where the duplicate was already gone. The cheap per-commit loop in the entry above is the repair; committing as each piece is accepted is what keeps the pile small enough for that loop to be worth anything.
- **It verifies a fact before passing it on. A brief is a claim, not a fact.** This one has the most evidence. In one day the coordinator handed on at least five statements that were false and a worker or a later session caught every one: a spec described as "passing for two months on an action no player could perform", which was twelve days and pointed the other way — nothing had been passing wrongly at all, a layout change had invalidated the test's premise and a second defect concealed it; a register entry headed `(owner-reported)` for a match the coordinator's own read-only play session had played; two hazards about `advanceTicks` — that it throws the engine's re-entrancy error, and that it under-advances when the simulation is not paused — written down from a tick delta of zero, repeated in worker assignments as established fact, and refuted by measuring the live dev server (3,000 requested, 3,000 advanced, no throw); and a test count of 197 quoted into a paragraph about the green CI run, which belonged to the red one and was really 199. None of these had a gate that could see them. They sit in prose, they are adjacent and plausible, and they are load-bearing for whoever builds on them.
- **It decides rather than blocks.** Routine ambiguity is resolved with the standing tiebreak — behaviour matches Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition — and a reversible default, not by asking. It stops for destructive acts and for product calls that are genuinely the owner's; how much of a small window the command bar may occupy is one such call and is recorded OPEN as the owner's rather than settled by the coordinator (register, 2026-09-05).

**Half two — the implementer contract.**

- **Reproduce in the real game before writing a fixture.** The two `advanceTicks` hazards above came from reading a tick delta of zero rather than from driving the thing. What actually under-advances silently is a different mechanism entirely — a match paused through the real Esc menu, where `advanceTicks(3000)` delivers 0 ticks and throws nothing — and only driving the real menu found it.
- **Fix the class, not the instance.** One report of a building that ate its wood and never got built became a check over the whole map: ten unreachable cells on the starting map at tick 600, plus the same defect pointing the other way, four of twelve refusals naming ground nobody could reach. One greyed-out age-up button became refusal reasons for research, train, build and market — all four command kinds — computed at the bridge so a tooltip cannot disagree with the validator that would reject the same click.
- **Name the instrument that answers the question before writing a probe.** The hook in `.claude/settings.json` refuses a probe with no `harness:` line, and AGENTS.md's Debugging list is the list to name from. Note that the list was rewritten on 2026-09-06 because all three tools it used to name were wrong in three different ways: `replay:inspect` was presented as an engine capability and is this repo's own script, `createOccupancyDebugProbe` cannot attach here at all, and `createPathQueueDebugProbe` wants stats from a path queue this game does not have. An agent that reaches for the advertised tool, finds it does not fit, and never reaches again is behaving correctly — so if the named instrument does not fit, say so in the handoff and record it, rather than quietly falling back to a scratch probe.
- **Prove the gate red by mutation, and check that the mutation is not racing what it means to disprove.** On 2026-09-06 a falsifiability proof PASSED when it should have failed: the mutation froze the world at tick 11 rather than at zero, so the poll it was meant to break succeeded for the wrong reason. A mutation that races the condition it is trying to disprove proves nothing, and its green is indistinguishable from the green of a working gate. The other shape of the same error, met the same day: a case that stays green with the defect reintroduced because both sides are the same code agreeing with itself. And a third shape, met on 2026-09-11 in the Escape-layer work and worth the line it takes: **a green mutation is a no-op until you have shown it changed behaviour, and a no-op mutation is pixel-identical to a hole in the gate.** The first attempt at reversing the layer order swapped only the two `name:` lines of the layer records — display strings the arbitration never reads — so the spec stayed green, and the obvious reading was that the gate could not see the defect. It could: swapping the two BLOCKS, which is what actually reorders the stack, failed with `expect(locator).toBeHidden() failed / Expected: hidden / Received: visible`. The check that separates the two costs one step — before reporting a gate hole, confirm the mutated build BEHAVES differently (drive it, or assert the mutated value is one the code under test actually reads), because "the gate did not notice" and "there was nothing to notice" produce the same green.
- **State the bound — what a green run does not prove — in the gate's own header and in the handoff.** The placement gate is ONE map at ONE tick, land builders only, footprints of 1x1 and 2x2 of which only the 1x1 sweep can detect anything. The command-availability gate names the six commands that still refuse silently. A bound is not an apology; it is the part of the report that tells the next reader where to look.
- **Measure rather than argue, and verify the instrument before trusting the measurement.** Three self-caught cases on 2026-09-06, each one command from being missed: a visual comparison whose "before" arm answered with a sentence the base build never had, so the pixel diff came back 0.00% and measured nothing; the 197 quoted from the red CI run into a paragraph about the green one, caught by asking the run for its own count instead of reusing the number already on the page; and a three-way configuration comparison that would have been refreshed in one row only, assembling the comparison out of two different trees. The A/B rule above is the same point from the other side: arms that differ by more than the variable under test — including because the tree moved between them — produce a result that reads exactly like a finding.
- **Never weaken an assertion, raise a timeout or move a budget to get green. Report instead.** Two browser specs failed on the slower CI runner on 2026-09-06 and both were fixed by making them measure what they claimed to measure: the clock step now polls for the world to actually advance past the tick it read, and the stack-point scan aims at the middle of the qualifying region and fails by name below two screen pixels, taking its margin from 0.30 px to 6.00 px. No existing assertion was changed or weakened; one was added. The open risk recorded beside them says it plainly — if CI trips the two heavy combat specs, the answer is not a raised timeout. Where something does come off a floor, it is a deferral only if a control proves it: `blast-furnace` stayed on the coverage floor at the same tier and passed, which is what made the four removed names a gold-timing problem rather than a weakened gate.
- **Never end the turn while a gate is running.** `npm run verify` is 20-25 minutes here and the architecture suite is about 1.4 s; either way the run belongs to the worker who started it. A gate that finishes unattended has to have its result recovered from a log by someone who did not run it, and until then nobody can say whether the work is done.
- **Report what you did NOT check.** An area nobody opened is not a clean area, and a handoff that lists only what passed reads as if the rest was looked at. Say which files, viewports, states and paths were never exercised.
- **Correct the coordinator when the brief is wrong.** Every worker that did so on 2026-09-06 was right. A worker that treats the brief as authoritative builds on a false premise and the premise is the expensive part — a brief is the coordinator's current belief, and the worker is usually the first person to hold the actual file.
