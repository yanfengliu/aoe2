# AoE2 coverage roadmap — from vertical slice to the full game

The goal is the complete, publicly-known Age of Empires II (Definitive Edition / HD) experience, not the current early-game slice. `design/spec-final.md` is the TARGET (the rules); this file is the prioritized GAP between the current implementation and that target, and it is the backlog the LLM-playtest loop works through (run → `playtest:findings` → implement → re-run). It is derived from a full simulation-coverage audit (2026-06-13) that grounded every "implemented" claim in running code.

As of 2026-06-15 the north-star also covers VISUAL fidelity and UI polish toward a perfect Age of Empires II HD clone (see M7), so the loop advances three tracks — simulation correctness, graphics realism, UI polish — picking whichever has the highest payoff per iteration. All visual work uses ORIGINAL/procedural art that evokes the AoE2 look (isometric view, textured terrain, wood-and-stone HUD); no copyrighted Age of Empires assets (sprites, textures, UI art, audio) are reproduced. **HUD styling direction changed 2026-08-18 (owner):** the interface chrome targets a MODERN look — dark glass, one hairline, soft elevation, a token palette — rather than the AoE2-era wood-and-stone frame. The WORLD still targets AoE2 fidelity; only the HUD surface direction changed.

## Current state (audit headline)

A complete but **shallow** land-only 1v1 vertical slice: Dark → Feudal → Castle → Imperial all work, with a working economy, blacksmith + unit-upgrade tech, abstracted combat, monks, four win conditions, and a competent AI. Breadth coverage vs. the full game: **units 34/104, technologies 66/140, buildings 18/27, five civilizations with active bonuses (all 30 selectable), naval 0, victory 4/4.** (Building count re-derived 2026-08-23 against `structures.csv` itself, and then **closed**: every one of its 28 names is now in the game — the four that looked missing, Fortified Wall / Guard Tower / Keep / Gate, are UPGRADES of existing buildings and shipped as technologies; the Outpost landed in v0.3.39 and the Fish Trap in v0.3.40. A roster count that reads an upgrade row as a missing building overstates the work by a factor of two.) The slice is real and playable — it is depth and breadth that are missing.

Shipped alongside this re-grounding as backlog progress: the movement-speed seam + Husbandry/Squires/villager-speed (v0.1.66/68/69) and the Bloodlines conformance fix (v0.1.67).

- **AI economy, traffic-layer root cause (2026-08-20, v0.3.30) — the AI was never stuck on GATHERING; it was refused permission to MOVE.** A per-unit count of the traffic decision (not the transform, not the gather state) showed all 18 villagers on `wait` 300 ticks out of 300, zero moves. Five defects, all shipped: three in `movementTrafficOps` — the dependency closure aborted on an already-visited peer so a jam where several units share a cell elected nobody; a unit yielded to a co-located peer that was itself permanently blocked; and, once the closure DID elect a winner, the per-cell rules re-judged and vetoed that winner, which is a circular refusal — plus a spawn-egress rule one neighbour deep that let the AI strand seven of eleven units in a two-cell pocket it had sealed with its own buildings, and an unbounded gather fan-out that sent villagers forty to sixty cells into the enemy's base to die (twenty lost in one match). Measured at tick 30000 on the default map: villagers 20 frozen → 21 working, food 139 → 1010, units 20 → 25, and 9000 ticks run in 47s where clean main took 91s (the reachability probe was re-running per stuck villager per tick).
- **The "forward lumber-camp placement" fix recommended above was BUILT, MEASURED TWICE, AND REVERTED (2026-08-20). Do not retry it as stated.** Siting drop-off buildings at the resource they serve — the AoE2-standard behaviour, and this roadmap's own next-fix recommendation since 2026-07-01 — is net-negative here in both forms. All three camps sited: villagers 10 → 3 by tick 4000, food 20, wood 8. Lumber camp only (the Mill's kinds include BOAR, which kills the builder sent to it): villagers 12, food 36, against 21 and 1010 for the shipped baseline. Early wood is genuinely better (190 at tick 3000 vs 133) — the cost is elsewhere: a camp at the forest puts the build, the deliveries and the villagers far from home and out of the base's protection. A future attempt needs to pay for that separately (escort, or a camp sited on the near EDGE of the forest rather than in it), not simply re-point the anchor.
- **Also measured and reverted (2026-08-20): treating a long-stalled occupant as scenery.** A unit that holds a gather order it cannot act on never attempts a move, so the traffic closure has no heading for it and every unit behind it waits forever — which is exactly what the surviving wood stall is made of. Excluding peers that have not attempted in 30 ticks from the lane is logically right and spec §12.5 already says a stationary unit is ordinary occupancy rather than a permanent wall; it measured 9 villagers and food 0 against 21 and 1010, so it is not shippable as-is. It presumably lets units walk through each other into some worse state; that is what the next attempt has to find.
- **The surviving AI defect, narrowed (2026-08-20).** Wood income stays near zero and the whole economy stops around tick 15000 — state at tick 16000 and tick 24000 is byte-identical while the scouts keep moving. Ground truth: the AI's lumber camp at (46,25) has a resource against its south face at (46,27), so its only route to the forest is the single-cell corridor (47,27)-(47,29). Loaded carriers meet outgoing gatherers head-on in it, seven villagers pile into one tile, and the gatherers there have NO path at all (they make no move request), so the arbiter cannot reason about them. The next attempt should start from that corridor, and from a per-unit decision count rather than from the gather state.

- **The AI's stall was the MAP, not the AI (2026-08-23, v0.3.38) — resolved.** Four instruments, each answering the previous one's question, ended at map generation. A per-villager stationarity probe: 4 of 13 villagers frozen in one cell for a whole 600-tick window. A branch probe: six of them held the same sheep with `noPlan` on 200 of 200 ticks. An approach probe: that sheep's three free neighbours were `passable=true, path=false`, and the villagers' region was 1449 cells that did not include them — a pocket, walled by a solid line of the AI's OWN buildings ten cells wide. And then the one that mattered, a flood fill answering "what can this region actually work?": `boar, gold, stone, fish` and **no trees at all**. All 27 live trees stood on forest terrain, **18 of them had no walkable neighbour**, and 83 of their blocked faces were bare forest tiles. The default map painted 197 forest cells and put trees on 48; the other 149 held no wood and could never be cleared, so a woodline was harvestable only at its rim and everything behind it was unreachable forever. Black Forest had been doing it correctly (1718 forest cells, 1718 trees) since it shipped. **Fixed:** every forest cell on the standard map carries a tree; an exhausted tree clears its tile to grass (and releases the occupancy grid's static terrain blocker, which is not entity-keyed — without that the felled tree's ground stayed a wall); a villager with no reachable resource of its kind falls through to another kind instead of being handed the same unreachable one every tick; and the AI's placement search refuses an anchor that would cut the free ground beside it in two. **Measured on the same seed:** before, Feudal forever with food 19 / wood 19 / 15 villagers, byte-identical from tick 17000 to 30000; after, **Castle Age by tick 8000**, and 23 villagers / 35 units / 22 buildings / 988 food at tick 10000. **The open-face ranking recommended below was tried and is NOT the fix** — a probe of the reachability search showed the budget was usually not even exhausted (`candidates=13 probes=12 found=false`): the trees were genuinely unreachable, not merely badly ranked.
- **What the same run showed, and what has since been fixed.** (1) The OTHER AI hoarded 3300 wood in the Dark Age and never aged up — **it now reaches Feudal with 14 buildings** by tick 8000, a side effect of (2). (2) Both sides lost whole villager forces to raids they did not defend against — **fixed in v0.3.43**: the AI shelters villagers in its Town Center when enemies reach the base and releases them when the raid passes, and on the same seed the weaker side went from 0 villagers at tick 10000 to 6, with both owners still on the map and still changing at tick 12000. (3) The AI still never mines stone before Castle Age, so it cannot build a Castle when it wants one. **Still open beyond that:** the AI does not build Fish Traps or use the naval economy at all (its build targets are land-only), and the human slot in an AI-vs-AI run still plays worse than the AI slot.
- **The AI's late-game stall, six measured attempts (2026-08-20). The most useful result is that the SUCCESS METRIC was wrong.** Judging a change by villagers-and-stockpile at tick 30000 rewards an AI that has FROZEN with a large pile over one that is still playing: the shipped v0.3.30 build reads 21 villagers / food 1010, and its state is byte-identical from tick 17000 to 30000 — nothing happens for the last thirteen thousand ticks, and the pile is large precisely because nothing spends it. A better signal is how many sample windows differ from the one before: v0.3.30 manages 16 of 30, and stops.
- **The one change that reached 30 of 30 was an OPEN-FACE preference in gather assignment, and it is worth picking back up.** Deep in a forest almost every tree is walled in by other trees — there is no free cell beside it to stand on — so ranking candidates by distance sends villagers to exactly the trees they cannot work, and the bounded reachability probe cannot rescue them because the nearest sixteen candidates are all interior. Ranking resources that have at least one free orthogonal neighbour ahead of those that do not (one passability read per side, no pathfinding) kept the AI building, training and gathering through the whole 30000 ticks for the first time: buildings 8 → 12, villagers still climbing at the end, wood at tick 3000 up from 199 to 216, and Feudal by tick 4000. **It was reverted because it turns a green gate red** — `gatherDomain.test.ts` "keeps growing its villager force instead of losing it" wants 15 villagers within 14000 steps and this peaks at 14 — and a threshold must not be relaxed to admit the change it is judging. The gap is FOOD, not wood: food sits between 1 and 80 for the whole run while gold climbs past 2200, so the AI cannot afford the 50-food villagers it has the population room for. Anyone resuming this should start there, with the open-face ranking reapplied, and judge it on windows-that-changed rather than on the ending pile.
- **Two more measured and reverted the same day.** Treating a long-stalled occupant as scenery rather than as traffic (a unit holding a gather order it cannot act on never attempts a move, so the traffic closure has no heading for it and everything behind it waits forever) is logically right and spec §12.5 already implies it — it measured 9 villagers and food 0 against 21 and 1010. Raising `MAX_REACHABILITY_PROBES` from 16 to 48 — its comment's justification, "16 comfortably exceeds the number of same-kind resources clustered near any base", is disproved by any forest — changed nothing on its own.

## How the loop drives this

Each milestone item is a self-contained change: spec update (the rule), TDD, multi-CLI review, commit, then a playtest re-run whose `playtest:findings` either confirms the gap closed (the `unknown-kind` rejection for that command disappears; the agent uses the new capability) or surfaces the next gap. Cross-cutting note from the audit: nearly every gap is simulation-logic work in this repo, not a civ-engine limitation.

**Loop-strategy note (2026-06-15):** the LLM player reliably STALLS in the Dark Age (turtles with 0 military; leaves villagers idle even though the snapshot surfaces idle counts) — a player-strategy limitation, not a game/affordance gap. So an LLM campaign currently can't reach (and thus can't surface gaps in) Feudal/Castle combat, age-up, or the mid/late game. Two consequences for picking the next item: (1) gap-finding for deeper phases should be DECOUPLED from LLM skill — build known roadmap gaps and validate them with deterministic scenario fixtures (the repo already has combat/siege/castle/age fixtures) + the `playtest:findings` AoE2-rules critique, rather than waiting for the LLM to play there; (2) a fresh LLM campaign is still the right periodic re-grounding, but run it on a FRESH session (the subscription degrades over a long session — campaigns slow/hang) and treat its value as "did early-game survival/economy improve" rather than "did it reach the late game".

M7 (graphics/UI) items validate differently — they are visual, not rule changes — so they follow the AGENTS.md visual-change protocol: capture a before screenshot, apply the change, capture an after screenshot, and use a pixel diff as the verification alongside the normal test/build gates (rendering changes must not break the bridge contract tests). They still get the multi-CLI review and the devlog/spec/changelog discipline where applicable.
